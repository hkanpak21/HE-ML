### ch-dkw-atoms

Accepted. The proof of thm-dataflow-composition (c)(ii) gains the clause below, and thm-record-level-certificate (a) uses it on a law with atoms.

## Proofs

Standing facts. Rounds 01 and 02 list the test fact, data processing, the realization fact, Lyapunov's theorem, (G1) to (G4), the split, McDiarmid's inequality, the DKW inequality and lazy sampling. Five more facts are used below.

(F1) The reverse split. For laws $P,M,Q$ on one space and $\gamma\ge1$, $\mathrm H_\gamma(Q\|P)\le\mathrm H_\gamma(Q\|M)+\gamma\,\mathrm{TV}(M,P)$, since $Q(B)-\gamma P(B)=(Q(B)-\gamma M(B))+\gamma(M(B)-P(B))$ for every $B$.

(F2) The averaging identity. Let $\lambda$ be a law on a standard Borel space $\mathcal W$, let $w\mapsto Q^w_0$ and $w\mapsto Q^w_1$ be kernels from $\mathcal W$ to a standard Borel space, and let $J_b(dw,dy)=\lambda(dw)\,Q^w_b(dy)$. Then $\mathrm H_\gamma(J_0\|J_1)=\int\mathrm H_\gamma(Q^w_0\|Q^w_1)\,\lambda(dw)$. This is step 3 of the proof of thm-coupling-certificate (b) in round 01, written there for $\lambda=\mu$, and its proof uses only that $\lambda$ is a law on a standard Borel space.

(F3) Gaussian bounds. For $s>0$, $\Phi(s/2)-e^\varepsilon\Phi(-s/2)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;s)\le\Phi(s/2-\varepsilon/s)$, and the left side tends to one as $s\to\infty$. The density ratio of $\mathcal N(0,1)$ to $\mathcal N(s,1)$ at $y$ is $e^{s^2/2-sy}$, which exceeds $e^\varepsilon$ exactly when $y<s/2-\varepsilon/s$. So for every $B$, $\mathcal N(0,1)(B)-e^\varepsilon\mathcal N(s,1)(B)$ is at most the $\mathcal N(0,1)$-mass of $B\cap\{y<s/2-\varepsilon/s\}$, which gives the upper bound. The lower bound takes $B=\{y<s/2\}$.

(F4) Mills bounds. For $u>0$, $\frac{u}{1+u^2}\varphi(u)\le\bar\Phi(u)\le\varphi(u)/u$. The upper bound is $\int_u^\infty\varphi(v)\,dv\le\int_u^\infty\frac vu\varphi(v)\,dv=\varphi(u)/u$. For the lower bound let $g(u)=\bar\Phi(u)-\frac{u}{1+u^2}\varphi(u)$. With $\varphi'(u)=-u\varphi(u)$ one computes $g'(u)=-2\varphi(u)/(1+u^2)^2<0$, and $g(u)\to0$ as $u\to\infty$, so $g>0$.

(F5) Lower semicontinuity. On a metric space with its Borel sets, $\mathrm H_\gamma(P\|Q)=\sup_g\big(\int g\,dP-\gamma\int g\,dQ\big)$ over continuous $g$ with values in $[0,1]$, so $\mathrm H_\gamma$ is lower semicontinuous under weak convergence of both arguments. The supremum is at most $\mathrm H_\gamma(P\|Q)$ by the integral form of data processing in round 01. For the other direction fix a Borel set $B$ and $\lambda>0$. Regularity of the finite measure $P+Q$ (Billingsley, Convergence of Probability Measures, Theorem 1.1, from memory) gives a closed $F\subseteq B$ and an open $V\supseteq B$ with $(P+Q)(V\setminus F)<\lambda$. Let $g(y)=d(y,V^c)/(d(y,V^c)+d(y,F))$, with $g=0$ when $F$ is empty and $g=1$ when $V$ is the whole space and $F$ is not empty. It is continuous and $|g-\mathbf 1_B|\le\mathbf 1_{V\setminus F}$, so $\int g\,dP-\gamma\int g\,dQ\ge P(B)-\gamma Q(B)-\gamma\lambda$. Each map $(P,Q)\mapsto\int g\,dP-\gamma\int g\,dQ$ is continuous under weak convergence, and a supremum of continuous maps is lower semicontinuous.

### Needle lower bound [lem-needle-lower-bound]

Statement. Let $\theta$ be a target and $\vartheta\in[0,1]$. Let $\mathsf L^\circ$ be a learning function, and let $X_1,\dots,X_K$ be pairwise disjoint measurable sets of training inputs. For each $k\le K$ let $\mathsf L_k$ be a learning function with $\mathsf L_k(x;\rho,\xi)=\mathsf L^\circ(x;\rho,\xi)$ for every $x\notin X_k$ and all $\rho,\xi$, and with $\theta(\mathsf L_k)\ge\vartheta$. Then every certifier of $\theta$ with at most $N$ Train calls, in either discipline, sound at level $\alpha$ over a class that contains $\mathsf L_1,\dots,\mathsf L_K$, satisfies

$$\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha-\frac NK .$$

If such sets and functions exist for every $K$, then $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha$.

Label. Proven.

Proof. Fix the certifier and $k$. Put its run against $\mathsf L^\circ$ and its run against $\mathsf L_k$ on one probability space. Both runs use the same coins, and the $i$-th oracle call of both uses the same triple $(\rho'_i,\xi'_i,\upsilon'_i)\sim\mu\otimes\pi\otimes\nu$, independent across $i$ and of the coins. The seed $\rho'_i$ serves when the call draws one, $\xi'_i$ is its hardware randomness and $\upsilon'_i$ its evaluation randomness. By fidelity each run has the law that its oracle prescribes. The names a caller receives are integers fixed by its count of calls, so while the two transcripts agree the two oracles store the same objects under the same names. The certifier's next call is a function of its coins and of the answers so far. A Train call outside $X_k$ gets the same answer in both runs, because $\mathsf L_k$ and $\mathsf L^\circ$ agree there and the call uses the same seed and the same $\xi'_i$. An Eval or Open call gets the same answer, because it reads the same stored object with the same $\upsilon'_i$. By induction over calls the two runs agree up to the first Train call whose training input lies in $X_k$, and that call is the same call in both runs. Let $\mathrm{Hit}_k$ be the event that the run against $\mathsf L^\circ$ makes a Train call with training input in $X_k$. Off $\mathrm{Hit}_k$ the two runs have the same transcript and the same output, so

$$\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\le\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]+\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\mathrm{Hit}_k].$$

Soundness at $\mathsf L_k$ gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\theta(\mathsf L_k)]\ge1-\alpha$. A Train call names one training input, which lies in at most one of the disjoint sets, so $\sum_k\mathbf 1[\mathrm{Hit}_k]\le N$ on every run against $\mathsf L^\circ$. Averaging the display over $k$ gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha-\frac1K\sum_k\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\mathrm{Hit}_k]\ge1-\alpha-N/K$. Letting $K$ grow gives the last claim. $\square$

Remarks. The lemma uses neither the discipline nor the form of the target. It rests only on where the certifier trains. thm-dataflow-composition (d)(ii) is the instance with one-point sets. thm-active-choice-dichotomy (c) and (d) use points and balls of starting models, and thm-record-level-certificate (c) uses datasets.

Without the oracle. Without Train calls, a certifier sound over every learning function must output a bound that holds at every needle function, which is at least $\vartheta$. The lemma prices the escape from that bound. With $N$ training runs an experimenter rules out at most $N$ of $K$ disjoint places where a learning function can hide leakage, whatever seeds it uses.

Degenerate oracles. The lemma holds in both disciplines, so it separates neither and claims no criterion 4. Its role is the lower bounds of the three theorems that use it, which bind the top experimenter as well.

Prior work. This is the adversary argument of query complexity, in the form that Gilbert and McMillan use for their Theorem 10 on testing pure differential privacy. I claim no novelty for it.

### Dataflow composition, revised [thm-dataflow-composition]

Setting. $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases (def-isolated-client), and $P_b$ is the law of the adversary's transcript under $H_b$. Write $\gamma=e^\varepsilon$.

The ciphertext procedure. In $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$ a sub-protocol $\Sigma$ among the parties computes each release in place of $r_t$. In round $t$ every party $i$ holds an input $u_{t,i}\in\mathbb R^d$, with $u_{t,j}=f_t(c_t,W_t)$. $\Sigma$ outputs $y_t$ to the honest parties and delivers to the coalition a transcript $\mathrm{tr}_t$ of ciphertexts and decryption shares. Only $\Sigma$ reads client $j$'s values. Let $\mathsf t_{\mathsf L}$ bound the time of one run of $\mathsf L$ or $\mathsf{ev}$, let $n_G$ bound the number of honest runs of $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$ in $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$, and let $\mathsf t_G$ bound the time of its other honest nodes. For an adversary that runs in time $\mathsf t_{\mathcal A}$ and makes $q$ oracle calls let $\mathsf t'=\mathsf t_{\mathcal A}+(q+n_G)\,\mathsf t_{\mathsf L}+\mathsf t_G$.

Ideal releases. $\mathcal F$ computes $y_t=\sum_iu_{t,i}+e_t+Z_t$ and releases it through the plain channel. Here $Z_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is the noise of the honest parties, and $e_t$ has a fixed law independent of the inputs and of $Z_t$. $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$ replaces $\Sigma$ by $\mathcal F$, keeps the coalition's keys as the value of an input node owned by the coalition, and turns ciphertext channels into none. $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dev}}$ acts in round $t$ in four steps.

1. It receives $u_{t,i}$ from each honest party and, from the coalition, a value $u_{t,i}$ for each coalition party.
2. It computes $y_t=\sum_iu_{t,i}+e_t+Z_t$ as $\mathcal F$ does.
3. It gives $y_t$ to the coalition.
4. It receives $\tilde y_t\in\mathbb R^d\cup\{\bot\}$ from the coalition and delivers it to every honest party, which stops on $\bot$.

### Active-choice dichotomy [thm-active-choice-dichotomy]

Setting. $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases (def-isolated-client), and the choice $c_t$ of every round is a choice of the coalition. The procedure fixes admissible sets $\mathcal C_t\subseteq\mathcal C$ for $t\le R$, and $\mathcal C_\cup=\bigcup_t\mathcal C_t$. A strategy is admissible when $c_t\in\mathcal C_t$ in every round that runs. It may be adaptive, make any oracle calls, set any other choice that the isolation allows, and stop. Fix $\varepsilon\ge0$, $\gamma=e^\varepsilon$, $\Delta>0$ and a policy, realizable or seed-revealing. The targets are

$$\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L)=\sup\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1),\qquad\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L)=\sup\mathrm H_\gamma(P_1\|P_0),$$

with both suprema over the adversaries whose strategy is admissible. For a stateless client let $\bar\beta_t(\Delta)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal C_t}\beta_t(c,\Delta)$.

The tree execution, for a client with state and finite sets. Let $\mathbb T_t=\mathcal C_1\times\dots\times\mathcal C_t$ and $\mathbb T=\bigcup_{t\le R}\mathbb T_t$, the set of admissible choice prefixes. For every prefix $\mathbf c=(c_1,\dots,c_t)\in\mathbb T$ draw an independent triple $(\rho_{\mathbf c},\xi_{\mathbf c},\xi'_{\mathbf c})\sim\mu\otimes\pi\otimes\pi$, and set, with $\mathbf c^-=(c_1,\dots,c_{t-1})$ and $W_{()}=W'_{()}=w_0$,

$$W_{\mathbf c}=\mathsf L\big(x(c_t,D^0_j,W_{\mathbf c^-});\rho_{\mathbf c},\xi_{\mathbf c}\big),\qquad W'_{\mathbf c}=\mathsf L\big(x(c_t,D^1_j,W'_{\mathbf c^-});\rho_{\mathbf c},\xi'_{\mathbf c}\big).$$

Its exceedance is $D^{\mathbb T}=\max_{\mathbf c\in\mathbb T}\|f_t(c_t,W'_{\mathbf c})-f_t(c_t,W_{\mathbf c})\|$, with $t$ the length of $\mathbf c$, and $\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)=\Pr[D^{\mathbb T}>\Delta]$. An experimenter samples one tree execution with $2|\mathbb T|$ Train calls. At each prefix, in order of length, it calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^0_j,W_{\mathbf c^-}),\bot)$, which returns a seed name $h$, and then $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^1_j,W'_{\mathbf c^-}),h)$.

The lower-bound procedures. $G^{\mathcal C}$ has one honest client $j$, the server in the coalition, $R$ rounds, $\mathcal C=\mathbb R^d$, sets $\mathcal C_t\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, $O_t=0$, a fixed learner name $\ell$ and class $\mathcal H$, and a protocol that sets $c_t$ to a fixed element of $\mathcal C_t$. In the stateless form $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^d$, $x(c,D,w)=(\ell,c,D,\mathcal H)$ and $f_t(c,m)=m-c$. In the stateful form $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal S$, where $\mathcal S$ is the set of finite sequences of points of $\mathbb R^d$, a standard Borel space as the countable disjoint union of the spaces $\mathbb R^{dk}$. There $w_0=(0,())$, $x(c,D,(m,s))=(\ell,(c,s),D,\mathcal H)$ and $f_t(c,(m,s))=m-c$. The reference function $\mathsf L^\circ$ returns $c$ at $x(c,D,w)$ in the stateless form and $(c,s\Vert c)$ at $x(c,D,(m,s))$ in the stateful form, where $s\Vert c$ appends $c$ to $s$, for every $D$, $\rho$ and $\xi$, and it returns the starting model at every other training input. $G^{\mathrm{agg}}$ has $R=2$, the stateless form, one honest client $j$, the server honest and one coalition client $k$. The coalition client owns an input node with value $u_k\in\mathbb R^d$, the protocol sets $u_k=0$, and $O_1=u_k$ and $O_2=0$. The first starting model is $c_1=0$, and an honest general node sets $c_2=c_1+y_1$. Its target $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}$ is the supremum of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ over all strategies of the coalition client. In the bounded variant the coalition client must have $\|u_k\|\le B_A$.

Statement.

(a) Worst-case exceedance. (i) For a stateless client, with the suprema over admissible strategies,

$$\sup\epsilon_0(\Delta)=\sup\epsilon_1(\Delta)=1-\prod_{t\le R}\big(1-\bar\beta_t(\Delta)\big),$$

and a non-adaptive strategy attains this value when every $\bar\beta_t$ is attained. Hence

$$\max\{\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C},\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}\}\le\delta_{\mathcal N}\big(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\Delta/\sigma\big)+1-\prod_{t\le R}\big(1-\bar\beta_t(\Delta)\big).$$

(ii) For a client with state and finite sets, every admissible strategy has $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)$. Hence $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)$ and $\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\gamma\,\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)$.

(b) Certificates in the coupled discipline, sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function. (i) For a stateless client and finite sets, draw $n$ coupled pairs $(W^{c,k},W'^{c,k})_{k\le n}$ at $(x(c,D^0_j),x(c,D^1_j))$ for each $c\in\mathcal C_\cup$. For each $t$ and $c\in\mathcal C_t$ let $\bar p_{t,c}$ be the one-sided Clopper and Pearson upper bound at level $\alpha/K$, with $K=\sum_t|\mathcal C_t|$, from the number of $k$ with $\|f_t(c,W'^{c,k})-f_t(c,W^{c,k})\|>\Delta$. Then

$$\hat t=\min\Big\{1,\ \delta_{\mathcal N}\big(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\Delta/\sigma\big)+1-\prod_{t\le R}\big(1-\max_{c\in\mathcal C_t}\bar p_{t,c}\big)\Big\}$$

is sound for $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}$ and for $\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}$, with $N=2n|\mathcal C_\cup|$ Train calls. If every sampled pair coincides, $\hat t\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+R\ln(K/\alpha)/n$. (ii) For a client with state and finite sets, let $\bar p$ be the one-sided Clopper and Pearson upper bound at level $\alpha$ from the number of $n$ independent tree executions with $D^{\mathbb T}>\Delta$. Then $\min\{1,\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\bar p\}$ is sound for $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}$, and $\min\{1,\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\gamma\bar p\}$ for $\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}$, with $N=2n|\mathbb T|$. If no execution exceeds $\Delta$, $\bar p\le\ln(1/\alpha)/n$.

(c) Lower bounds in either discipline. In $G^{\mathcal C}$, under either policy, $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$, every coupled pair at $\mathsf L^\circ$ coincides, and in the stateful form every tree execution at $\mathsf L^\circ$ has $D^{\mathbb T}=0$. Let a certifier of $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}$ with $N$ Train calls be sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function, and let $\eta\in(0,1)$. (i) In the stateless form, $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha-N/|\mathcal C_\cup|$. (ii) In the stateful form with finite sets, $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha-N/\prod_{t\le R}|\mathcal C_t|$. (iii) If some $\mathcal C_t$ is infinite, in either form, $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha$.

(d) Choice through the aggregate. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}$ let $\mathsf L^\circ(x(c,D);\rho,\xi)=c$. Then $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$, and the certificate of thm-dataflow-composition (c)(i) for the protocol's strategy, from $N'$ runs, outputs at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt2\Delta/\sigma)+\ln(1/\alpha)/N'$ at $\mathsf L^\circ$. Yet for every $\eta\in(0,1)$ every certifier of $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}$ sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function has $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha$. In the bounded variant it has $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha-N/J$, with $J\ge(B_A/(2r))^d-1$ and $r=\sigma\sqrt{2d/\eta}$.

Corollary, the price. Let the sets be finite and let $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}<1-\eta$. In $G^{\mathcal C}$, a certifier sound over every learning function that outputs at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ at $\mathsf L^\circ$ with probability at least $1-\alpha'$ needs the following numbers of Train calls, and the certificates of (b) achieve them with certainty.

1. Stateless form. At least $(1-\alpha-\alpha')\,|\mathcal C_\cup|$, and (b)(i) uses $2|\mathcal C_\cup|\lceil R\ln(K/\alpha)/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\rceil$.
2. Stateful form. At least $(1-\alpha-\alpha')\prod_t|\mathcal C_t|$, and (b)(ii) uses $2|\mathbb T|\lceil\ln(1/\alpha)/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\rceil$, where $|\mathbb T|\le2\prod_t|\mathcal C_t|$ when every $|\mathcal C_t|\ge2$.

With an infinite set no number suffices.

Label. Proven, (a) to (d) and the corollary.

Which parts are corollaries. The bound in (a)(i) is one line from thm-dataflow-composition (b), and (b)(i) follows from (a)(i) with the Clopper and Pearson bound, so both are corollaries. The new content lies in four places. The equality in (a)(i) says that adaptivity buys a coalition nothing in the exceedance term against a stateless client, whatever the depth of the strategy tree. The embedding of every admissible strategy into the tree execution in (a)(ii) gives a certificate that covers adaptive strategies against a client with state. The matching lower bounds of (c) show that state turns the price from the number of distinct choices into the number of choice sequences. Part (d) shows that an indirect choice through a Gaussian release already makes the price infinite.

Proof of (a)(i). Fix an admissible strategy and an adversary, and work on the space of step 1 of the proof of thm-dataflow-composition (b), with $\mathcal F_t$ generated by $\omega$, $\zeta_{<t}$ and $Z_{<t}$. Let $E_t$ be the event that round $t$ runs and $\|d_t\|>\Delta$ in $\mathsf{Ex}_0$. For a stateless client, $d_t$ is a function of $c_t$ and $\zeta_t$ alone. The event that round $t$ runs and the choice $c_t\in\mathcal C_t$ are $\mathcal F_t$-measurable, and $\zeta_t$ is independent of $\mathcal F_t$, so $\Pr[E_t\mid\mathcal F_t]\le\beta_t(c_t,\Delta)\le\bar\beta_t(\Delta)$. Each $E_s$ with $s<t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. With $G_t=E^c_1\cap\dots\cap E^c_t$,

$$\Pr[G_t]=\mathbb E\big[\mathbf 1_{G_{t-1}}\Pr[E^c_t\mid\mathcal F_t]\big]\ge\big(1-\bar\beta_t(\Delta)\big)\Pr[G_{t-1}],$$

and induction gives $\epsilon_0(\Delta)=1-\Pr[G_R]\le1-\prod_t(1-\bar\beta_t(\Delta))$. For the other direction let $\lambda>0$ and choose $c^\lambda_t\in\mathcal C_t$ with $\beta_t(c^\lambda_t,\Delta)\ge\bar\beta_t(\Delta)-\lambda$, or $c^\lambda_t$ attaining $\bar\beta_t$ when it is attained. The non-adaptive strategy that sets $c_t=c^\lambda_t$ and never stops is admissible. Its events $E_t$ are functions of the independent $\zeta_t$, so $\epsilon_0(\Delta)=1-\prod_t(1-\beta_t(c^\lambda_t,\Delta))$, which tends to $1-\prod_t(1-\bar\beta_t(\Delta))$ as $\lambda\to0$. In $\mathsf{Ex}_1$ the base and the twin exchange datasets, and the law of $\|d_t\|$ given $c_t$ is the same, since $\beta_t(c,\Delta)$ involves the pair $x(c,D^0_j),x(c,D^1_j)$ with a shared seed and independent hardware randomness. The same argument gives the value for $\epsilon_1$. The bounds on the targets follow from thm-dataflow-composition (b), applied to each admissible adversary, through its terms $\epsilon_0$ and $\epsilon_1$. $\square$

Proof of (a)(ii). Fix an admissible strategy and an adversary. Build the execution on a space that carries $\omega$, $Z_1,\dots,Z_R$ and the triples of the tree execution, all independent. In round $t$, if it runs, the choice $c_t=\chi_t(\omega,y_{<t},\bar\rho_{<t})$ lies in $\mathcal C_t$, so the prefix $\mathbf c_{\le t}=(c_1,\dots,c_t)$ lies in $\mathbb T_t$. Client $j$'s node uses $(\rho_{\mathbf c_{\le t}},\xi_{\mathbf c_{\le t}})$, the twin uses $\xi'_{\mathbf c_{\le t}}$, and under the seed-revealing policy $\bar\rho_t=\rho_{\mathbf c_{\le t}}$. This gives $\mathsf{Ex}_0$ its law. The prefix used in round $t$ is a function of $\omega$, of $Z_{<t}$ and of the triples at the prefixes used before round $t$. It has length $t$, so no earlier round used it. By lazy sampling for the table indexed by $\mathbb T$, with $\omega$ and the $Z_t$ as the randomness independent of the table, the triple at the prefix of round $t$ is independent of everything revealed before and has law $\mu\otimes\pi\otimes\pi$. That is the conditional law of $\zeta_t$ in step 1 of thm-dataflow-composition (b), so the execution has the law of $\mathsf{Ex}_0$. By induction over rounds the chain and the twin of the execution are $W_t=W_{\mathbf c_{\le t}}$ and $W'_t=W'_{\mathbf c_{\le t}}$, since both pairs satisfy the same recursion from $w_0$. Hence $D^\star_0\le D^{\mathbb T}$ on this space, and $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)$. The bounds on the targets follow from thm-dataflow-composition (b), the second through its term $\gamma\,\epsilon_0$. $\square$

Proof of (b). For (b)(i) fix the learning function. The pairs drawn at one $c$ are independent draws of the coupled pair, by fidelity, so for each $(t,c)$ with $c\in\mathcal C_t$ the indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $\beta_t(c,\Delta)$. Each Clopper and Pearson bound covers its parameter with probability at least $1-\alpha/K$, and a union bound over the $K$ pairs $(t,c)$ makes all of them hold with probability at least $1-\alpha$. The union bound does not need independence, so one sample may serve several rounds. On that event $\bar\beta_t(\Delta)\le\max_{c\in\mathcal C_t}\bar p_{t,c}$ for every $t$, and $x\mapsto1-\prod_t(1-x_t)$ is nondecreasing in each coordinate on $[0,1]^R$, so (a)(i) gives $\max\{\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C},\theta^{\mathrm{act},\leftarrow}_{\mathcal C}\}\le\hat t$. Each $c\in\mathcal C_\cup$ costs $2n$ calls. If every pair coincides, every count is zero, $\bar p_{t,c}=1-(\alpha/K)^{1/n}\le\ln(K/\alpha)/n$, and $1-\prod_t(1-x_t)\le\sum_tx_t$ gives the last claim. For (b)(ii) the tree executions are independent, by fidelity, so the indicators of $D^{\mathbb T}>\Delta$ are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $\epsilon^{\mathbb T}(\Delta)$. The Clopper and Pearson bound covers it with probability at least $1-\alpha$, and (a)(ii) gives soundness. The experimenter holds $W_{\mathbf c^-}$ and $W'_{\mathbf c^-}$ from its calls at the shorter prefix, so each prefix costs two calls. $\square$

Proof of (c). At $\mathsf L^\circ$ the model part of every trained model is the starting model $c_t$, so $f_t(c_t,W_t)=0$ in every round under both hypotheses and every strategy, and every release is $Z_t$. By step 1 of the proof of thm-dataflow-composition (b) the transcript is $\Psi(\omega,y_{\le R},\bar\rho_{\le R})$, and $(\omega,y_{\le R},\bar\rho_{\le R})$ has the same law under both hypotheses. So $P_0=P_1$ for every adversary, and $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$. At $\mathsf L^\circ$ the outputs at $x(c,D^0_j,w)$ and $x(c,D^1_j,w)$ coincide, and in the stateful form the two chains of the tree execution carry the same states, so every coupled pair and every tree execution coincide.

Choose $L>0$ with $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma)\ge1-\eta$, which (F3) allows. For (i) let $c^{(1)},\dots,c^{(K)}$ be distinct points of $\mathcal C_\cup$, with $K=|\mathcal C_\cup|$ when the union is finite and any $K$ otherwise. Let $X_k=\{x(c^{(k)},D^1_j)\}$, which are disjoint since $x$ is injective in $c$. Let $\mathsf L_k$ agree with $\mathsf L^\circ$ except that $\mathsf L_k(x(c^{(k)},D^1_j);\rho,\xi)=c^{(k)}+Le_1$. Let $t_k$ be a round with $c^{(k)}\in\mathcal C_{t_k}$, and consider the admissible non-adaptive strategy that sets $c_{t_k}=c^{(k)}$ and $c_t$ to the protocol's element in the other rounds, together with the adversary that only reads the releases. Under $H_0$ every release is $Z_t$. Under $H_1$ the releases are independent, the release of round $t_k$ is $Le_1+Z_{t_k}$, and each other release is $Z_t$ or $Le_1+Z_t$. The divergence of the transcript is at least that of the release of round $t_k$ alone, by data processing, which is $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma)$ by (G1). So $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L_k)\ge1-\eta$, and lem-needle-lower-bound with $\vartheta=1-\eta$ gives (i), and (iii) in the stateless form.

For (ii) let $K=\prod_t|\mathcal C_t|$, and for each sequence $\mathbf c=(c_1,\dots,c_R)\in\mathbb T_R$ let $x_{\mathbf c}=(\ell,(c_R,(c_1,\dots,c_{R-1})),D^1_j,\mathcal H)$, the training input of round $R$ under $H_1$ along $\mathbf c$. These are distinct points. Let $\mathsf L_{\mathbf c}$ agree with $\mathsf L^\circ$ except that $\mathsf L_{\mathbf c}(x_{\mathbf c};\rho,\xi)=(c_R+Le_1,(c_1,\dots,c_R))$. Under $\mathsf L_{\mathbf c}$ the state after round $t$ is $(c_1,\dots,c_t)$ under both hypotheses, since the needle changes only the model part. So the non-adaptive strategy that plays $\mathbf c$ trains at $x_{\mathbf c}$ in round $R$ under $H_1$, and its releases are $Z_t$ in every round except round $R$ under $H_1$, where the release is $Le_1+Z_R$. As in (i), $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}(\mathsf L_{\mathbf c})\ge\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma)\ge1-\eta$, and lem-needle-lower-bound gives (ii). If some $\mathcal C_{t_0}$ is infinite, take $K$ sequences that differ only in the coordinate $t_0$, which give distinct points $x_{\mathbf c}$, and let $K$ grow. That gives (iii) in the stateful form. $\square$

Proof of (d). At $\mathsf L^\circ$ the releases are $y_1=u_k+Z_1$ and $y_2=Z_2$ under both hypotheses for every strategy, so $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$ by the argument of (c). Under the protocol's strategy every coupled pair coincides, so $D^\star_0=0$ in every run, and the Clopper and Pearson bound is $1-\alpha^{1/N'}\le\ln(1/\alpha)/N'$.

Let $r=\sigma\sqrt{2d/\eta}$, so that $\Pr[\|Z\|>r]\le\mathbb E\|Z\|^2/r^2=\eta/2$ for $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$, by Markov's inequality. Let $\mathbf p_k=4kr\,e_1$ for $k\ge1$, and $X_k=\{x(c,D^1_j):\|c-\mathbf p_k\|<r\}$. The balls are disjoint and none contains $c_1=0$. Let $\mathsf L_k(x(c,D);\rho,\xi)=c+L\,\mathbf 1[\|c-\mathbf p_k\|<r,\ D=D^1_j]\,e_1$, which agrees with $\mathsf L^\circ$ outside $X_k$. Consider the strategy $u_k=\mathbf p_k$ and the adversary that only reads the releases. In round 1, $c_1$ lies in no ball, so $y_1=\mathbf p_k+Z_1$ under both hypotheses, and $c_2=y_1$. Given $y_1$, the release $y_2$ is $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ under $H_0$ and $\mathcal N(L\,\mathbf 1[\|y_1-\mathbf p_k\|<r]\,e_1,\sigma^2I_d)$ under $H_1$. The averaging identity (F2), with $y_1$ as the common first coordinate, gives

$$\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)=\Pr\big[\|Z_1\|<r\big]\,\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma)\ge(1-\eta/2)\,\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma).$$

Choose $L$ with $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;L/\sigma)\ge(1-\eta)/(1-\eta/2)$, which (F3) allows. Then $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}(\mathsf L_k)\ge1-\eta$ for every $k$, and lem-needle-lower-bound with infinitely many sets gives the first claim.

In the bounded variant take a maximal set of points $\mathbf p_1,\dots,\mathbf p_{J'}$ in the closed ball of radius $B_A$ around $0$ with pairwise distances above $2r$. Such a set is finite, since the open balls of radius $r$ around its points are disjoint and lie in a bounded region. By maximality the balls of radius $2r$ around the points cover the ball of radius $B_A$, so $J'(2r)^d\ge B_A^d$ by comparing volumes. The balls of radius $r$ around the points are disjoint, and at most one of them contains $0$, since two such centres would lie within $2r$ of each other. Dropping that one leaves $J\ge(B_A/(2r))^d-1$ balls, each with an admissible input $u_k=\mathbf p_k$, and lem-needle-lower-bound gives the second claim. $\square$

Proof of the corollary. The lower bounds are (c), since an output at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}<1-\eta$ with probability at least $1-\alpha'$ forces $1-\alpha'\le\alpha+N/K$. The upper bounds are (b) at $\mathsf L^\circ$, where every pair and every tree execution coincide. Finally $|\mathbb T|=\sum_{t\le R}\prod_{s\le t}|\mathcal C_s|\le\prod_{s\le R}|\mathcal C_s|\sum_{i\ge0}2^{-i}$ when every $|\mathcal C_s|\ge2$. $\square$

Application to the aggregation sub-protocol. Under $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dev}}$ with output substitution, the release procedure of thm-dataflow-composition (a)(ii) lets the server deliver any $\tilde y_1$, and an honest node sets $c_2=c_1+\tilde y_1$. With one honest client and the server as the coalition, this contains $G^{\mathcal C}$ with $\mathcal C_1=\{c_1\}$ and $\mathcal C_2=\mathbb R^d$, so by (c)(iii) no certifier sound over every learning function certifies anything below one (proven). Under $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dev}}_{\mathrm{abort}}$ the delivered value is $y_t$ or a stop, and with coalition inputs as the protocol prescribes, thm-dataflow-composition (c) certifies every strategy (proven). A coalition client whose input departs from the protocol falls under (d). Verifiable aggregation in the oracle model is therefore exactly what collapses the admissible set of starting models to the protocol's value, and what makes the active case certifiable.

Without the oracle. A privacy bound against an active coalition without the oracle needs the law of the trained transformer at every admissible training input, and with state at every admissible history. With the oracle, (a) reduces every admissible strategy, adaptive ones included, to one tail per round for a stateless client and to one Bernoulli parameter of the tree execution for a client with state, and (b) certifies them for every learning function. Parts (c) and (d) show that no certifier sound over every learning function lowers these costs, and that an indirect choice through the aggregate makes the cost infinite. Criteria 1 to 3 hold. The tails are defined by coupled Train calls, the problem without the oracle needs the law of the trained model, and the bounds depend on the learning function only through oracle-measured tails.

Degenerate oracles. Part (a) is a statement about laws and holds under the bottom and the top oracle, since thm-dataflow-composition (b) does. Parts (b) to (d) concern the experimenter. In the drawn-seed discipline (b) fails. Take the realizable policy, $R=1$ and $\mathcal C_1=\{c\}$. The adversary's own calls are answered from $\omega$, independently of the release, so $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mathcal C}$ is the divergence of the release, which is the target $\theta$ of thm-dataflow-composition (e) after the translation $m\mapsto m-c$. By (e)(i), no drawn-seed certifier sound over every learning function outputs below $1-\eta$ with probability above $\alpha+\eta$ at the reference functions there, while every coupled pair coincides at them and (b)(i) outputs at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\Delta/\sigma)+\ln(1/\alpha)/n$ with certainty. The theorem therefore meets criterion 4 through its certificate, under item 3 of def-degenerate-oracles and the orchestrator's ruling. Parts (c) and (d) hold in both disciplines. The coupled discipline removes the price of the hidden seed and leaves the price of the admissible set.

### Record-level certificate [thm-record-level-certificate]

Setting. $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases (def-isolated-client), the coalition follows the protocol, and client $j$ holds a fixed dataset $D$, listed with multiplicity as $(z_1,\dots,z_{|D|})$. For $i\le|D|$, $D^{-i}$ is $D$ with one copy of $z_i$ removed. Pair $i$ is $H_0$, with $D_j=D$, against $H^i_1$, with $D_j=D^{-i}$. For a passive adversary $P_0$ and $P^i_1$ are its view laws, and under a fixed policy, realizable or seed-revealing, let

$$\mathrm H^{\to}_i(\varepsilon)=\sup\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P_0\|P^i_1),\qquad\mathrm H^{\leftarrow}_i(\varepsilon)=\sup\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P^i_1\|P_0),$$

with both suprema over passive adversaries. $\epsilon^i_0(\Delta)$ is the exceedance probability of def-isolated-client for pair $i$ along $H_0$, the same for every passive adversary. Fix $\Delta>0$ and write $\delta_R(\varepsilon)=\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)$. For $\varepsilon\ge0$ and $\bar\epsilon\ge0$ the fractions of records above threshold are
