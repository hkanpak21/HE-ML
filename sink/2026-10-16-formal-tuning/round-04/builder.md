Its cost, $\exp((\sqrt{\mathfrak e}+\sqrt{\ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})})^2)$ runs of the protocol's own execution, is free of $d$ and of $B$. It holds for clients with state and for every next-model rule.

Third, the lower bound is proven in the clipped procedure with $R$ rounds, through needles that fire only in round $R$. The learner name of each round is part of the choice, as def-isolated-client allows. Two packings give two bounds. Balls give the challenger's form, $(\frac{B_A}{2\sigma}\sqrt{(R-1)\eta/(2d)})^d$, useful when $B_A\gg\sigma\sqrt d$. Voronoi cells of the sphere code give $\min\{2^{0.18d},\eta e^{(R-1)B_A^2/(8\sigma^2)}\}$, useful from $B_A\approx\sigma$ on.

## Proofs

Standing facts. Rounds 01 to 03 list the test fact, data processing, the realization fact, Lyapunov's theorem, (G1) to (G4), the split, McDiarmid's inequality, the DKW inequality, lazy sampling and (F1) to (F4). Fact (F5) is withdrawn, since its only use was the limit form of thm-dataflow-composition (a)(iii). Two facts are added.

(F6) Change of measure. Let $P$ and $Q$ be laws on a standard Borel space with $P\ll Q$, and let $\varpi>1$. For every measurable $g$ with values in $[0,1]$,

$$\mathbb E_Pg\le\big(\mathbb E_Qg\big)^{1-1/\varpi}\Big(\mathbb E_Q\Big(\frac{dP}{dQ}\Big)^{\varpi}\Big)^{1/\varpi}.$$

Hölder's inequality with exponents $\varpi/(\varpi-1)$ and $\varpi$ gives

$$\mathbb E_P g=\mathbb E_Q\Big[g\,\frac{dP}{dQ}\Big]\le\big(\mathbb E_Qg^{\varpi/(\varpi-1)}\big)^{1-1/\varpi}\Big(\mathbb E_Q\Big(\frac{dP}{dQ}\Big)^{\varpi}\Big)^{1/\varpi},$$

and $g^{\varpi/(\varpi-1)}\le g$ on $[0,1]$. Moreover, with $\varphi_\sigma$ the density of $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ and $u\in\mathbb R^d$,

$$\int\Big(\frac{\varphi_\sigma(y-u)}{\varphi_\sigma(y)}\Big)^{\varpi}\varphi_\sigma(y)\,dy=\exp\Big(\frac{\varpi(\varpi-1)\|u\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big).$$

The ratio is $\exp(\langle y,u\rangle/\sigma^2-\|u\|^2/(2\sigma^2))$, and (G4) with the vector $\varpi u/\sigma^2$ gives $\mathbb E_Y e^{\varpi\langle Y,u\rangle/\sigma^2}=e^{\varpi^2\|u\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ for $Y\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$.

(F7) Gaussian norm. For $G\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and $\alpha'\in(0,1)$ let $r_d(\alpha')=(2d\ln2+4\ln(1/\alpha'))^{1/2}$. Then $\Pr[\|G\|>r_d(\alpha')]\le\alpha'$. Each coordinate has $\mathbb E e^{G_i^2/4}=(1-1/2)^{-1/2}=\sqrt2$, a Gaussian integral, so $\mathbb E e^{\|G\|^2/4}=2^{d/2}$, and Markov's inequality gives $\Pr[\|G\|^2>r^2]\le2^{d/2}e^{-r^2/4}$, which equals $\alpha'$ at $r=r_d(\alpha')$. The concentration of Lipschitz functions of a Gaussian vector gives the smaller radius $\sqrt d+\sqrt{2\ln(1/\alpha')}$ (Boucheron, Lugosi and Massart, Concentration Inequalities, 2013, Theorem 5.6, from memory). The proofs below use the proven form.

### Sphere code [lem-sphere-code]

Statement. (a) For every integer $d\ge1$ there are $J\ge2^{0.18d}$ points of the unit sphere of $\mathbb R^d$ whose pairwise distances are at least one. (b) Let $\mathbf p_1,\dots,\mathbf p_J\in\mathbb R^d$ have pairwise distances at least $s>0$, and let $V_k=\{y:\|y-\mathbf p_k\|<\|y-\mathbf p_l\|\text{ for every }l\neq k\}$. The sets $V_k$ are open and pairwise disjoint, and for every $\sigma'>0$ and $G\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma'^2I_d)$,

$$\Pr\big[\mathbf p_k+G\notin V_k\big]\le(J-1)\,\bar\Phi\Big(\frac{s}{2\sigma'}\Big)\le\frac J2\,e^{-s^2/(8\sigma'^2)}.$$

Label. Proven.

Proof. (a) Let $\mathrm H_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)$. For $p\in(0,1/2]$ and every integer $r\le pd$, $\sum_{i\le r}\binom di\le2^{d\mathrm H_2(p)}$. Indeed $1=\sum_i\binom dip^i(1-p)^{d-i}\ge\sum_{i\le r}\binom di(1-p)^d(p/(1-p))^i$, and $(p/(1-p))^i\ge(p/(1-p))^{pd}$ for $i\le pd$, since $p/(1-p)\le1$, while $(1-p)^d(p/(1-p))^{pd}=2^{-d\mathrm H_2(p)}$. Let $\mathcal W\subseteq\{\pm1\}^d$ be a maximal set of words with pairwise Hamming distances at least $\lceil d/4\rceil$. Every word lies within Hamming distance $\lceil d/4\rceil-1$ of some word of $\mathcal W$, or it could be added. Since $\lceil d/4\rceil-1\le d/4$, the bound with $p=1/4$ gives $|\mathcal W|\,2^{d\mathrm H_2(1/4)}\ge2^d$. Now $1-\mathrm H_2(1/4)=0.1887\ldots\ge0.18$ (computed), so $|\mathcal W|\ge2^{0.18d}$. The points $\omega/\sqrt d$ for $\omega\in\mathcal W$ lie on the unit sphere, and two of them have squared distance $4\,\mathrm{Ham}(\omega,\omega')/d\ge4\lceil d/4\rceil/d\ge1$.

(b) $V_k$ is a finite intersection of open half-spaces, and a point of $V_k\cap V_l$ would be strictly nearer to each of $\mathbf p_k,\mathbf p_l$ than to the other. The point $\mathbf p_k+G$ lies outside $V_k$ only if $\|\mathbf p_k+G-\mathbf p_l\|\le\|G\|$ for some $l\neq k$. Expanding the square, this is $\langle G,\mathbf e\rangle\ge\|\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k\|/2$ with the unit vector $\mathbf e=(\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k)/\|\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k\|$, an event of probability $\bar\Phi(\|\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k\|/(2\sigma'))\le\bar\Phi(s/(2\sigma'))$. A union bound over $l$ and (G3) finish. $\square$

Without the oracle. The lemma is geometry and involves no oracle. Its role is the lower bounds of thm-smoothed-choice-certificate (d) and thm-dataflow-composition (e)(ii), where it gives exponentially many disjoint places in which a learning function can hide leakage.

Degenerate oracles. Not applicable. The lemma claims no criterion of non-triviality.

Prior work. Part (a) is the Gilbert and Varshamov bound (Gilbert 1952, Varshamov 1957, from memory) with the standard entropy bound on binomial sums. Part (b) is the union bound for nearest-point decoding of a Gaussian channel. I claim no novelty.

### Smoothed-choice certificate [thm-smoothed-choice-certificate]

Setting, the procedure $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$. Fix $R\ge2$, $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^d$, $B\in(0,\infty]$, $B_A\ge0$, a class $\mathcal H$, distinct learner names $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_R$ and $m_1\in\mathbb R^d$. The parties are an honest stateless client $j$, an honest server and a coalition client $j'$, and the coalition is $\{j'\}$. The choice of round $t$ is $c_t=(t,m_t)\in\{1,\dots,R\}\times\mathbb R^d$, with training input $x(c_t,D)=(\ell_t,m_t,D,\mathcal H)$ and release map $f_t((t,m),W)=\mathrm{clip}_B(W-m)$. Client $j$ owns the input node of $D_j$, released through no channel. Client $j'$ owns, for each $t$, an input node $a_t$ with value $u_t\in\mathbb R^d$ and only child $r_t$, and $O_t=u_t$. The release $y_t=u_t+\mathrm{clip}_B(W_t-m_t)+Z_t$ reaches the coalition through the plain channel, and an honest general node, a parent of $v_{t+1}$, sets $m_{t+1}=m_t+y_t$. The protocol sets $u_t=0$. A strategy is admissible when $\|u_t\|\le B_A$ in every round that runs. It may be adaptive, make any oracle calls and stop after a release. $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases, since $c_t$ is the value of a parent of $v_t$, the only child of $v_t$ is $r_t$, and the coalition owns neither $v_t$ nor $r_t$. Fix $\varepsilon\ge0$, $\gamma=e^\varepsilon$, $\Delta>0$ and a policy, realizable or seed-revealing. The target is

$$\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R(\mathsf L)=\sup\max\big\{\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1),\,\mathrm H_\gamma(P_1\|P_0)\big\},$$

the supremum over the adversaries whose strategy is admissible.

Setting of part (c), any isolated procedure. $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases, with or without state. For each $t\le R$ the coalition owns input nodes whose only child is $r_t$, $u_t\in\mathbb R^d$ is the sum of their values, and $O_t=u_t+o_t$, where $o_t$ is a measurable function of the parents of $r_t$ other than $v_t$ and these input nodes, and of randomness of $r_t$ independent of $Z_t$. A strategy is $B_A$-bounded when every choice that enters a node other than these input nodes is the protocol's, $\|u_t\|\le B_A$ in every round that runs, and it may stop after a release. The null strategy is the $B_A$-bounded strategy with $u_t=0$ that never stops, and $\epsilon^{\mathrm{nl}}_b(\Delta)$ is its exceedance probability along $H_b$. Let $\theta^{\mathrm{bd}}$ and $\theta^{\mathrm{bd},\leftarrow}$ be the suprema of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ and of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_1\|P_0)$ over the adversaries with a $B_A$-bounded strategy. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ the admissible strategies are the $B_A$-bounded ones and the protocol's strategy is the null strategy. Let

$$\mathfrak e=\frac{(R-1)B_A^2}{2\sigma^2},\qquad\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)=\exp\Big(-\big(\sqrt{\ln(1/p)}-\sqrt{\mathfrak e}\big)_+^2\Big)\ \text{ for }p\in(0,1],\qquad\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(0)=0.$$

Smoothed tails. For $t\le R$ and $z\in\mathbb R^d$ let $\tilde\beta_t(z)=\mathbb E\,\beta_t((t,z+Z),\Delta)$ with $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$, where $\beta_t$ is the coupled tail of a stateless client. For $\alpha_{\mathrm{esc}}\in(0,1)$ let $\varrho_2=B_A+B$ and $\varrho_t=(t-1)(B_A+B)+\sigma\sqrt{t-2}\;r_d(\alpha_{\mathrm{esc}}/R)$ for $t\ge3$. Let $\bar\beta^{\mathrm{sm}}_1=\beta_1((1,m_1),\Delta)$ and $\bar\beta^{\mathrm{sm}}_t=\sup\{\tilde\beta_t(z):\|z-m_1\|\le\varrho_t\}$ for $t\ge2$.

Statement.

(a) Product bound. Let $B<\infty$. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$, for every admissible strategy, every $b\in\{0,1\}$ and every $\alpha_{\mathrm{esc}}\in(0,1)$,

$$\epsilon_b(\Delta)\le1-\prod_{t\le R}\big(1-\bar\beta^{\mathrm{sm}}_t\big)+\alpha_{\mathrm{esc}},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R\le\delta_{\mathcal N}\big(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma\big)+1-\prod_{t\le R}\big(1-\bar\beta^{\mathrm{sm}}_t\big)+\alpha_{\mathrm{esc}}.$$

Fix $\varepsilon'$ with $\gamma'<e^{\varepsilon'}$ and $\varepsilon'<\varepsilon\sqrt{(R-1)/R}$. Let $\iota=\varepsilon'/\Delta'$ and let $\Delta'\to0$. By (F4) in both directions,

$$\frac{\bar\Phi(\iota-\Delta')}{\bar\Phi(\iota)}\ge\frac{\iota(\iota-\Delta')}{1+(\iota-\Delta')^2}\,e^{\iota\Delta'-\Delta'^2/2}=\frac{\iota(\iota-\Delta')}{1+(\iota-\Delta')^2}\,e^{\varepsilon'-\Delta'^2/2}\to e^{\varepsilon'}.$$

By (F3), $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;v)\le\bar\Phi(u)$ with $u=\varepsilon/v-v/2$, positive for small $\Delta'$, and (F4) gives

$$\frac{\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;v)}{\bar\Phi(\iota)}\le\frac{1+\iota^2}{\iota\,u}\exp\Big(-\frac{u^2-\iota^2}{2}\Big),\qquad u^2-\iota^2\ge\frac{\varepsilon^2(R-1)/R-\varepsilon'^2}{\Delta'^2}-\varepsilon,$$

since $u^2\ge\varepsilon^2/v^2-\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon^2/v^2=\varepsilon^2(R-1)/(R\Delta'^2)$. The coefficient $\varepsilon^2(R-1)/R-\varepsilon'^2$ is positive, and $(1+\iota^2)/(\iota u)$ stays bounded because $\iota$ and $u$ both grow like $1/\Delta'$. So this ratio tends to zero, and the ratio of the two sides of the needed inequality tends to at least $e^{\varepsilon'}>\gamma'$. For small $\Delta'$ and then large $\mathfrak s$ the strict inequality holds. Letting $R$ grow makes $e^{\varepsilon\sqrt{(R-1)/R}}$ approach $e^\varepsilon$. $\square$
