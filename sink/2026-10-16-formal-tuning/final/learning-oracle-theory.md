# Definitions and prominent results of the learning oracle

## What the theory shows

A privacy or security proof for a procedure that trains a model must say how the trained model depends on the data. For a transformer trained by stochastic gradient descent nobody can write that law down, and one sample of it costs one training run. Proofs about procedures with transformer-sized learning nodes therefore assume a mechanism with a known law, such as clipping and noise inside training.

The theory replaces each training run by a call, Train, to an idealized training oracle. It runs an arbitrary learning function on a training input and a seed, and names the seed. A caller can rerun training on another input with the same seed, which samples a coupling of two runs. Every bound below depends on the learning function only through quantities that such calls define. So each bound holds for every learning function, transformers included. Train calls certify those quantities where a result says so.

Two degenerate oracles test whether a result uses the oracle. The bottom oracle gives an adversary what a real training service gives, and the top oracle also hands it the honest seeds. For an experimenter or an auditor the two cases are seeds that the oracle draws and hides, and seeds that the caller passes. A result counts as non-trivial when four conditions hold. The oracle is in it, and removing the oracle leaves a problem that needs the law of the trained model. Its bound depends on the learning function only through oracle-measured quantities, and it fails, or becomes vacuous, under one degenerate case.

The main results follow. Each claim is labelled proven, with a proof here or in a round file, heuristic, with its assumption, or open. Here $R$ is a number of rounds, $\sigma$ a noise scale, $d$ the model dimension, $B_A$ a bound on a coalition's deviation and $\alpha$ the error probability of a certifier.

1. Coupling certificate. One training node leaks at most the coupled divergence. Coupled Train calls certify it for every learning function when the divergence of the channel at two models is computable. Through a deterministic channel with atomless view laws, drawn seeds certify nothing below one for the total variation (proven).
2. Black-box certifiability. Label-only leakage at a fixed number of queries, on a finite query set with finitely many outputs, is certifiable from both sides. With deterministic evaluation and atomless model laws, drawn seeds never certify that leakage below one equals white-box leakage (proven).
3. Dataflow composition. If client data reach a coalition only via $R$ Gaussian releases, as def-isolated-client makes precise, leakage is at most a Gaussian term plus the exceedance probability. That is the probability that coupled updates differ by more than a threshold. Coupled runs of one strategy certify it, with a number of runs free of $d$ (proven).
4. Bounded-deviation transfer. Over the same releases, let a coalition follow the protocol except for additive inputs within $B_A$ of the prescribed ones. Its exceedance probability is then at most a fixed function of the protocol's, set by $\mathfrak e=(R-1)B_A^2/(2\sigma^2)$. Coupled runs of the protocol certify this bound at level $\alpha$, free of $d$, and $e^{(1+o(1))\mathfrak e}\ln(1/\alpha)$ runs reach a fixed accuracy when none exceeds the threshold. In the clipped aggregation instance, let a certifier sound for every learning function be accurate where the leakage is zero, below a fixed fraction of one clipped release's leakage. At fixed error probabilities summing below one, it needs $e^{(1-o(1))\mathfrak e}$ Train calls when $\mathfrak e/d\to0$ (proven).
5. Replay-audit steering. Let seeds come from a random oracle and the dataset be committed. A prover then passes a replay audit with a model in a target set with probability at most a multiple of the target's reach. Under deterministic kernels, grinding with at least one random-oracle query attains this bound within a factor $3.2$ if the reach times the query count is at most one (proven).
6. Needle lower bound. A certifier sound for every learning function that makes $N$ Train calls excludes leakage placed in one of $J$ disjoint places with probability at most $\alpha+N/J$ (proven).

## Definitions

Every space below is a standard Borel space. A remark after a definition is not part of it. `theory/CURRENT.md` adds the clauses that only the results stated with pointers use.

### Learning oracle [def-learning-oracle]

Fix the data space $\mathcal Z$, the model space $\mathcal M$, the query space $\mathcal U$, the output space $\mathcal Y$, the set $\Lambda$ of learner names and the space $\mathcal X$ of training inputs. A *dataset* is a finite multiset over $\mathcal Z$. A *training input* $x=(\ell,m,D,\mathcal H)$ holds a learner name $\ell$, a starting model $m$, a dataset $D$ and a hypothesis class $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal M$ from a finite family. The *learning function* is a measurable map $\mathsf L:\mathcal X\times\mathcal R\times\Xi\to\mathcal M$, written $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$. The *seed* $\rho$ has an atomless law $\mu$ on $\mathcal R$, and the *hardware randomness* $\xi$ has a law $\pi$ on $\Xi$. $\Pi_x$ is the law of $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ under $\mu\otimes\pi$. Training has *deterministic kernels* when $\Xi$ is a point. The *evaluation map* $\mathsf{ev}(m,u;\upsilon)\in\mathcal Y$, with $\upsilon$ of law $\nu$, is public.

A *learning oracle* $\mathcal O$ for $(\mathsf L,\mu,\pi)$ stores models and seeds and names them per caller. The $k$-th name a caller receives is the integer $k$, and each name carries a label, open or eval. $\mathcal O$ answers three calls.

1. $\mathsf{Train}(x,s)$. The starting model in $x$ is a model or a model name, the dataset is given by value, and $s$ is a seed, a seed name or $\bot$. On $\bot$ the oracle draws $\rho\sim\mu$ and stores it under a new name. Otherwise the run uses the seed that $s$ gives. The oracle draws $\xi\sim\pi$, stores $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ under a new name and returns the new names.
2. $\mathsf{Eval}(h,u)$, for a model name $h$, returns $\mathsf{ev}(m,u;\upsilon)$ for the stored model $m$ and a fresh $\upsilon\sim\nu$.
3. $\mathsf{Open}(h)$ returns the stored object.

A *grant* gives a caller a new name of a stored object, with a label, when a procedure or a policy directs it. A grant is not a call. Fidelity. Every seed drawn on $\bot$, every $\xi$ and every $\upsilon$ is independent of all other randomness. Access. Train and Open require label open on every name among their arguments, and Eval requires label eval or open. The oracle refuses any other call. A caller holds label open on every name that its own call returns, and it holds a name only in this way or through a grant.

Remark. Label eval gives black-box access to a model, and label open white-box access. Fidelity of hardware randomness is a modelling assumption, heuristic for GPU training.

### Learning-oracle model [def-learning-oracle-model]

A *procedure* is a dataflow graph $G$ whose nodes run in a fixed topological order. Each node has an owner and one of four operations. An input node holds a dataset, a seed or a public object, and a sampler of fresh records is one. A learning node calls $\mathsf{Train}(x_v,s_v)$, with $x_v$ formed from parent values and choices, and $s_v=\bot$ unless the procedure feeds the node a seed node. An evaluation node calls Eval. A general node computes a randomized function of its parents with randomness of its own, which the oracle does not hold. In $G^{\mathcal O}$ the calls go to $\mathcal O$, and the procedure grants each honest owner label open on the objects its node uses. In the real procedure $G$ the nodes compute $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$ directly with fresh randomness.

Each node value reaches a *coalition* $A$ through one of three channels. Through the plain channel $A$ receives the value. Through the query channel the procedure grants $A$ a name of the value with label eval, and it fixes a query set $U\subseteq\mathcal U$ and an allowance $Q$. The oracle then answers at most $Q$ Eval calls of $A$ on that name, each at a query in $U$. Through channel none $A$ receives nothing.

The coalition acts through an *adversary* $\mathcal A$, an oracle algorithm that reaches $\mathsf L$ only through $\mathcal O$, computes $\mathsf{ev}$ itself and stops with probability one. It is unbounded, except in a statement with a computational assumption, which bounds its running time. Its *choices* are the node arguments that $A$ sets, the values and learner names of nodes that $A$ owns, and its queries. They also include the learner names of nodes whose training code $A$ supplies. A strategy is *passive* when the choices that enter procedure nodes are the functions of procedure values that the protocol prescribes, and *active* otherwise.

For each node in order, the adversary receives the channel outputs of the nodes run so far, makes oracle calls and sets its choices for the node. Then the node runs. After the last node it makes further calls and stops. Its *view* is its transcript, made of the channel outputs, the answers to its calls and its coins. The *budget* $(q_T,q_E)$ bounds its Train calls and its Eval calls on names with label eval. Calls that the procedure requires of an owner in $A$ are not charged, and an Eval call on a name with label open is free.

To *distinguish*, the adversary faces hypotheses $H_0,H_1$ on the inputs and outputs a bit, with advantage $\Pr[1\mid H_1]-\Pr[1\mid H_0]$. A privacy statement bounds the hockey-stick divergence $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)=\sup_E(P_0(E)-\gamma P_1(E))$, for $\gamma=e^\varepsilon\ge1$, over measurable sets $E$ of views. Here $P_b$ is the view's law under $H_b$. At $\gamma=1$ the divergence is the total variation $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$. To *steer*, the adversary faces a node, a target set $T\subseteq\mathcal M$ and an acceptance event, and succeeds when the node's value is in $T$ and the event holds.

Remarks. A sum, a ciphertext or a noisy value is a general node released through the plain channel. To invert, an adversary wins on an event $E$ of views, and $P_0(E)\le e^\varepsilon P_1(E)+\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P_0\|P_1)$ by the definition of $\mathrm H_\gamma$ (proven). So every privacy bound below also bounds inversion.

### Certifier [def-certifier]

An *experimenter* is a caller outside every procedure that reaches $\mathsf L$ only through $\mathcal O$. A caller works in the *drawn-seed discipline* when every Train call it makes has $s=\bot$ and it opens no seed name, and in the *coupled discipline* otherwise. A *target* is a functional $\theta(\mathsf L)\in[0,1]$. A *certifier* is an experimenter that makes at most $N$ Train calls and outputs $\hat t\in[0,1]$. It is *sound* at level $\alpha$ over a class $\mathfrak L$ of learning functions when $\Pr_{\mathsf L}[\theta(\mathsf L)\le\hat t]\ge1-\alpha$ for every $\mathsf L\in\mathfrak L$. A lower certifier outputs $\underline t$ with $\Pr_{\mathsf L}[\underline t\le\theta(\mathsf L)]\ge1-\alpha$.

Remarks. The main targets have the channel form. For training inputs $x_0\neq x_1$ and a channel $K$, a Markov kernel from $\mathcal M$ to a view space $\mathcal V$, the view laws are $P_b=\int K(\cdot\mid m)\,\Pi_{x_b}(dm)$, and $\theta$ is $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ or $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$. A deterministic channel is a measurable map $\kappa:\mathcal M\to\mathcal V$. A certifier sound for a target $\theta'\ge\theta$ is sound for $\theta$. So each lower bound below binds certifiers of every larger target (proven).

### Degenerate oracles [def-degenerate-oracles]

Fix a procedure $G$ and a coalition $A$. A *policy* directs grants to $A$ beyond those of the procedure.

1. The *bottom oracle* is the realizable policy, which grants nothing, with no bound on $q_T$.
2. The *top oracle* is the seed-revealing policy, which grants $A$ label open on the seed name of every honest learning node when the node runs.
3. For an experimenter, or an honest party whose Train calls a theorem designs such as an auditor, the bottom and the top case are the drawn-seed and the coupled discipline.

A statement about adversaries is tested under the bottom and the top oracle, and a statement about an experimenter or an auditor under the two disciplines.

Remark. The bottom oracle is the real procedure against an adversary that samples $\mathsf L$ (thm-perfect-substitution). An adversary can hard-wire one fixed learning function, so knowing $\mathsf L$ matters only when a statement chooses the adversary first. Drawn seeds match a training service that hides its randomness.

### Isolated client [def-isolated-client]

Fix a client $j$, candidate datasets $D^0_j\neq D^1_j$, a space $\mathcal C$ of choices, a number $R\ge1$ of rounds, a dimension $d$ and a noise scale $\sigma>0$. Fix also an initial state $w_0\in\mathcal M$, a measurable map $(c,D,w)\mapsto x(c,D,w)\in\mathcal X$ and measurable release maps $f_t:\mathcal C\times\mathcal M\to\mathbb R^d$. A choice holds the starting model and everything else that the node's input takes from outside the client, the learner name of the round included. Let $G$ be a procedure in which client $j$ owns the input node of $D_j$. Let its nodes include, for $t\le R$, a learning node $v_t$ owned by $j$ and a general node $r_t$, with $v_1\prec r_1\prec\dots\prec v_R\prec r_R$. Then $G$ *isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases* when the following hold.

1. $v_t$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D_j,W_{t-1}),\bot)$, where $W_t$ is the value of $v_t$ and $W_0=w_0$. Here $c_t$ is the value of a parent of $v_t$ other than $v_{t-1}$ and the input node of $D_j$, or a choice of the coalition.
2. No node other than $v_1,\dots,v_R$ reads $D_j$, and the children of $v_t$ are among $r_t$ and $v_{t+1}$.
3. $r_t$ outputs the release $y_t=O_t+f_t(c_t,W_t)+Z_t$, where the noise $Z_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is randomness of $r_t$. The contribution $O_t$ is a measurable function of the other parents of $r_t$ and of randomness of $r_t$ independent of $Z_t$.
4. The coalition owns no $v_t$ or $r_t$, and the input node of $D_j$ and every $v_t$ reach it through channel none.

The client is stateless when $x(c,D,w)$ does not depend on $w$. The procedure may stop after a release, on a value the coalition sets, and then no later node runs. Under $H_b$, $D_j=D^b_j$ and every other input is fixed, the same under both.

The *coupled execution* $\mathsf{Ex}_b$ runs $G$ under $H_b$ against a given strategy. At each $v_t$ that runs it also computes the twin $W'_t=\mathsf L(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j,W'_{t-1});\rho_t,\xi'_t)$, with $W'_0=w_0$, the seed $\rho_t$ of $v_t$ and fresh $\xi'_t$. No node reads a twin. With the maximum over the rounds that run, the *exceedance* and the *exceedance probability* at a threshold $\Delta>0$ are $D^\star_b=\max_t\|f_t(c_t,W'_t)-f_t(c_t,W_t)\|$ and $\epsilon_b(\Delta)=\Pr[D^\star_b>\Delta]$.

For states $w^0,w^1$ the *coupled tail* is $\beta_t(c,w^0,w^1,\Delta)=\Pr[\|f_t(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^1_j,w^1);\rho,\xi_1))-f_t(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^0_j,w^0);\rho,\xi_0))\|>\Delta]$, with $\rho\sim\mu$ and $\xi_0,\xi_1\sim\pi$ independent. For a stateless client it is $\beta_t(c,\Delta)$.

Remark. An experimenter that holds every input and runs the strategy samples $\mathsf{Ex}_b$. Per round it makes two Train calls for client $j$, the second with the first's seed name, plus those of the other nodes and of the strategy. A passive strategy also takes the protocol's stop. Its choices fix the law of $D^\star_b$, so every passive adversary has the same $\epsilon_b$, and the experimenter need not make the strategy's own calls.

## Standing facts

The proofs use the following facts. Each is standard, and a source or a one-line proof is given.

- (F1) Test fact. The best advantage of a test between laws $P_0$ and $P_1$ is $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$, attained on a set where the density of $P_1$ exceeds that of $P_0$ (Neyman and Pearson).
- (F2) Data processing. For a Markov kernel $K$ and $\gamma\ge1$, $\mathrm H_\gamma(KP_0\|KP_1)\le\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$. A measurable function with values in $[0,1]$ has integral against $P_0-\gamma P_1$ at most $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$.
- (F3) Splits. For laws $\mathcal L,\mathcal L',\mathcal L''$ on one space, $\mathrm H_\gamma(\mathcal L\|\mathcal L'')\le\mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L,\mathcal L')+\mathrm H_\gamma(\mathcal L'\|\mathcal L'')$ and $\mathrm H_\gamma(\mathcal L''\|\mathcal L)\le\mathrm H_\gamma(\mathcal L''\|\mathcal L')+\gamma\,\mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L',\mathcal L)$. Add and subtract $\mathcal L'(E)$ inside the supremum.
- (F4) Mixtures. For a law $\Theta$ of a pair $(\mathfrak w_0,\mathfrak w_1)$ and kernels $K_0,K_1$ to $\mathcal V$, $\mathrm H_\gamma(\Theta K_0\|\Theta K_1)\le\int\mathrm H_\gamma(K_0(\cdot\mid\mathfrak w_0)\|K_1(\cdot\mid\mathfrak w_1))\,\Theta(d\mathfrak w)$. Integrate $K_0(E\mid\mathfrak w_0)-\gamma K_1(E\mid\mathfrak w_1)$ against $\Theta$.
- (F5) Averaging. For a law $\mathbb P_{\mathfrak w}$ of $\mathfrak w$ and kernels $\mathfrak w\mapsto P^{\mathfrak w}_0,P^{\mathfrak w}_1$, the laws $\mathbb P_{\mathfrak w}(d\mathfrak w)P^{\mathfrak w}_b(dy)$ have divergence $\int\mathrm H_\gamma(P^{\mathfrak w}_0\|P^{\mathfrak w}_1)\,\mathbb P_{\mathfrak w}(d\mathfrak w)$. Sections give the upper bound, and densities with Dynkin's theorem the lower one (round-01/builder.md, coupling certificate, step 3 of (b)).
- (F6) Gaussian facts. $\Phi$, $\bar\Phi=1-\Phi$ and $\varphi$ are the standard normal distribution function, tail and density, and $\varphi_\sigma$ is the density of $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$. (i) $\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(\mathcal N(\mathbf a_0,\sigma^2I_d)\|\mathcal N(\mathbf a_1,\sigma^2I_d))=\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\|\mathbf a_0-\mathbf a_1\|/\sigma)$, where $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;r)=\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(\mathcal N(0,1)\|\mathcal N(r,1))$ is nondecreasing in the shift $r\ge0$ and, for $r>0$, equals $\Phi(r/2-\varepsilon/r)-e^\varepsilon\Phi(-r/2-\varepsilon/r)$ (Balle and Wang, ICML 2018, from memory). (ii) For $\mathfrak f\in[0,1]$ and $r>0$, the kernel $\mathfrak z\mapsto\mathfrak f(\mathfrak z-r)+\sqrt{1-\mathfrak f^2}\,Z'$ with $Z'\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ maps $\mathcal N(r,1)$ to $\mathcal N(0,1)$ and $\mathcal N(0,1)$ to $\mathcal N(-\mathfrak fr,1)$. (iii) $\bar\Phi(r)\le\frac12e^{-r^2/2}$ for $r\ge0$. (iv) $\frac{r}{1+r^2}\varphi(r)\le\bar\Phi(r)\le\varphi(r)/r$ for $r>0$. (v) $\mathbb Ee^{\langle Z,\mathbf a\rangle}=e^{\sigma^2\|\mathbf a\|^2/2}$ for $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$.
- (F7) Change of measure. For laws $\mathbb P'\ll\mathbb P$ and $\varpi>1$, Hölder's inequality gives $\mathbb P'(\cdot)\le\mathbb P(\cdot)^{1-1/\varpi}(\int(d\mathbb P'/d\mathbb P)^{\varpi}d\mathbb P)^{1/\varpi}$ on every measurable set. By (F6)(v), $\int(\varphi_\sigma(y-\mathbf a)/\varphi_\sigma(y))^{\varpi}\varphi_\sigma(y)\,dy=\exp(\varpi(\varpi-1)\|\mathbf a\|^2/(2\sigma^2))$, the Gaussian Rényi moment.
- (F8) Statistics. The one-sided Clopper and Pearson upper bound at level $\alpha$ covers a binomial parameter with probability at least $1-\alpha$ at every value. With no success in $n$ trials it equals $1-\alpha^{1/n}\le\ln(1/\alpha)/n$. Hoeffding's inequality bounds the deviation of a mean of $n$ independent variables in $[0,1]$ by $\lambda$ except with probability $e^{-2n\lambda^2}$ per side. McDiarmid's inequality gives $e^{-2\lambda^2/\sum_k\mathfrak b_k^2}$ for a function of $n$ independent arguments whose $k$-th argument moves it by at most $\mathfrak b_k$.
- (F9) Realization. A standard atomless probability space is isomorphic modulo null sets to $[0,1)$ with Lebesgue measure (Kechris, Classical Descriptive Set Theory, Theorem 17.41).

The round files number these facts differently, and `theory/CURRENT.md` maps their numbers to these.

## Needle lower bound [lem-needle-lower-bound]

Statement. Let $\theta$ be a target and $\vartheta\in[0,1]$. Let $\mathsf L^\circ$ be a learning function, the *reference function*, and $X_1,\dots,X_J$ pairwise disjoint measurable sets of training inputs. For each $k\le J$ let $\mathsf L_k$ agree with $\mathsf L^\circ$ at every training input outside $X_k$ and satisfy $\theta(\mathsf L_k)\ge\vartheta$. Every certifier of $\theta$ with at most $N$ Train calls, in either discipline, sound at level $\alpha$ over a class that contains $\mathsf L_1,\dots,\mathsf L_J$, satisfies $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha-N/J$.

Label. Proven.

Without the oracle, that is without Train calls, a certifier sound at the needle functions behaves at the reference function as at each of them. So it outputs at least $\vartheta$ there with probability at least $1-\alpha$. With $N$ Train calls it rules out at most $N$ of the $J$ places where leakage can hide, whatever seeds it uses.

Proof. Fix the certifier and $k$. Put its runs against $\mathsf L^\circ$ and against $\mathsf L_k$ on one probability space. Both runs use the same coins, and the $i$-th oracle call of both uses the same triple $(\rho'_i,\xi'_i,\upsilon'_i)\sim\mu\otimes\pi\otimes\nu$, independent across $i$. By fidelity each run has the law that its oracle prescribes. Names are integers fixed by the count of calls, so while the transcripts agree the two oracles store the same objects under the same names. A Train call outside $X_k$ gets the same answer in both runs, and so does every Eval and Open call. By induction the runs agree up to the first Train call with training input in $X_k$. Let $\mathrm{Hit}_k$ be the event that the run against $\mathsf L^\circ$ makes such a call. Off $\mathrm{Hit}_k$ the outputs agree, so $\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\le\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]+\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\mathrm{Hit}_k]$. Soundness at $\mathsf L_k$ gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge\Pr_{\mathsf L_k}[\hat t\ge\theta(\mathsf L_k)]\ge1-\alpha$. A Train call names one training input, lying in at most one $X_k$, so $\sum_k\mathbf 1[\mathrm{Hit}_k]\le N$. Averaging over $k$ gives the claim. $\square$

## Sphere code [lem-sphere-code]

Statement. (a) For every integer $d\ge1$ there are at least $2^{0.18d}$ points of the unit sphere of $\mathbb R^d$ with pairwise distances at least one. (b) Let $\mathbf p_1,\dots,\mathbf p_J\in\mathbb R^d$ have pairwise distances at least $\mathfrak s>0$, and let $V_k$ be the set of points strictly nearer to $\mathbf p_k$ than to every other $\mathbf p_l$. The cells $V_k$ are open and pairwise disjoint, and $\Pr[\mathbf p_k+\mathbf g\notin V_k]\le(J-1)\bar\Phi(\mathfrak s/(2\sigma'))\le\frac J2e^{-\mathfrak s^2/(8\sigma'^2)}$ for $\mathbf g\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma'^2I_d)$.

Label. Proven.

Proof. (a) Let $\mathrm h_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)$. For $p\le1/2$ and every integer $r\le pd$, $\sum_{i\le r}\binom di\le2^{d\mathrm h_2(p)}$, since $1\ge\sum_{i\le r}\binom di(1-p)^d(p/(1-p))^i$ and $(p/(1-p))^i\ge(p/(1-p))^{pd}$. Let $\mathcal W\subseteq\{\pm1\}^d$ be maximal with pairwise Hamming distances at least $\lceil d/4\rceil$. Every word lies within $\lceil d/4\rceil-1\le d/4$ of $\mathcal W$, so $|\mathcal W|\,2^{d\mathrm h_2(1/4)}\ge2^d$. Since $1-\mathrm h_2(1/4)=0.1887$ (computed), $|\mathcal W|\ge2^{0.18d}$. The points $\mathbf w/\sqrt d$ for $\mathbf w\in\mathcal W$ have squared distances $4\,\mathrm{Ham}(\mathbf w,\mathbf w')/d\ge1$. (b) $V_k$ is an intersection of open half-spaces. $\mathbf p_k+\mathbf g\notin V_k$ needs $\|\mathbf p_k+\mathbf g-\mathbf p_l\|\le\|\mathbf g\|$ for some $l\neq k$, which is $\langle\mathbf g,\mathbf e\rangle\ge\|\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k\|/2$ for the unit vector $\mathbf e$ along $\mathbf p_l-\mathbf p_k$. A union bound and (F6)(iii) finish. $\square$

## Coupling certificate [thm-coupling-certificate]

Setting. One learning node trains $x_b$ under $H_b$, with $x_0\neq x_1$, and its model reaches the view through a channel $K$, with view laws $P_b$ as in def-certifier. The *identity coupling* $\Gamma$ is the law of $(W^\Gamma_0,W^\Gamma_1)=(\mathsf L(x_0;\rho,\xi_0),\mathsf L(x_1;\rho,\xi_1))$, with $\rho\sim\mu$ and $\xi_0,\xi_1\sim\pi$ all independent. An experimenter samples it with $\mathsf{Train}(x_0,\bot)$, which returns a seed name $s$, and $\mathsf{Train}(x_1,s)$. For $\gamma\ge1$ the *coupled divergence* is $\beta_\gamma(K)=\mathbb E_\Gamma\,\mathrm H_\gamma(K(\cdot\mid W^\Gamma_0)\|K(\cdot\mid W^\Gamma_1))$, and the *divergence probability* is $\beta=\Pr_\Gamma[W^\Gamma_0\neq W^\Gamma_1]$. Let $P^\top_b$ be the law of $(\rho,Y)$ with $\rho\sim\mu$, $\xi\sim\pi$ and $Y\sim K(\cdot\mid\mathsf L(x_b;\rho,\xi))$, which is what the top oracle shows an adversary that sees the node through $K$. The *top divergence* is $\mathrm D^\top_\gamma(K)=\mathrm H_\gamma(P^\top_0\|P^\top_1)$.

Statement. (a) Drawn seeds. (i) Fix an event $E\subseteq\mathcal V$ in advance and make $n$ drawn-seed calls per input. One-sided Clopper and Pearson bounds at level $\alpha/2$, below on $P_1(E)$ and above on $P_0(E)$, give a lower certifier of $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ at level $\alpha$, sound for every learning function. (ii) For finite $\mathcal V$ and the empirical law $\hat p_b$ of $n$ drawn-seed views at $x_b$, $\min\{1,\mathrm{TV}(\hat p_0,\hat p_1)+\sqrt{|\mathcal V|/n}+\sqrt{2\ln(2/\alpha)/n}\}$ is sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function. (iii) For a deterministic channel and atomless $P_0,P_1$, no drawn-seed certifier of $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ sound over every learning function outputs $\hat t<1$ with probability above $\alpha$.

(b) Shared seeds. For every learning function, every channel $K$ and every $\gamma\ge1$, $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)\le\mathrm D^\top_\gamma(K)\le\beta_\gamma(K)\le\beta$, with $\mathrm D^\top_\gamma(K)=\beta_\gamma(K)$ under deterministic kernels. At $\gamma=1$ the left end is the best advantage under the bottom oracle, and $\mathrm D^\top_1(K)$ the best under the top oracle, at every budget. If $(m,m')\mapsto\mathrm H_\gamma(K(\cdot\mid m)\|K(\cdot\mid m'))$ is computable, then from $n$ independent draws of $\Gamma$, at $2n$ Train calls, $\hat t=\min\{1,\frac1n\sum_{i\le n}\mathrm H_\gamma(K(\cdot\mid W^{\Gamma,i}_0)\|K(\cdot\mid W^{\Gamma,i}_1))+\sqrt{\ln(1/\alpha)/(2n)}\}$ is sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function, for $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ and for $\mathrm D^\top_\gamma(K)$. For the Gaussian channel $K(\cdot\mid m)=\mathcal N(f(m),\sigma^2I_d)$ and every $\Delta>0$, $\beta_{e^\varepsilon}(K)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\Delta/\sigma)+\Pr_\Gamma[\|f(W^\Gamma_0)-f(W^\Gamma_1)\|>\Delta]$.

## Black-box certifiability [thm-black-box-certifiability]

Setting. One learning node trains $x_b$ under $H_b$, and its model reaches the adversary through the query channel, with a finite query set $U$ and an allowance $Q\ge q_E$. The output space $\mathcal Y$ is finite with $C\ge2$ elements, and $\mathsf{ev}$ may be randomized. A *strategy tree* $\mathfrak t$ with $q$ queries is a complete $C$-ary tree of depth $q$ whose internal nodes carry queries in $U$ and whose leaves carry bits. $\mathfrak T_q$ is the set of these trees, so $|\mathfrak T_q|\le|U|^{(C^q-1)/(C-1)}2^{C^q}$. For a tree let $\mathrm{Adv}(\mathfrak t)=\Pr_{H_1}[\mathfrak t=1]-\Pr_{H_0}[\mathfrak t=1]$, with the model drawn from $\Pi_{x_b}$ and fresh evaluation randomness per query. Let $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)=\max_{\mathfrak t\in\mathfrak T_q}\mathrm{Adv}(\mathfrak t)$ and $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}=\mathrm{TV}(\Pi_{x_0},\Pi_{x_1})$.

Statement. (o) Reduction. Under the bottom oracle every adversary with $q_E$ Eval calls on the served name has advantage at most $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$, whatever its Train calls, also when it knows $\mathsf L$.

(a) Certificate from both sides. An experimenter draws $n$ models at each input with drawn seeds, opens them, and runs every tree once on each model with fresh evaluation randomness. Let $\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}(\mathfrak t)$ be the frequency of output one over the models at $x_1$ minus that over the models at $x_0$. For every learning function, with probability at least $1-\alpha$, $\max_{\mathfrak t\in\mathfrak T_q}|\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}(\mathfrak t)-\mathrm{Adv}(\mathfrak t)|\le\sqrt{2\ln(4|\mathfrak T_q|/\alpha)/n}$. The same bound holds between $\max_{\mathfrak t}\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}(\mathfrak t)$ and $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)$.

(b) to (d), stated. One bottom-oracle adversary with $2n$ shadow models comes within $2\sqrt{\ln(2|\mathfrak T_{q_E}|)/n}$ of $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$ for every learning function. With deterministic $\mathsf{ev}$ and atomless model laws, a drawn-seed procedure that rejects every false claim $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}\le\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(Q)+\lambda<1$ except with probability $\alpha$ accepts it with probability at most $\alpha$ everywhere. Coupled pairs certify $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}\le\beta$.

Label. Proven, (o) to (d). Open, a matching lower bound for (b) (open problem 3). Parts (b) to (d) are proven in round-01/builder.md, section Black-box certifiability, and revised in round-02/builder.md.

Without the oracle, a bound on what $q_E$ label queries reveal needs the law of the trained transformer's labels on $U$. With the oracle, part (a) certifies $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}$ from both sides for every learning function, at a cost fixed by $C$, $|U|$, the number of queries, $\alpha$ and the accuracy. Part (c) concerns deterministic evaluation and atomless model laws. There drawn seeds can refute, and never certify, a claim that label queries reveal all that the model reveals, when the claimed value is below one.

Degenerate oracles. Part (o) holds under the bottom oracle and fails under the top oracle. In the example of thm-coupling-certificate (c)(i), let $\mathsf{ev}(m,u)$ be the $u$-th binary digit of $m$. Both model laws are uniform, so $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)=0$ for every $q$. An adversary that opens the honest seed $\rho$ reads its first digit. It queries that digit of the served model once and wins with advantage one, since the shift by one half flips it.

Proof of (o). Represent the randomness of the adversary's own calls in advance, and call it $\omega$ together with the coins. It is independent of the hypothesis and of the served model. Given $\omega$ the adversary is deterministic, and its next query on the served name is a function of the earlier answers on that name. Each answered query lies in $U$, and a refused call returns a fixed symbol that can be removed. Padded to $q_E$ queries, the interaction with the served model is a tree $\mathfrak t_\omega\in\mathfrak T_{q_E}$, and the output is its leaf bit. The advantage is $\mathbb E_\omega\mathrm{Adv}(\mathfrak t_\omega)\le\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$. Nothing restricts $\omega$, so it may hold knowledge of $\mathsf L$. $\square$

Proof of (a). For a fixed tree the $n$ outcomes at $x_b$ are independent Bernoulli draws with mean $\Pr_{H_b}[\mathfrak t=1]$. Hoeffding's inequality bounds each frequency's deviation by $\lambda/2$ except with probability $2e^{-n\lambda^2/2}$, so $|\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}(\mathfrak t)-\mathrm{Adv}(\mathfrak t)|\le\lambda$ except with probability $4e^{-n\lambda^2/2}$. A union bound over $\mathfrak T_q$, with $4|\mathfrak T_q|e^{-n\lambda^2/2}=\alpha$, gives the bound, and $|\max\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}-\max\mathrm{Adv}|\le\max|\widehat{\mathrm{Adv}}-\mathrm{Adv}|$. $\square$

## Dataflow composition [thm-dataflow-composition]

Proof of (e)(ii). Step 1, the pair code. By lem-sphere-code (a) there are $2J_{\mathrm{cd}}$ points of the sphere of radius $B$ with pairwise distances at least $B$. Let $\mathrm{ix}(\rho)=\lfloor J_{\mathrm{cd}}\rho\rfloor+1$, and let $\mathrm{sg}(\rho)=+1$ when the fractional part of $J_{\mathrm{cd}}\rho$ is below one half and $-1$ otherwise. Then $(\mathrm{ix},\mathrm{sg})$ is uniform. Let $\mathsf L^\circ(x_0;\rho)=\mathsf L^\circ(x_1;\rho)=\mathbf p^{\mathrm{sg}(\rho)}_{\mathrm{ix}(\rho)}$. For signs $\varsigma\in\{\pm1\}^{J_{\mathrm{cd}}}$ let $\mathsf L_\varsigma(x_0;\rho)=\mathbf p^{\varsigma_i}_i$ and $\mathsf L_\varsigma(x_1;\rho)=\mathbf p^{-\varsigma_i}_i$, with $i=\mathrm{ix}(\rho)$. All of them agree at every other training input, have deterministic kernels and lie in $\mathfrak L_B$.

Step 2, drawn seeds see nearly the same thing. In the drawn-seed discipline every Train call has $s=\bot$ and the certifier opens no seed name, so each answer at $x_b$ is a fresh draw of $\Pi_{x_b}$. So the transcript is a fixed map of $N$ independent draws at $x_0$, $N$ at $x_1$, used in order, and $\omega'$. Here $\omega'$ holds the coins and an independent triple $(\rho',\xi',\upsilon')$ per call, which serves every other input and every Eval call. It has the same law under every member of the family. Under $\mathsf L^\circ$ the draws are $\mathbf p^{\mathrm{sg}}_{\mathrm{ix}}$ with independent uniform index and sign. Under the mixture over a uniform $\varsigma$ they are $\mathbf p^{\pm\varsigma_{\mathrm{ix}}}_{\mathrm{ix}}$ with independent uniform indices. Given $2N$ distinct indices, the signs are independent and uniform in both. So the two laws of the $2N$ draws differ in total variation by at most the chance that two of $2N$ indices coincide, which is at most $N(2N-1)/J_{\mathrm{cd}}$. By (F2) the same holds for every event of the transcript.

Step 3, every member leaks. Let $\mathbb 1_\varsigma(y)=1$ when the code point nearest to $y$ lies in $\{\mathbf p^{\varsigma_i}_i\}$, the support of the model at $x_0$ under $\mathsf L_\varsigma$. Under $H_0$ the view is a code point of that support plus $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ noise, and lem-sphere-code (b) with $\mathfrak s=B$ bounds the decoding error by $(2J_{\mathrm{cd}}-1)\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))$. Under $H_1$ the view is a point of the other half plus noise, and $\mathbb 1_\varsigma=1$ needs the same error. So $\theta(\mathsf L_\varsigma)\ge1-(1+\gamma)(2J_{\mathrm{cd}}-1)\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))=:\vartheta$ for every $\varsigma$.

Step 4, transfer. Soundness gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L_\varsigma}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha$ for every $\varsigma$, and step 2 gives the claim at $\mathsf L^\circ$. At $\mathsf L^\circ$ the model does not depend on the dataset, so $\theta=0$ and every coupled pair coincides. For the reading, (F6)(iii) gives $(2J_{\mathrm{cd}}-1)\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))\le J_{\mathrm{cd}}e^{-B^2/(8\sigma^2)}\le e^{-B^2/(16\sigma^2)}$. $\square$

## Bounded-deviation transfer [thm-bounded-deviation-transfer]

Setting. $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases, with or without state, under the bottom or the top oracle. For each $t\le R$ the coalition may own input nodes whose only child is $r_t$. Let $\mathbf u_t\in\mathbb R^d$ be the sum of their values in round $t$, or zero when it owns none. Assume $O_t=\mathbf u_t+\bar o_t$, where $\bar o_t$ is a measurable function of the parents of $r_t$ other than $v_t$ and these input nodes, and of randomness of $r_t$ independent of $Z_t$. The protocol prescribes the values of these input nodes, and $\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ is the sum of the prescribed values. It is a measurable function of the values of the nodes that the coalition owns or receives through the plain channel before $r_t$ runs, other than these input nodes.

Fix $B_A\ge0$. A strategy is *$B_A$-deviating* when three conditions hold. Every argument and value that it sets for a node other than these input nodes is the protocol's. In every round that runs, $\|\mathbf u_t-\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t\|\le B_A$. It stops, if at all, after a release. Its queries and other oracle calls are unrestricted. The *protocol's strategy* is the one with $\mathbf u_t=\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ that never stops, and $\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_b(\Delta)$ is its exceedance probability along $H_b$. Let $\theta^{\mathrm{dev}}$ and $\theta^{\mathrm{dev},\leftarrow}$ be the suprema of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ and of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_1\|P_0)$ over adversaries with a $B_A$-deviating strategy. Let $\mathfrak e=(R-1)B_A^2/(2\sigma^2)$, $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)=\exp(-(\sqrt{\ln(1/p)}-\sqrt{\mathfrak e})_+^2)$ for $p\in(0,1]$, $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(0)=0$ and $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)=1$ for $p>1$.

The instance $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ has $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^d$, an honest stateless client $j$, an honest server, and one coalition client $j'$ whose prescribed input is zero. The choice of round $t$ is $(t,m_t)$, with training input $x((t,m_t),D)=(\ell_t,m_t,D,\mathcal H)$ for distinct learner names $\ell_t$. The release is $y_t=\mathbf u_t+\mathrm{clip}_B(W_t-m_t)+Z_t$ with $B\in(0,\infty]$, and the server sets $m_{t+1}=m_t+y_t$ from a fixed $m_1$. Its target is $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R=\max\{\theta^{\mathrm{dev}},\theta^{\mathrm{dev},\leftarrow}\}$.

Statement. (a) Transfer. For every learning function, every $B_A$-deviating strategy, every $b\in\{0,1\}$, every $\Delta>0$ and every $\varpi>1$, $\epsilon_b(\Delta)\le\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_b(\Delta)^{1-1/\varpi}e^{(\varpi-1)\mathfrak e}$, hence $\epsilon_b(\Delta)\le\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_b(\Delta))$. So $\theta^{\mathrm{dev}}\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_0(\Delta))$ and $\theta^{\mathrm{dev},\leftarrow}\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_1(\Delta))$. At $B_A=0$, $\Upsilon_0(p)=p$ for $p\le1$, and the statement is the passive case of thm-dataflow-composition with stops.

(b) Certificate. Let $\bar\epsilon_b$ be the one-sided Clopper and Pearson upper bound at level $\alpha$ from $N'$ independent runs of $\mathsf{Ex}_b$ under the protocol's strategy, for $\Delta$ fixed in advance. Then $\hat t_b=\min\{1,\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\bar\epsilon_b)\}$ is sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function, $\hat t_0$ for $\theta^{\mathrm{dev}}$ and $\hat t_1$ for $\theta^{\mathrm{dev},\leftarrow}$. Each run costs $2R$ Train calls for client $j$ plus the calls of the other nodes, and the protocol's strategy adds none. If no run exceeds $\Delta$ and $N'\ge\ln(1/\alpha)\exp((\sqrt{\mathfrak e}+\sqrt{\ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})})^2)$ with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\in(0,1]$, then $\hat t_b\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$.

(c) Tightness of the transfer. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ let $R\ge2$, $B_A>0$ and $B\ge2\Delta$. For every $\iota>\sqrt{2\mathfrak e}+1$ some learning function and $B_A$-deviating strategy have $\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_0(\Delta)=\bar\Phi(\iota)$ and $\epsilon_0(\Delta)=\bar\Phi(\iota-\sqrt{2\mathfrak e})$, and $\ln(1/\epsilon_0(\Delta))\le\ln(1/\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_0(\Delta)))+\ln(\iota+1)+1$. Let a function $\Upsilon'$ satisfy $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\Upsilon'(\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_0(\Delta))$ in every instance of $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with these $R$, $B_A$, $\sigma$, $\Delta$ and $B$. Then for every $p\in(0,\bar\Phi(\sqrt{2\mathfrak e}+1))$, $\Upsilon'(p)\ge e^{-1}\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)/\sqrt{8\ln(1/p)}$, while $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)/p=\exp(2\sqrt{\mathfrak e\ln(1/p)}-\mathfrak e)$. So no such $\Upsilon'$ improves on $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}$ by more than a factor $e\sqrt{8\ln(1/p)}$, while $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)/p$ is unbounded as $p\to0$.

(d) Lower bound. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ let $B<\infty$ and $B_A>0$, and let $\mathsf L^\circ$ return the starting model at every training input. Then $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$, and every coupled pair at $\mathsf L^\circ$ coincides. For $\mathfrak c,\eta\in(0,1)$ let $\mathfrak q_\eta$ solve $\bar\Phi(\mathfrak q_\eta)=\eta/4$, and let $J_3=\min\{\lfloor e^{\mathfrak c^2d/4}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac\eta2\exp(\frac12((1-\mathfrak c)\sqrt{2\mathfrak e}-\mathfrak q_\eta)_+^2)\rfloor\}$. If $J_3\ge1$, every certifier of $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $N$ Train calls, in either discipline, sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function, satisfies $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge(1-\eta/2)\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;B/\sigma)]\ge1-\alpha-N/J_3$. If $\mathfrak e<d/4$, $\mathfrak c=2\sqrt{\mathfrak e/d}$, $(1-\mathfrak c)\sqrt{2\mathfrak e}\ge\mathfrak q_\eta$ and the second argument of the minimum is at least one, then $\ln J_3\ge\mathfrak e(1-4\sqrt{\mathfrak e/d})-\mathfrak q_\eta\sqrt{2\mathfrak e}+\ln(\eta/4)$.

(e) Drawn seeds. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ let $B<\infty$ and $1\le J_{\mathrm{cd}}\le\lceil2^{0.18d}\rceil/2$. Let a drawn-seed certifier of $\theta^{\mathrm{dev}}$ with $N$ Train calls be sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function. Some learning function with all coupled pairs equal and $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R=0$ gives $\Pr[\hat t\ge1-(1+\gamma)(2J_{\mathrm{cd}}-1)\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))]\ge1-\alpha-N(2N-1)/J_{\mathrm{cd}}$. There the certificate (b) outputs at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(\ln(1/\alpha)/N')$ with certainty. By the remark of def-certifier the bound binds certifiers of $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$.

Corollary, the price. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ let $B<\infty$, $B_A>0$, $\eta\in(0,1)$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\in(0,1]$ and $\alpha+\alpha'<1$, with $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}<(1-\eta/2)\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;B/\sigma)$. Call a certifier of $\theta^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ accurate at $\mathsf L^\circ$ when it outputs at most $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ there with probability at least $1-\alpha'$. Let $N^\star$ be the least number of Train calls of a certifier sound at level $\alpha$ over every learning function and accurate at $\mathsf L^\circ$. For $\mathfrak c$ with $J_3\ge1$, $(1-\alpha-\alpha')J_3\le N^\star\le4R\lceil\ln(2/\alpha)\exp((\sqrt{\mathfrak e}+\sqrt{\ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})})^2)\rceil$. Hence $\ln N^\star=(1+o(1))\,\mathfrak e$ as $\mathfrak e\to\infty$ with $\mathfrak e/d\to0$ and $\ln R=o(\mathfrak e)$, with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$, $\alpha$, $\alpha'$ and $\eta$ fixed. In units of $(R-1)B_A^2/\sigma^2$ the constant is one half.

Label. Proven, (a) to (e) and the corollary.

Reading. With $B_A=B=\sigma$, as for a deviating client in DP-FedAvg with noise multiplier one, $\mathfrak e=(R-1)/2$. Fix $\varepsilon$, $\eta$, $\alpha$, $\alpha'$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma$ so that the conditions of the corollary hold. That set is a ball of dimension $d$ around a Gaussian random walk. Proof of (a). Fix a learning function, a $B_A$-deviating strategy, an adversary that plays it, and $b=0$. The case $b=1$ exchanges the hypotheses.

Step 0, no stop. The strategy that plays as the given one until it would stop, and then plays $\mathbf u_t=\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ forever, is $B_A$-deviating and has an exceedance at least as large. So assume no stop.

Step 1, the execution as a function of $(\Omega,y_{<t})$. Work on the space of step 1 of the proof of thm-dataflow-composition (b), and let $\Omega=(\omega,\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_R)$, independent of $Z_1,\dots,Z_R$. Consider $\mathsf{Ex}_0$ under the given strategy and under the protocol's strategy. Three facts make the maps of step 1 of thm-dataflow-composition (b) the same under both, except for the coalition's input nodes and the adversary's state. The input nodes of round $t$ have the single child $r_t$, so they reach later nodes only through $y_t$. Every other argument and value that the coalition sets is the protocol's. The randomness of each node is one coordinate of $\omega$, whatever calls the adversary makes on its own. By induction over the order of $G$, the same measurable maps give $c_t$, $W_t$, $W'_t$, $\bar o_t$ and $\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ as functions of $(\Omega,y_{<t})$ under both strategies. Write $\bar y_t=\bar o_t+f_t(c_t,W_t)$. Under the given strategy $\mathbf u_t$ is a function of the adversary's state, hence of $(\Omega,y_{<t})$, with $\|\mathbf u_t-\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t\|\le B_A$. Under the protocol's strategy $\mathbf u_t=\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$. In both, $y_t=\mathbf u_t+\bar y_t+Z_t$. The exceedance reads $c_t$, $W_t$ and $W'_t$ for $t\le R$, and $c_R$ is set before $v_R$ runs. So $\{D^\star_0>\Delta\}=\{(\Omega,y_{<R})\in\mathbb A\}$ for one measurable set $\mathbb A$, the same under both strategies.

Step 2, the density. Let $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{dev}}$ and $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}$ be the laws of $(\Omega,y_1,\dots,y_{R-1})$ under the given and the protocol's strategy. $\Omega$ has the same law under both. Given $(\Omega,y_{<t})$, a function of $(\Omega,Z_{<t})$, the noise $Z_t$ is independent. So $y_t$ has law $\mathcal N(\mathbf u_t+\bar y_t,\sigma^2I_d)$ under $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{dev}}$ and $\mathcal N(\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t+\bar y_t,\sigma^2I_d)$ under $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}$. Both laws have positive densities with respect to the law of $\Omega$ times Lebesgue measure, and $d\mathbb P^{\mathrm{dev}}/d\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}=\prod_{t<R}\mathfrak r_t$ with $\mathfrak r_t=\varphi_\sigma(y_t-\bar y_t-\mathbf u_t)/\varphi_\sigma(y_t-\bar y_t-\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t)$.

Step 3, the moment. Write $\mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}$ for the expectation under $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}$, and let $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{rel}}_t$ be generated by $\Omega$ and $y_{<t}$. Under $\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}$, given $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{rel}}_t$, the vector $y_t-\bar y_t-\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ is $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ and $\mathbf u_t-\mathbf u^{\mathrm{pr}}_t$ is fixed. So (F7) gives $\mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}[\mathfrak r_t^{\varpi}\mid\mathcal F^{\mathrm{rel}}_t]\le\exp(\varpi(\varpi-1)B_A^2/(2\sigma^2))$. The earlier factors are $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{rel}}_t$-measurable, so the tower property over $t<R$ gives $\mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}(d\mathbb P^{\mathrm{dev}}/d\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}})^{\varpi}\le e^{\varpi(\varpi-1)\mathfrak e}$.

Step 4, the transfer. $\epsilon_0(\Delta)=\mathbb P^{\mathrm{dev}}(\mathbb A)$ and $\epsilon^{\mathrm{pr}}_0(\Delta)=\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbb A)$, and (F7) on $\mathbb A$ gives the first bound. For the closed form let $p=\mathbb P^{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathbb A)>0$ and $\mathfrak l=\ln(1/p)$. The logarithm of the bound is $-\mathfrak l(1-1/\varpi)+(\varpi-1)\mathfrak e$, with derivative $\mathfrak e-\mathfrak l/\varpi^2$ in $\varpi$. If $\mathfrak l\le\mathfrak e$ the infimum over $\varpi>1$ is $0$. If $\mathfrak l>\mathfrak e>0$ the minimum is at $\varpi=\sqrt{\mathfrak l/\mathfrak e}$, with value $-(\sqrt{\mathfrak l}-\sqrt{\mathfrak e})^2$. If $\mathfrak e=0$ the infimum is $-\mathfrak l$. So the infimum of the bound is $\Upsilon_{\mathfrak e}(p)$, and $p=0$ gives zero. The bounds on the targets are thm-dataflow-composition (b). $\square$

Proof of (b). The experimenter holds every input, runs the other nodes, plays the protocol's strategy, and in round $t$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^b_j,W_{t-1}),\bot)$ and then $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j,W'_{t-1}),s)$ with the returned seed name $s$. Draw $\mathbf q_1,\dots,\mathbf q_J$ independently and uniformly from $\{\pm d^{-1/2}\}^d$. The two bounds, the expansion in (d) and $\ln(4R)=o(\mathfrak e)$ give the asymptotics. $\square$

## Replay-audit steering [thm-replay-audit-steer]

Setting. A training input $x=(\ell,m^{\mathrm{st}},D,\mathcal H)$ is fixed, and a commitment $\mathrm{cm}$ that the auditor can open binds the prover's dataset $D$. Steering through the choice of $D$ lies outside the theorem. $(\mathcal M,\mathrm{dist})$ is a metric space whose closed balls are compact, with its Borel sets. $T\subseteq\mathcal M$ is a nonempty closed target set. A random oracle $\mathsf{RO}$ maps strings to $\mathcal R$ with independent values of law $\mu$. The audit game with tolerance $\tau\ge0$ and $n_{\mathrm{rep}}\ge1$ replays has three steps.

1. The prover, with access to $\mathsf{RO}$ and $\mathcal O$, outputs a model $m$ and a nonce $\mathrm{nc}$.
2. The auditor sets $\rho=\mathsf{RO}(\mathrm{cm},\mathrm{nc})$, makes $n_{\mathrm{rep}}$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x,\rho)$ with answers $m^{\mathrm{rep}}_1,\dots,m^{\mathrm{rep}}_{n_{\mathrm{rep}}}$, and accepts when $\min_i\mathrm{dist}(m,m^{\mathrm{rep}}_i)\le\tau$.
3. The prover wins when $m\in T$ and the auditor accepts.

This is the goal steer. The prover is unbounded, may know $\mathsf L$, and draws fresh hardware randomness in its Train calls. For $\rho\sim\mu$ and independent $\xi,\xi'\sim\pi$ let $M=\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ and $M'=\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi')$. The *reach*, the *spread* and the *joint reach* are $p_\tau(T)=\Pr[\mathrm{dist}(M,T)\le\tau]$, $\mathrm{sp}_\tau=\Pr[\mathrm{dist}(M,M')>\tau]$ and $p^\circ_\tau(T)=\Pr[\mathrm{dist}(M,T)\le\tau,\ \mathrm{dist}(M,M')\le\tau]$. Drawn-seed calls measure the reach, and coupled calls with one seed name measure the spread and the joint reach.

Statement. (a) Soundness. Every prover that makes $q_{\mathsf{RO}}$ queries to $\mathsf{RO}$ and any number of Train calls, and that may know $\mathsf L$, wins with probability at most $(q_{\mathsf{RO}}+1)\,n_{\mathrm{rep}}\,p_\tau(T)$.

(b) Completeness. The honest prover, which outputs $\mathsf{Train}(x,\mathsf{RO}(\mathrm{cm},\mathrm{nc}))$ for a nonce $\mathrm{nc}$, is accepted with probability at least $1-\mathrm{sp}_\tau$.

(c) Attack. For $n_{\mathrm{nc}}\ge1$ the grinding prover tries $n_{\mathrm{nc}}$ new nonces. It trains at each seed $\rho_k=\mathsf{RO}(\mathrm{cm},\mathrm{nc}_k)$ and outputs a nearest point of $T$ to the first run $M_k$ within $\tau/2$ of $T$, with $\mathrm{nc}_k$. With $p=p_{\tau/2}(T)>0$ it wins with probability at least $\frac{p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)}{p}(1-(1-p)^{n_{\mathrm{nc}}})\ge(1-\mathrm{sp}_{\tau/2}/p)(1-(1-p)^{n_{\mathrm{nc}}})$. Under deterministic kernels (a) holds without the factor $n_{\mathrm{rep}}$, and stopping at $\tau$ matches it within a factor $3.2$ when $n_{\mathrm{nc}}=q_{\mathsf{RO}}$ and $q_{\mathsf{RO}}\,p_\tau(T)\le1$.

(d) Drawn-seed auditors. On the circle $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ the learning functions $\rho\mapsto\rho+a\bmod1$ make the replay auditor with $\tau=0$ complete and sound for point targets. A drawn-seed auditor complete up to $\lambda'$ against all of them accepts some fixed point with probability at least $1-\lambda'-\lambda$, for every $\lambda>0$.

(e) Oblivious provers. If the learning function reads its seed at $x$ through a random table unknown to the prover, $q_T$ Train calls at $x$ win with expected probability at most $(q_T+1)n_{\mathrm{rep}}p_\tau(T)$. Train calls at other inputs and queries to $\mathsf{RO}$ are unrestricted.

Label. Proven, (a) to (e). Open, the gap from $\tau$ to $\tau/2$ under hardware randomness (open problem 7).

Without the oracle, an auditor cannot replay at a chosen seed. By (d), a drawn-seed auditor complete up to $\lambda'$ for every rotation of the circle accepts some fixed point with probability at least $1-\lambda'-\lambda$, for every $\lambda>0$. Parts (a) to (c) bound the steering probability from both sides by reach, spread and joint reach. Nobody can write these down for a transformer, and Train calls measure them for any learning function.

Degenerate oracles. The prover holds $\mathsf{RO}(\mathrm{cm},\mathrm{nc})$ and passes it to Train under the bottom oracle, so the top oracle adds nothing. So the test runs through the auditor, an honest party whose Train calls the theorem designs. In the coupled discipline the auditor has the soundness of (a) and the completeness of (b). Under drawn seeds the statement fails for the rotations of (d). Without the random oracle (a) fails, since a prover that picks its seed picks one whose run lands in $T$ (proven, round-02/builder.md).

Proof of (a). Implement $\mathsf{RO}$ by lazy sampling. Let $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{q_{\mathsf{RO}}+1}$ be independent with law $\mu$. The $k$-th distinct point the prover queries receives $\rho_k$, and a point the auditor evaluates without a prior query receives the first unused one. This gives $\mathsf{RO}$ its law. Let $k^*$ be the index of the value the auditor uses, and let independent $\xi_{k,i}\sim\pi$, for $k\le q_{\mathsf{RO}}+1$ and $i\le n_{\mathrm{rep}}$, serve the auditor's $i$-th replay when $k^*=k$. The prover's transcript does not involve them, so the replays have the prescribed law. A win needs $m\in T$ and $\mathrm{dist}(m,m^{\mathrm{rep}}_i)\le\tau$ for some $i$, so $\mathrm{dist}(m^{\mathrm{rep}}_i,T)\le\tau$. So the win lies in the union of the events $\{\mathrm{dist}(\mathsf L(x;\rho_k,\xi_{k,i}),T)\le\tau\}$, each of probability $p_\tau(T)$ for any prover. A union bound finishes. $\square$

Proof of (b). The seed $\mathsf{RO}(\mathrm{cm},\mathrm{nc})$ has law $\mu$. The honest model and the first replay are two runs at that seed with independent hardware randomness. The auditor rejects only if they lie more than $\tau$ apart, which has probability $\mathrm{sp}_\tau$. $\square$

Proofs of (c) to (e). They are in round-02/builder.md, section Replay-audit steering, with the match under deterministic kernels in round-03/builder.md, section Replay-audit steering, corrections, and the notation of (e) in round-04/builder.md. The attack uses independent seeds from new nonces and the triangle inequality between its output, its run and the auditor's replay. $\square$

## Further accepted results

Each result below is accepted, and its pointer names the round files that hold its proof.

Perfect substitution [thm-perfect-substitution]. Against an adversary that samples $\mathsf L$ once per Train call, the real procedure gives the bottom oracle's joint law of honest values and transcript. So bottom-oracle bounds hold for the real procedure (proven, round-01/builder.md with round-02/builder.md).

Reference access [thm-reference-access]. On one node seen through a deterministic channel, the top oracle's best advantage is the coupled divergence under deterministic kernels. Under the bottom oracle no attack reaches the total variation at a rate uniform over learning functions when the views can be atomless (proven, round-01/builder.md and round-02/builder.md).

Atomless two-point lemma [lem-atomless-two-point]. A drawn-seed certifier of $\mathrm{TV}$ through a deterministic channel, sound for all learning functions, outputs one with probability at least $1-\alpha$ on atomless views (proven). Its proof is in round-01/builder.md, corrected in round-02 and round-03.

Model banks [def-model-bank]. A design lists training inputs with seeds, each fixed or drawn, and a bank stores one run per entry. Its empirical oracle answers a Train call with the first unused matching entry and refuses a call that no unused entry matches. It agrees with a learning oracle up to its first refusal [lem-bank-fidelity] (proven, round-01/builder.md). A bank on random halves of a pool estimates the random-subset game for all records at once, for a finite set of tests fixed before the bank is built [thm-bank-soundness]. That game can differ by one from the fixed-dataset game. The estimate (a), the gap (b) and the first half of (d) are proven. That half bounds from below the Train calls that certify every record of a fixed dataset through a Gaussian release. Part (c), on shadow models in the random-subset game, is heuristic (round-01/builder.md with round-02 to round-05).

Active-choice dichotomy [thm-active-choice-dichotomy]. Let the coalition pick choices from admissible sets. Let a certifier be sound for every learning function and accurate where the leakage is zero, to a fixed level in $(0,1)$, at error probabilities summing below one. In some procedure it then needs Train calls linear in the number of distinct choices, or of choice sequences with state. Coupled certificates achieve this up to a logarithmic factor. No number suffices for an infinite set (proven, round-03/builder.md and round-04/builder.md).

Record-level certificate [thm-record-level-certificate]. One shared base certifies the fraction of a fixed dataset's records whose leakage to a passive coalition exceeds a threshold. Its cost is free of the dataset size and $d$ (proven, round-03/builder.md with round-04/builder.md).

Smoothed-choice certificate [thm-smoothed-choice-certificate]. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $B<\infty$ a covering of smoothed tails certifies a coalition client with inputs of norm at most $B_A$ at a cost exponential in $d$ (proven, round-04/builder.md with round-05/builder.md). It costs less than thm-bounded-deviation-transfer (b) only when $\mathfrak e$ exceeds about $d\ln(R^{3/2}\sqrt d/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})$ (heuristic comparison of the two costs).

## Open problems

A certifier is uniformly accurate when its output lies in $[\theta,\theta+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}]$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$ at every learning function, at a fixed number of Train calls. `theory/CURRENT.md` states each problem in full.

1. Sum channel. Let $n_h\ge1$ further honest clients, isolated and stateless, add their clipped updates to the releases of $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $B_A=0$. Is there a functional of $\mathsf L$ with three properties? It bounds every passive adversary as $\epsilon_0$ does in thm-dataflow-composition (b). It is never above $\epsilon_0$ and lies below it at some learning function. Some coupled-discipline certifier is uniformly accurate for it with Train calls fixed by $\alpha$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$, $R$ and $n_h$.
2. Drawn-seed price above $d$. When $B^2/\sigma^2>d$, is there a drawn-seed certifier of the divergence of thm-dataflow-composition (e), uniformly accurate over $\mathfrak L_B$, with $e^{O(d)}\,\mathrm{poly}(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}},\ln(1/\alpha))$ calls?
3. Shadow lower bound. Does some family of learning functions force every shadow-model attack within $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ of $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$ to use order $C^{q_E}\ln|U|/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}^2$ Train calls?
4. Multi-round drawn seeds. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $B_A=0$ and $B<\infty$, is there a uniformly accurate drawn-seed certifier of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ whose Train calls depend on $R$, $B/\sigma$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $\alpha$ and not on $d$?
5. The price at a fixed ratio. Fix $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak e/d$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$, $\alpha$, $\alpha'$ and $\eta$, and let $\mathfrak e\to\infty$ with $\ln R=o(\mathfrak e)$. Let $J^{\mathrm{fb}}$ be the largest number of disjoint Borel sets such that, at $\mathsf L^\circ$, some $B_A$-deviating strategy without oracle calls puts $m_R$ in each with probability at least $1-\eta/2$. Do $\ln N^\star/\mathfrak e$ and $\ln J^{\mathrm{fb}}/\mathfrak e$ have the same limit? Known (proven), under the hypotheses of the corollary, $N^\star\ge(1-\alpha-\alpha')J^{\mathrm{fb}}$ by the proof of thm-bounded-deviation-transfer (d), and $(1+2\sqrt{\mathfrak a})^{-2}\le\liminf\ln N^\star/\mathfrak e\le\limsup\ln N^\star/\mathfrak e\le1$. By Shannon's coding theorem the second limit is the capacity per unit of $\mathfrak e$ of the channel from the coalition's inputs to $m_R$ with the releases fed back (heuristic). It exceeds the value $\ln(1+2\mathfrak a)/(2\mathfrak a)$ without feedback at every ratio computed (heuristic).
6. Threshold CKKS. Does threshold CKKS with smudging realize the ideal releases under RLWE with negligible error, against semi-honest and, with six additions, deviating coalitions?
7. The gap from $\tau$ to $\tau/2$. Under hardware randomness, does some prover always win with probability at least a universal constant times $\min\{1,q_{\mathsf{RO}}p_\tau(T)\}$?
8. Seed maps. Under deterministic kernels, with a deterministic channel and atomless views, does some seed map make the coupling of thm-coupling-certificate (c)(ii) exact for $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ in the limit?
9. Finite seeds. Does each impossibility proven here with an atomless seed hold on an $n_{\mathrm{bit}}$-bit seed space, with an additive term that vanishes as $n_{\mathrm{bit}}$ grows at fixed $N$?

## Prior work

Program logics build couplings for known code (Barthe et al., LICS 2016), and the coupling certificate samples them for an opaque learning function. The drawn-seed lower bounds are the atomless form of the impossibility of testing differential privacy from samples (Gilbert and McMillan, Allerton 2018, Theorems 10 and 12). The composition rests on Gaussian differential privacy (Dong, Roth and Su, from memory), and the transfer on the Rényi divergence of the Gaussian mechanism (Mironov, CSF 2017). The replay audit applies Fiat and Shamir grinding to proof of learning (Jia et al., IEEE S&P 2021), whose spoofs motivate the random oracle (Zhang et al., IEEE S&P 2022). Novelty is open until the auditing literature (Jagielski et al., NeurIPS 2020, Steinke et al., NeurIPS 2023) is read.
