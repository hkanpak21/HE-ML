## Proofs

Standing facts. Round 01 lists the test fact, data processing, the realization fact and Lyapunov's theorem. Six more facts are used below.

Gaussian facts. (G1) $\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(\mathcal N(a,\sigma^2I_d)\|\mathcal N(b,\sigma^2I_d))=\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\|a-b\|/\sigma)$, and $s\mapsto\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;s)$ is nondecreasing, both proven in thm-coupling-certificate (b). Adding an independent component with the same law under both hypotheses leaves $\mathrm H_\gamma$ unchanged, by data processing in both directions. (G2) For $0\le s\le\mu$ with $\mu>0$, the kernel $g\mapsto(s/\mu)(g-\mu)+\sqrt{1-s^2/\mu^2}\,Z'$ with $Z'\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ maps $\mathcal N(0,1)$ to $\mathcal N(-s,1)$ and $\mathcal N(\mu,1)$ to $\mathcal N(0,1)$, since a sum of independent Gaussian variables is Gaussian. (G3) $\bar\Phi(x)\le\frac12e^{-x^2/2}$ for $x\ge0$. Substituting $u=x+v$ in the integral and using $u^2\ge x^2+v^2$ gives $\bar\Phi(x)\le e^{-x^2/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-v^2/2}\,dv/\sqrt{2\pi}$. (G4) For $Y\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ and $a\in\mathbb R^d$, $\mathbb E\,e^{\langle Y,a\rangle}=e^{\sigma^2\|a\|^2/2}$.

The split. For laws $P,M,Q$ and $\gamma\ge1$, $\mathrm H_\gamma(P\|Q)\le\mathrm{TV}(P,M)+\mathrm H_\gamma(M\|Q)$, since $P(B)-\gamma Q(B)=(P(B)-M(B))+(M(B)-\gamma Q(B))$ for every $B$.

McDiarmid's inequality. If $F$ changes by at most $c_k$ when its $k$-th independent argument changes, then $\Pr[F-\mathbb EF\ge t]\le\exp(-2t^2/\sum_kc_k^2)$ (McDiarmid, 1989).

The DKW inequality. For the empirical distribution function $\hat F_N$ of $N$ independent real variables with distribution function $F$, $\Pr[\sup_x|\hat F_N(x)-F(x)|>\kappa]\le2e^{-2N\kappa^2}$ for every $\kappa>0$ (Massart, Annals of Probability 1990, from memory).

Lazy sampling. Let $(U_i)_{i\in I}$ be independent with a common law, and let an interaction choose indices one at a time, each a function of the values revealed so far and of randomness independent of the table. Then the value at an index chosen for the first time is independent of everything revealed before, and it has the common law. For a fixed index $i$, on the event that $i$ is new at that step, everything revealed before is a function of variables independent of $U_i$. Summing over $i$ gives the claim. This is the standard argument for random oracles.

### Dataflow composition [thm-dataflow-composition]

Setting. Fix a client $j$ with candidate datasets $D^0_j\neq D^1_j$, and a standard Borel space $\mathcal C$ of choices. For $c\in\mathcal C$ and a dataset $D$, $x(c,D)$ is a training input, measurable in $c$. A choice holds the starting model and everything else that the node's input takes from outside the client, such as a learner name, a hypothesis class, or records that the coalition adds. Fix $R\ge1$, $d\ge1$, $\sigma>0$, and measurable release maps $f_t:\mathcal C\times\mathcal M\to\mathbb R^d$ for $t\le R$, for instance $f_t(c,m)=w_j(m-c)$ when $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^d$ and $c$ is the starting model. The parameter vector of a transformer is such an $m$.

A procedure $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases when its nodes include, for $t=1,\dots,R$, a learning node $v_t$ owned by $j$ and a general node $r_t$, with $v_1\prec r_1\prec v_2\prec r_2\prec\cdots\prec v_R\prec r_R$ in the order of $G$, such that the following hold.

1. $v_t$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D_j),\bot)$, where $c_t$ is the value of a parent of $v_t$ or a choice of the coalition.
2. No node other than $v_1,\dots,v_R$ reads $D_j$, and the only child of $v_t$ is $r_t$.
3. $r_t$ outputs $y_t=A_t+f_t(c_t,W_t)+Z_t$. Here $W_t$ is the value of $v_t$, $Z_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is randomness of $r_t$, and $A_t\in\mathbb R^d$ is a measurable function of the other parents of $r_t$ and of randomness of $r_t$ independent of $Z_t$.
4. The coalition $A$ owns no $v_t$ and no $r_t$, and the value of $v_t$ reaches $A$ through channel none.

Local steps of client $j$ between two releases form one learning function, so condition 2 loses no generality. The hypothesis $H_b$ sets $D_j=D^b_j$ and fixes every other input of $G$, the same under both. $P_b$ is the law of the adversary's transcript under $H_b$.

The coupled tail of round $t$ at choice $c$ is

$$\beta_t(c,\Delta)=\Pr\Big[\big\|f_t\big(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^1_j);\rho,\xi_1)\big)-f_t\big(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^0_j);\rho,\xi_0)\big)\big\|>\Delta\Big],\qquad\rho\sim\mu,\ \xi_0,\xi_1\sim\pi,$$

all independent. It is the tail of thm-coupling-certificate (b) at the pair $x(c,D^0_j),x(c,D^1_j)$.

The coupled execution $\mathsf{Ex}_b$ runs $G$ under $H_b$ against a given strategy. At each $v_t$ it also computes the twin $W'_t=\mathsf L(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j);\rho_t,\xi'_t)$, with the seed $\rho_t$ of $v_t$ and fresh $\xi'_t$, and no node reads the twin. Let

$$D^\star_b=\max_{t\le R}\big\|f_t(c_t,W'_t)-f_t(c_t,W_t)\big\|,\qquad\epsilon_b(\Delta)=\Pr[D^\star_b>\Delta].$$

$\epsilon_b(\Delta)$ is the exceedance probability along $H_b$. An experimenter that holds all inputs and runs the strategy samples $\mathsf{Ex}_b$. At each $v_t$ it calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^b_j),\bot)$, which returns a seed name $h$, and then $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j),h)$. For a passive strategy, $D^\star_b$ is a function of procedure values and of client $j$'s seeds and hardware randomness. So its law, and $\epsilon_b$, are the same for every passive adversary.

The ciphertext procedure of part (a). In $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$ each release is computed by a sub-protocol $\Sigma$ among the parties, in place of $r_t$. In round $t$ every party $i$ holds an input vector $u_{t,i}$, with $u_{t,j}=f_t(c_t,W_t)$, and $\Sigma$ outputs $y_t$ and delivers to the coalition a transcript $\mathrm{tr}_t$ of ciphertexts and decryption shares. The release procedure $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$ replaces $\Sigma$ by a general node $\mathcal F$ that computes $y_t=\sum_iu_{t,i}+e_t+Z_t$, with $e_t$ from a fixed law independent of $Z_t$, and releases $y_t$ through the plain channel. Ciphertext channels become none. If client $j$'s values are read only by $\Sigma$, then $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases with $A_t=\sum_{i\neq j}u_{t,i}+e_t$. Fix a simulator $\mathcal S_\Sigma$ that receives $y_t$ and the inputs and keys of the coalition and outputs a transcript. $\Sigma$ realizes $\mathcal F$ against $A$ with error $\epsilon_\Sigma(t)$ when every environment $\mathcal Z$ that runs in time $t$, chooses the honest inputs of every round adaptively, plays the parties of $A$ in $\Sigma$ and outputs a bit has

$$\big|\Pr[\mathcal Z\to1\mid\Sigma]-\Pr[\mathcal Z\to1\mid\mathcal F,\mathcal S_\Sigma]\big|\le\epsilon_\Sigma(t).$$

Statement.

(a) Channel reduction. Let $T_{\mathsf L}$ bound the time of one run of $\mathsf L$ or of $\mathsf{ev}$, let $n_G$ bound the number of honest runs of $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$ in $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$, and let $t_G$ bound the time of its other honest nodes. Let $\mathcal A$ be an adversary in $G^{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{enc}}$, under the realizable or the seed-revealing policy, that runs in time $t_{\mathcal A}$ and makes $q$ oracle calls. Let $\mathcal A'$ be the adversary in $G^{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{rel}}$, under the same policy, that runs $\mathcal A$ and produces each $\mathrm{tr}_t$ with $\mathcal S_\Sigma$. Then for $b\in\{0,1\}$

$$\big|\Pr_{G_{\mathrm{enc}},H_b}[\mathcal A\to1]-\Pr_{G_{\mathrm{rel}},H_b}[\mathcal A'\to1]\big|\le\epsilon_\Sigma(t'),\qquad t'=t_{\mathcal A}+(q+n_G)\,T_{\mathsf L}+t_G .$$

For every $\varepsilon\ge0$, with $P^{\mathrm{rel}}_b$ the view law of $\mathcal A'$ in $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$ under $H_b$,

$$\Pr_{H_0}[\mathcal A\to1]-e^\varepsilon\Pr_{H_1}[\mathcal A\to1]\le\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}\big(P^{\mathrm{rel}}_0\|P^{\mathrm{rel}}_1\big)+(1+e^\varepsilon)\,\epsilon_\Sigma(t').$$

(b) Composition. For every adversary under the realizable or the seed-revealing policy, passive or active, every $\varepsilon\ge0$ and every $\Delta>0$,

$$\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P_0\|P_1)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}\big(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\Delta/\sigma\big)+\epsilon_0(\Delta),\qquad\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P_1\|P_0)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}\big(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\Delta/\sigma\big)+\epsilon_1(\Delta),$$

where $\epsilon_b$ is computed for the adversary's strategy, and

$$\epsilon_b(\Delta)\le\sum_{t=1}^R\mathbb E_{H_b}\big[\beta_t(c_t,\Delta)\big],$$

with the expectation along the execution under $H_b$. The bound holds for an adversary that knows $\mathsf L$. It is attained. By induction over the order, the value of every node other than $v_1,\dots,v_R$, and the adversary's state at every time, are measurable functions of $\omega$, of the values $y_{t'}$ of the release nodes already run, and of the $\Lambda_{t'}$ of the learning nodes already run. The induction step holds because $D_j$ and $W_t$ enter no node other than $v_t$ and $r_t$, by condition 2. The maps do not involve $D_j$, so they do not depend on $b$. Since $r_{t-1}\prec v_t\prec r_t$, the choice $c_t$, which exists before $v_t$ runs, is a function of $(\omega,y_{<t},\Lambda_{<t})$, and $A_t$ is a function of $(\omega,y_{<t},\Lambda_{\le t})$.

So under $H_b$ the releases solve the recursion

$$y_t=\alpha_t(\omega,y_{<t},\Lambda_{\le t})+f_t\big(c_t,\mathsf L(x(c_t,D^b_j);\rho_t,\xi^b_t)\big)+Z_t,\qquad c_t=\chi_t(\omega,y_{<t},\Lambda_{<t}).$$

Write $S^b=(\omega,y_{\le R},\Lambda_{\le R})$ for the solution under $H_b$ and $L_b$ for its law. Then $P_b$ is the image of $L_b$ under $\Psi$, and by data processing it suffices to prove both bounds for $L_0$ and $L_1$.

Step 2, the hybrid. On the same probability space define $\tilde y$ recursively by

$$\tilde c_t=\chi_t(\omega,\tilde y_{<t},\Lambda_{<t}),\qquad F^b_t=f_t\big(\tilde c_t,\mathsf L(x(\tilde c_t,D^b_j);\rho_t,\xi^b_t)\big),\qquad\tilde d_t=F^1_t-F^0_t,$$

$$\tilde y_t=\alpha_t(\omega,\tilde y_{<t},\Lambda_{\le t})+F^1_t-\mathrm{clip}_\Delta(\tilde d_t)+Z_t .$$

Let $L_M$ be the law of $(\omega,\tilde y_{\le R},\Lambda_{\le R})$.

Step 3, $\mathrm{TV}(L_0,L_M)\le\epsilon_0(\Delta)$. Let $y^0$ be the solution under $H_0$ on this space. It uses $\xi^0_t$, and $\xi^1_t$ serves as the hardware randomness of the twin. So the space carries $\mathsf{Ex}_0$, and $D^\star_0=\max_t\|d_t\|$ with $d_t$ computed along $y^0$. Let $t^\star$ be the first $t$ with $\|\tilde d_t\|>\Delta$, and $t^\star=\infty$ if there is none. For $t<t^\star$ we have $\mathrm{clip}_\Delta(\tilde d_t)=\tilde d_t$, so $\tilde y_t$ satisfies the recursion of $y^0_t$, and by induction $\tilde y_{\le t}=y^0_{\le t}$. In particular $\tilde d_{t^\star}=d_{t^\star}$ when $t^\star<\infty$, since both are computed from the same past and the same $\zeta_{t^\star}$. The same induction from the side of $y^0$ shows that the first exceedance along $y^0$ is also $t^\star$. Hence $\{t^\star<\infty\}=\{D^\star_0>\Delta\}$, and off this event $(\omega,\tilde y,\Lambda)=(\omega,y^0,\Lambda)$. The coupling inequality gives $\mathrm{TV}(L_0,L_M)\le\Pr[D^\star_0>\Delta]=\epsilon_0(\Delta)$.

For the sum, let $\mathcal F_t$ be generated by $\omega$, $\zeta_{<t}$ and $Z_{<t}$. The choice $c_t$ along $y^0$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable, and $\zeta_t$ is independent of $\mathcal F_t$. By Fubini's theorem $\Pr[\|d_t\|>\Delta\mid\mathcal F_t]=\beta_t(c_t,\Delta)$, which is measurable in $c_t$ because the integrand is jointly measurable. A union bound over $t$ gives $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\sum_t\mathbb E_{H_0}[\beta_t(c_t,\Delta)]$.

Step 4, $\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(L_M\|L_1)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\mu)$ with $\mu=\Delta/\sigma$. We build one kernel $\mathcal K$ from $\mathbb R^R$ to the space of $(\omega,y_{\le R},\Lambda_{\le R})$ with $\mathcal K\,\mathcal N(0,I_R)=L_M$ and $\mathcal K\,\mathcal N(\mu\mathbf 1,I_R)=L_1$, where $\mathbf 1$ is the all-ones vector. Given $g=(g_1,\dots,g_R)$, the kernel draws $\omega$, and then for $t=1,\dots,R$ it draws $\zeta_t$, sets $\Lambda_t$, and computes $c_t$, $A_t$, $F^0_t$, $F^1_t$ and $d_t=F^1_t-F^0_t$ from its own past outputs, as in step 2. Let $c'_t=\mathrm{clip}_\Delta(d_t)$ and $s_t=\|c'_t\|/\sigma\le\mu$. Let $e_t=c'_t/\|c'_t\|$ when $c'_t\neq0$, and a fixed unit vector otherwise. With fresh $Z'_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ and $Z''_t\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ the kernel outputs

$$U_t=\frac{s_t}{\mu}(g_t-\mu)+\sqrt{1-s_t^2/\mu^2}\,Z'_t,\qquad y_t=A_t+F^1_t+\sigma U_te_t+\sigma(I_d-e_te_t^{\top})Z''_t .$$

Given the past and $\zeta_t$, the coordinate $g_t$ is independent of both. If $g_t\sim\mathcal N(\mu,1)$, then $U_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ by (G2), so $U_te_t+(I_d-e_te_t^\top)Z''_t\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and $y_t\sim\mathcal N(A_t+F^1_t,\sigma^2I_d)$, which is the conditional law of the release under $H_1$. If $g_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, then $U_t\sim\mathcal N(-s_t,1)$ and $y_t\sim\mathcal N(A_t+F^1_t-\sigma s_te_t,\sigma^2I_d)=\mathcal N(A_t+F^1_t-c'_t,\sigma^2I_d)$, which is the conditional law of the hybrid release. In both cases $\zeta_t$ has its law given the past. By the chain rule for kernels the outputs have law $L_1$ and $L_M$ respectively. Data processing, and (G1) after a rotation that maps $\mu\mathbf 1$ to $\sqrt R\,\mu$ times a unit vector, give

$$\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(L_M\|L_1)\le\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}\big(\mathcal N(0,I_R)\|\mathcal N(\mu\mathbf 1,I_R)\big)=\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\,\mu).$$

Step 5, both bounds. The split with $M=L_M$ gives $\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(L_0\|L_1)\le\epsilon_0(\Delta)+\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)$. For the second bound exchange the roles of 0 and 1. The hybrid then releases $A_t+F^0_t+\mathrm{clip}_\Delta(F^1_t-F^0_t)+Z_t$, which coincides with the execution under $H_1$ until the first exceedance along $H_1$, and step 4 applies with $c'_t=-\mathrm{clip}_\Delta(F^1_t-F^0_t)$ and $A_t+F^0_t$ in place of $A_t+F^1_t$. The bound holds for each fixed $\mathsf L$ and every adversary, so it holds for an adversary that knows $\mathsf L$, by def-degenerate-oracles (ii). $\square$

Proof of (b), tightness. Take one honest client $j$, the server in $A$, $A_t=0$, $\mathcal M=\mathcal C=\mathbb R^d$, $f_t(c,m)=m-c$, and the protocol $c_1=0$ and $c_{t+1}=c_t+y_t$. Let $\Xi$ be a point, let $g:\mathcal R\to\mathbb R^d$ be measurable with $g(\rho)$ of law $\mathcal N(0,s^2I_d)$ for some $s>0$, and let $\mathsf L(x(c,D);\rho)=c+g(\rho)+\Delta\,\mathbf 1[D=D^1_j]\,e$ for a unit vector $e$. Then $\|f_t(c_t,W'_t)-f_t(c_t,W_t)\|=\Delta$ in every round, so $D^\star_b=\Delta$ and $\epsilon_b(\Delta)=0$. The release is $y_t=g(\rho_t)+\Delta\,b\,e+Z_t$ under $H_b$. Consider the passive adversary that makes no call other than Open on the honest seed names. Under the seed-revealing policy it opens each $\rho_t$, and its transcript is a bijective image of $(\rho_t,z_t)_{t\le R}$ with $z_t=y_t-g(\rho_t)=\Delta\,b\,e+Z_t$. The seeds are independent of the $z_t$ and have the same law under both hypotheses, and the $z_t$ are independent draws of $\mathcal N(\Delta be,\sigma^2I_d)$. By (G1) the transcript has hockey-stick divergence $\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(\mathcal N(0,I_{dR})\|\mathcal N(\tfrac\Delta\sigma(e,\dots,e),I_{dR}))=\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)$, which equals the bound of (b). Under the realizable policy the transcript is a bijective image of $(y_t)_{t\le R}$, independent draws of $\mathcal N(\Delta be,(\sigma^2+s^2)I_d)$, and its divergence is $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sqrt{\sigma^2+s^2})$. The bound of (b) is therefore exact under the top oracle in this instance, and loose under the bottom oracle by the noise $g(\rho_t)$ that the shared seed cancels. $\square$

Proof of (a). Fix $b$. Let $\mathcal Z_b$ be the environment that runs $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$ under $H_b$ around $\Sigma$. It computes every honest node outside $\Sigma$, running $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$ with fresh randomness at honest learning and evaluation nodes. It runs $\mathcal A$ and answers its oracle calls with the simulator of step 2 of thm-perfect-substitution, which keeps the names of $\mathcal A$ and computes $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$. Under the seed-revealing policy it also gives $\mathcal A$ the name of each honest seed when that node runs, which it can do because it drew the seed. It feeds $\Sigma$ the honest inputs $u_{t,i}$, relays the messages of $\mathcal A$ for the parties of $A$ in $\Sigma$, and outputs the bit of $\mathcal A$. It makes at most $q+n_G$ runs of $\mathsf L$ or $\mathsf{ev}$, so it runs in time at most $t'$.

With the real $\Sigma$, the joint law of the honest values and the transcript of $\mathcal A$ is the one of $G^{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{enc}}$ under $H_b$. Step 3 of thm-perfect-substitution proves this event by event. The same step applies under the seed-revealing policy, because a Train call with an honest seed name returns $\mathsf L$ at that seed with fresh $\xi$ in both executions. So $\Pr[\mathcal Z_b\to1\mid\Sigma]=\Pr_{G_{\mathrm{enc}},H_b}[\mathcal A\to1]$. With $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal S_\Sigma$ in place of $\Sigma$, $\mathcal Z_b$ is the execution of $\mathcal A'$ in $G^{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ under $H_b$, with the oracle implemented in the same way, so $\Pr[\mathcal Z_b\to1\mid\mathcal F,\mathcal S_\Sigma]=\Pr_{G_{\mathrm{rel}},H_b}[\mathcal A'\to1]$. The realization error gives the first inequality. For the second,

$$\Pr_{H_0}[\mathcal A\to1]-e^\varepsilon\Pr_{H_1}[\mathcal A\to1]\le\big(\Pr_{H_0}[\mathcal A'\to1]+\epsilon_\Sigma(t')\big)-e^\varepsilon\big(\Pr_{H_1}[\mathcal A'\to1]-\epsilon_\Sigma(t')\big),$$

and $\Pr_{H_0}[\mathcal A'\to1]-e^\varepsilon\Pr_{H_1}[\mathcal A'\to1]\le\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P^{\mathrm{rel}}_0\|P^{\mathrm{rel}}_1)$, since the output of $\mathcal A'$ is a function of its view. $\square$

With (b) applied to $\mathcal A'$ in $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$, a computationally bounded adversary in $G_{\mathrm{enc}}$ satisfies $\Pr_{H_0}[\mathcal A\to1]-e^\varepsilon\Pr_{H_1}[\mathcal A\to1]\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon_0(\Delta)+(1+e^\varepsilon)\epsilon_\Sigma(t')$. Here $\epsilon_0$ is computed in $G_{\mathrm{rel}}$ for the strategy of $\mathcal A'$, which is passive when $\mathcal A$ is, since $\mathcal S_\Sigma$ sets no choice.

The oracle-relative reading of (a). The reduction implements $\mathcal O$, so the loss is evaluated at a time that includes $(q+n_G)T_{\mathsf L}$. Proof of (c). In $N$ independent samples of $\mathsf{Ex}_0$ the indicators of $D^\star_0>\Delta$ are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $\epsilon_0(\Delta)$, by fidelity, since each run draws its seeds and hardware randomness fresh. The one-sided Clopper and Pearson bound has coverage at least $1-\alpha$ at every value of the parameter (Clopper and Pearson, 1934), which gives (c)(i). With no exceedance the bound is the $p$ that solves $(1-p)^N=\alpha$. Proof of (d). For (d)(i), let $\Xi$ be a point, let $c^\star\neq0$ and $L>0$, and let $\mathsf L(x(c,D);\rho)=c+L\,\mathbf 1[c=c^\star,\,D=D^1_j]\,e_1$. Part (b) holds for that strategy with $\epsilon_0(\Delta)=1$ for $\Delta<L$, which is consistent.

For (d)(ii), let $\mathsf L^\circ(x(c,D);\rho)=c$, which ignores the dataset. Every strategy sees $y_1=Z_1$ under both hypotheses, so $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$. For $c^\star\in\mathbb R^d$ let $\mathsf L_{c^\star}$ be the function of (d)(i), with $L$ chosen so that $2\Phi(L/(2\sigma))-1\ge1-\eta$. It differs from $\mathsf L^\circ$ only at training inputs with starting model $c^\star$ and dataset $D^1_j$, and $\theta^{\mathrm{act}}(\mathsf L_{c^\star})\ge1-\eta$. Fix $K$ distinct points $c^{(1)},\dots,c^{(K)}$ and draw $c^\star$ uniformly among them, independently of the certifier. Run the certifier against $\mathsf L^\circ$. Its at most $N$ Train calls name at most $N$ starting models. Its transcript depends only on its coins and on fresh evaluation and oracle randomness, since every model it obtains is a starting model it supplied, so the transcript is independent of $c^\star$. The event $H$ that some call names the starting model $c^\star$ therefore has probability at most $N/K$. Off $H$ the run against $\mathsf L_{c^\star}$ receives the same answers, so for every event $E$ of the transcript $|\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[E]-\mathbb E_{c^\star}\Pr_{\mathsf L_{c^\star}}[E]|\le N/K$. Soundness gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L_{c^\star}}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha$ for every $c^\star$, hence $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge1-\eta]\ge1-\alpha-N/K$. Take $K\ge N/\eta$. The argument never uses the discipline. It rests on where the certifier trains. $\square$

Proof of (e). Step 1, a pair code. Let $u^+_i,u^-_i$ for $i\le K$ be $2K$ points of $\mathbb R^d$. Identify $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ with $[0,1)$ under Lebesgue measure. Let $I(\rho)=\lfloor K\rho\rfloor+1$, and let $\upsilon(\rho)=+1$ when the fractional part of $K\rho$ is below $1/2$ and $-1$ otherwise. Then $(I,\upsilon)$ is uniform on $\{1,\dots,K\}\times\{\pm1\}$. The reference function is $\mathsf L^\circ(x_0;\rho)=\mathsf L^\circ(x_1;\rho)=u^{\upsilon(\rho)}_{I(\rho)}$. For $\varsigma\in\{\pm1\}^K$ let $\mathsf L_\varsigma(x_0;\rho)=u^{\varsigma_{I(\rho)}}_{I(\rho)}$ and $\mathsf L_\varsigma(x_1;\rho)=u^{-\varsigma_{I(\rho)}}_{I(\rho)}$. All of them agree at every other training input and have deterministic kernels.

Claim. For every drawn-seed certifier with $N$ Train calls and every event $E$ of its transcript, $|\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[E]-\mathbb E_\varsigma\Pr_{\mathsf L_\varsigma}[E]|\le N(2N-1)/K$, with $\varsigma$ uniform. By step 2 of lem-atomless-two-point, in its corrected form under Proofs, the transcript is $\Phi(Y_0,Y_1,W)$. Here $Y_b$ holds $N$ independent draws of the model at $x_b$, and $W$ is independent of $(Y_0,Y_1)$ and has the same law under every function of the family. Under $\mathsf L^\circ$, $Y^k_b=u^{\upsilon^k_b}_{I^k_b}$ with all pairs $(I^k_b,\upsilon^k_b)$ independent and uniform. Under the mixture, $Y^k_0=u^{\varsigma(I^k_0)}_{I^k_0}$ and $Y^k_1=u^{-\varsigma(I^k_1)}_{I^k_1}$, with independent uniform indices and an independent uniform $\varsigma$. Both laws of $(Y_0,Y_1)$ are images of the law of the indices and the signs under one map. The indices have the same law in both. Given indices whose $2N$ entries are distinct, the $2N$ signs are independent and uniform in both, under the mixture because distinct coordinates of $\varsigma$ are independent uniform signs. So the total variation between the two laws of $(Y_0,Y_1)$ is at most the probability that two of the $2N$ indices coincide, which is at most $\binom{2N}2/K$. Data processing gives the claim.

Step 2, the transfer. Suppose $\theta(\mathsf L_\varsigma)\ge\vartheta$ for every $\varsigma$, and the certifier is sound over a class that contains every $\mathsf L_\varsigma$. Then $\Pr_{\mathsf L_\varsigma}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha$ for every $\varsigma$, and the claim gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge1-\alpha-N(2N-1)/K$. Since $\mathsf L^\circ(x_0;\rho)=\mathsf L^\circ(x_1;\rho)$ for every $\rho$, $\theta(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$, every coupled pair coincides, and $D^\star_0=0$.

Step 3, part (e)(i). Let $u^+_i=2iL\,e_1$ and $u^-_i=(2i+1)L\,e_1$, so the $2K$ points lie on a line at spacing $L$. Let $T_\varsigma(y)=1$ when the code point nearest to $y$ in the first coordinate lies in $\{u^{\varsigma_i}_i\}$, the support of the model law at $x_0$ under $\mathsf L_\varsigma$. Under $P^\varsigma_0$ the view is $u+\sigma G$ with $u$ in that support and $G\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d)$, and the nearest point is $u$ unless $\sigma|G_1|\ge L/2$. So $P^\varsigma_0(T_\varsigma=0)\le2\bar\Phi(L/(2\sigma))$, and the same argument gives $P^\varsigma_1(T_\varsigma=1)\le2\bar\Phi(L/(2\sigma))$. Hence $\theta(\mathsf L_\varsigma)\ge P^\varsigma_0(T_\varsigma=1)-\gamma P^\varsigma_1(T_\varsigma=1)\ge1-2(1+\gamma)\bar\Phi(L/(2\sigma))$. Choose $L$ so that this is at least $1-\eta$, choose $K\ge N(2N-1)/\eta$, and apply step 2.

Step 4, part (e)(ii). Let $K\le d$ and $u^\pm_i=\pm B\,e_i$, so every model has norm $B$ and every function of the family lies in $\mathfrak L_B$. Let $T_\varsigma(y)=1$ when $\max_{i\le K}\varsigma_iy_i>B/2$. Under $P^\varsigma_0$ the view is $\varsigma_IBe_I+\sigma G$, so $\varsigma_Iy_I=B+\sigma\varsigma_IG_I$ and $P^\varsigma_0(T_\varsigma=0)\le\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))$. Under $P^\varsigma_1$ the view is $-\varsigma_IBe_I+\sigma G$. Then $\varsigma_Iy_I$ exceeds $B/2$ with probability $\bar\Phi(3B/(2\sigma))$, and each of the other $K-1$ coordinates $\varsigma_iy_i=\sigma\varsigma_iG_i$ does so with probability $\bar\Phi(B/(2\sigma))$. Then $\phi_\sigma(y-x)=\phi_\sigma(y)\psi(y,x)$, so the density of $P_b$ is $\phi_\sigma(y)h_b(y)$ with $h_b(y)=\mathbb E_{X\sim Q_{x_b}}\psi(y,X)$, and

$$\theta=\int\big(\phi_\sigma h_0-\gamma\,\phi_\sigma h_1\big)_+\,dy=\mathbb E_Y\big(h_0(Y)-\gamma h_1(Y)\big)_+ .$$

$$\mathbb E\,D_b\le\sqrt{\mathbb E_Y\operatorname{Var}\hat h_b(Y)}\le\sqrt{\mathbb E_Y\,\mathbb E_X\,\psi(Y,X)^2/n}\le\sqrt{e^{B^2/\sigma^2}/n}.$$

McDiarmid's inequality gives $\Pr[D_b\ge\mathbb ED_b+\kappa]\le e^{-n\kappa^2/2}$, and $\kappa=\sqrt{2\ln(2/\alpha)/n}$ with a union bound over $b$ gives the claim. Reading of (e). In dimension $d\ge e^{B^2/(16\sigma^2)}$, a drawn-seed certifier that certifies a value below $1-\frac{1+\gamma}2e^{-B^2/(16\sigma^2)}$ at a reference function with probability above $\alpha+\eta$ needs $N(2N-1)>\eta K$. The coupled certificate (c)(i) certifies $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\ln(1/\alpha)/N$ at every reference function, so $\lceil\ln(1/\alpha)/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\rceil$ runs suffice for any $B$, $\sigma$ and $d$ (proven). The worst-case bound $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;2B/\sigma)$, which needs no call, is itself $1-e^{-\Theta(B^2/\sigma^2)}$ at fixed $\varepsilon$, by the closed form in thm-coupling-certificate. So until their cost is exponential in $B^2/\sigma^2$, drawn seeds certify no value below $1-e^{-\Omega(B^2/\sigma^2)}$, the order of the worst case.

Without the oracle. A privacy bound for this procedure without the oracle needs either the law of the sequence of trained models, which nobody can write down for a transformer, or a worst-case bound on the release, which an unclipped update does not have. Part (e)(i) shows that sampling training runs does not replace the oracle when updates are unclipped, and part (e)(ii) shows that with clipping at $B$ it costs $e^{\Omega(B^2/\sigma^2)}$ runs. With the oracle, part (b) holds for every learning function and every strategy. It uses the learning function only through $\epsilon_b(\Delta)$, the probability that a coupled pair of updates differs by more than $\Delta$ in some round of the execution. Part (c) certifies that probability at a cost of $2RN$ Train calls for client $j$, free of $d$, of $B$ and of the architecture. Criteria 1 to 3 hold. The quantity is defined by coupled Train calls, the problem without the oracle needs the law of the trained model, and the bound depends on the learning function only through an oracle-measured probability.

Degenerate oracles. Criterion 4 is checked against the objects of def-degenerate-oracles. Under the bottom oracle, part (b) is a statement about laws and holds. Under the top oracle it holds as well, since the proof treats both policies, and the tightness clause shows that it can be an equality there. So part (b) alone does not meet criterion 4. It should not, because the coupled execution never credits randomness that the seed controls (ch-identity-coupling-scope). Part (c) is a statement about an experimenter. In the coupled discipline it certifies $\epsilon_0(\Delta)\le\ln(1/\alpha)/N$ at every reference function of (e). In the drawn-seed discipline the statement fails. No drawn-seed certifier sound over every learning function certifies a value below $1-\eta$ at those functions, by (e)(i). Over $\mathfrak L_B$ none certifies a value below $1-\frac{1+\gamma}2e^{-B^2/(16\sigma^2)}$ with fewer than about $e^{B^2/(32\sigma^2)}$ runs, by (e)(ii). The theorem therefore meets criterion 4 through its certificate, as thm-coupling-certificate does, and quantitatively in the clipped case. Part (d)(ii) shows a limit that no discipline removes. Against all active strategies at once, no certifier in either discipline certifies a value below $1-\eta$ uniformly over learning functions.

Scope. Four limits follow from the proof. Noise that the learning function draws from its seed is not credited, as in ch-identity-coupling-scope, so noisy gradient descent inside client $j$ adds nothing to $\sigma$ (heuristic for the size of the resulting tail). The other clients' contributions $A_t$ are not credited, since the coupled execution shares their randomness. Rounds with noise levels $\sigma_t$ compose to $\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;(\sum_t\Delta^2/\sigma_t^2)^{1/2})$, by the same kernel with $\mu_t=\Delta/\sigma_t$ (proven, the same proof). For threshold CKKS the smudging noise that IND-CPA-D needs is calibrated to the decryption error, which is far smaller than any useful $\Delta$, so the noise that (b) credits must be added for privacy and does not come with the encryption (heuristic, a statement about common parameters).

Prior work. Six lines of work bear on the statement.

- Gaussian differential privacy (Dong, Roth and Su, JRSS-B 2022, from memory) composes mechanisms with known sensitivity. Step 4 proves the case needed here directly, for mixtures whose shift is bounded by $\Delta$.
- DP-SGD (Abadi et al., CCS 2016, from memory) bounds the sensitivity by clipping and credits noise inside training. Part (b) needs no clipping and credits noise outside training.
- Individual privacy accounting (Feldman and Zrnic, NeurIPS 2021, abstract read in round 01) uses realized per-record gradient norms under clipping with a Rényi filter. Its realized norms play the part of $D^\star_0$. They are random quantities of one run, while $\epsilon_0(\Delta)$ is a probability fixed by the procedure.
- Sensitivity sampling (Rubinstein and Aldà, ICML 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02562, search summary only) estimates the sensitivity of a black-box function by sampling pairs of databases and obtains random differential privacy, a guarantee for most pairs under a distribution. Part (c) estimates a tail over the training seed for one fixed pair, and the coupling turns that estimate into a bound for the pair.
- For the ciphertext layer, Li and Micciancio (EUROCRYPT 2021, ePrint 2020/1533) show that IND-CPA does not suffice for CKKS once decryptions are released. Li, Micciancio, Schultz and Sorrell (CRYPTO 2022, https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/816, search summary only) show that Gaussian noise added to CKKS decryptions gives IND-CPA-D. Mouchet, Troncoso-Pastoriza, Bossuat and Hubaux (PoPETs 2021, https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/304, search summary only) give the multiparty scheme with collective decryption. These are the sources of the heuristic label on the realization of $\mathcal F$.
- For (e)(iii), Goldfeld, Greenewald, Niles-Weed and Polyanskiy (IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13576, search summary only) give the rate $e^{O(d)}n^{-1/2}$ for Gaussian-smoothed empirical measures of sub-Gaussian laws. For laws on a ball of radius $B$, (e)(iii) has the dimension-free factor $e^{B^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ in its rate. I do not claim (e)(iii) as new.

No search found a composition bound whose learning-specific term is a coupled tail certified from training runs. Novelty of (b) with (c) stays open until the auditing literature is read.

### Replay-audit steering [thm-replay-audit-steer]

Setting. A training input $x=(\ell,m_0,D,\mathcal H)$ is fixed. $D$ is the prover's dataset, bound by a commitment $c$ that the auditor can open, and steering through the choice of $D$ is outside the theorem. $(\mathcal M,d)$ is a proper metric space, one whose closed balls are compact, and its Borel sets form the standard Borel structure, as for $\mathbb R^p$ with a norm. $T\subseteq\mathcal M$ is a nonempty closed target set, so $m\mapsto d(m,T)$ is continuous and its infimum is attained. A random oracle $R$ maps strings to $\mathcal R$, with independent values of law $\mu$. The audit game with tolerance $\tau\ge0$ and $r\ge1$ replays runs as follows.

1. The prover, with access to $R$ and $\mathcal O$, outputs a model $m$ and a nonce $\eta$.
2. The auditor sets $\rho=R(c,\eta)$, makes $r$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x,\rho)$ with answers $m_1,\dots,m_r$, and accepts when $\min_{i\le r}d(m,m_i)\le\tau$.
3. The prover wins when $m\in T$ and the auditor accepts.

This is the goal steer of def-learning-oracle-model, with the auditor's acceptance as the acceptance event. The prover is unbounded and may know $\mathsf L$. Its Train calls draw fresh hardware randomness, independent of the auditor's. For $\rho\sim\mu$ and independent $\xi,\xi'\sim\pi$, let $M=\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ and $M'=\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi')$. The reach, the spread and the joint reach are

$$p_\tau(T)=\Pr[d(M,T)\le\tau],\qquad s_\tau=\Pr[d(M,M')>\tau],\qquad p^\circ_\tau(T)=\Pr[d(M,T)\le\tau,\ d(M,M')\le\tau].$$

Drawn-seed calls measure the reach. Coupled calls with one seed name measure the spread and the joint reach. Each is a Bernoulli parameter, certifiable as in thm-coupling-certificate (b).

Statement.

(a) Soundness. Every prover that makes $q_R$ queries to $R$ and any number of Train calls, and that may know $\mathsf L$, wins with probability at most $(q_R+1)\,r\,p_\tau(T)$.

(b) Completeness. The honest prover, which outputs $\mathsf{Train}(x,R(c,\eta))$ for a nonce $\eta$, is accepted with probability at least $1-s_\tau$.

(c) Attack. For $q\ge1$ the grinding prover does the following for $k=1,\dots,q$. It makes one query $\rho_k=R(c,\eta_k)$ at a new nonce and one call $M_k=\mathsf{Train}(x,\rho_k)$, and it stops at the first $k$ with $d(M_k,T)\le\tau/2$, outputting a nearest point of $T$ to $M_k$ and the nonce $\eta_k$. With $p=p_{\tau/2}(T)>0$ it wins with probability at least

$$\frac{p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)}{p}\big(1-(1-p)^q\big)\ \ge\ \Big(1-\frac{s_{\tau/2}}{p}\Big)\big(1-(1-p)^q\big).$$

(d) Drawn-seed auditors. Let $\mathcal M=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ with the circle distance, and identify $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ with $[0,1)$ under Lebesgue measure. For $a\in[0,1)$ let $\mathsf L_a(x;\rho,\xi)=\rho+a\bmod1$, with $\mathsf L_a$ constant at every other training input. Every member has deterministic kernels, $s_0=0$, and $p_0(\{t\})=0$ for every point $t$. So by (a) and (b) the replay auditor with $\tau=0$ and $r=1$ accepts the honest prover with probability one, and no prover with target $\{t\}$ wins, whatever its number of queries. Now let an auditor work in the drawn-seed discipline, with any number of Train calls and any acceptance rule, and accept the honest prover with probability at least $1-s$ under every member. Then for every $\kappa>0$ there is a point $t$ such that the prover that queries $R$ once and outputs $t$ with that nonce wins with probability at least $1-s-\kappa$ under every member.

(e) Oblivious provers. Identify $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ with $[0,1)$ under Lebesgue measure and fix $k\ge1$. For a table $U=(U_i)_{i<2^k}$ of independent uniform variables let $\mathsf L_U$ agree with $\mathsf L$ except at $x$, where $\mathsf L_U(x;\rho,\xi)=\mathsf L(x;U_{\lfloor2^k\rho\rfloor},\xi)$. Then $\mathbb E_U\,p_\tau(T;\mathsf L_U)=p_\tau(T;\mathsf L)$ and $\mathbb E_U\,s_\tau(\mathsf L_U)=s_\tau(\mathsf L)$. Every prover that reaches the learning function only through $\mathcal O$, with at most $q_T$ Train calls at $x$ and any number of queries to $R$ and of Train calls at other inputs, satisfies

$$\mathbb E_U\Pr_{\mathsf L_U}[\text{win}]\le(q_T+1)\,r\,p_\tau(T;\mathsf L).$$

Label. Proven, (a) to (e).

Proof of (a). Implement $R$ by lazy sampling. Let $\rho_1,\rho_2,\dots,\rho_{q_R+1}$ be independent with law $\mu$. The $k$-th distinct point the prover queries receives $\rho_k$. If the auditor evaluates $R$ at a point the prover never queried, that point receives the first unused $\rho_k$. This gives $R$ its law. Let $K^*\le q_R+1$ be the index of the value the auditor uses. Let $(\xi_{k,i})_{k\le q_R+1,\,i\le r}$ be independent with law $\pi$ and independent of everything else, and let the auditor's $i$-th replay use $\xi_{K^*,i}$. The prover's transcript and output do not involve these variables, since its own calls draw other hardware randomness. So the auditor's replays have the law the game prescribes. Suppose the prover wins. Then $m\in T$ and $d(m,m_i)\le\tau$ for some $i$, so $d(m_i,T)\le d(m_i,m)\le\tau$ with $m_i=\mathsf L(x;\rho_{K^*},\xi_{K^*,i})$. So the win lies in the union of the events $F_{k,i}=\{d(\mathsf L(x;\rho_k,\xi_{k,i}),T)\le\tau\}$ over $k\le q_R+1$ and $i\le r$. Each pair $(\rho_k,\xi_{k,i})$ has law $\mu\otimes\pi$, so $\Pr[F_{k,i}]=p_\tau(T)$, whatever the prover does. A union bound finishes. $\square$

Proof of (b). The seed $\rho=R(c,\eta)$ has law $\mu$. The honest model is $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ and the first replay is $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi_1)$, with independent $\xi,\xi_1$. The auditor rejects only if $d(m,m_1)>\tau$, an event of probability $s_\tau$. $\square$

Proof of (c). The nearest-point map $m\mapsto\{t\in T:d(m,t)=d(m,T)\}$ has nonempty compact values in a proper space and admits a Borel selection (Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardzewski, from memory), which the prover uses. The nonces are new, so $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_q$ are independent with law $\mu$. Let $M_k=\mathsf L(x;\rho_k,\xi'_k)$ be the prover's run, and $M^{\mathrm a}_k=\mathsf L(x;\rho_k,\xi_{k,1})$ the auditor's first replay if the prover stops at $k$, with all hardware randomness independent. The triples $(\rho_k,\xi'_k,\xi_{k,1})$ are independent across $k$. Let $A_k=\{d(M_k,T)\le\tau/2\}$ and $B_k=\{d(M_k,M^{\mathrm a}_k)\le\tau/2\}$, so $\Pr[A_k]=p$ and $\Pr[A_k\cap B_k]=p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)$. If the prover stops at $k$ and $B_k$ holds, its output $t_k\in T$ has $d(t_k,M^{\mathrm a}_k)\le d(t_k,M_k)+d(M_k,M^{\mathrm a}_k)\le\tau$, so it wins. The prover stops at $k$ on $A^c_1\cap\dots\cap A^c_{k-1}\cap A_k$. By independence,

$$\Pr[\text{win}]\ge\sum_{k=1}^q(1-p)^{k-1}\,p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)=\frac{p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)}{p}\big(1-(1-p)^q\big).$$

Finally $p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)\ge\Pr[A_k]-\Pr[B^c_k]=p-s_{\tau/2}$. $\square$

Reading of (a) and (c). For $qp\le1$, $1-(1-p)^q\ge(1-e^{-1})\,qp$. The grinding prover with $q=\min(q_R,q_T)$ attempts wins with probability at least $0.63\,q\,p^\circ_{\tau/2}(T)$, and no prover with $q_R$ queries wins with probability above $(q_R+1)\,r\,p_\tau(T)$. The two match up to the change from $\tau$ to $\tau/2$, the factor $r$, and the ratio $p^\circ/p$, which is near one when the spread at $\tau/2$ is small against the reach (proven, from (a) and (c)).

Proof of (d). The first claims are direct. $\mathsf L_a$ does not use $\xi$, so $s_0=0$, and $\rho+a\bmod1$ is uniform, so $\Pr[\mathsf L_a(x;\rho)=t]=0$. Now fix a drawn-seed auditor and a nonce $\eta_0$, and let $\rho=R(c,\eta_0)$, uniform on $[0,1)$. Under $\mathsf L_a$ a drawn-seed Train call at $x$ returns $\rho'+a\bmod1$ for a fresh uniform $\rho'$, which is uniform whatever $a$. Calls at other inputs return a constant, and the auditor never opens a seed name. So given the prover's output $m$ and the seed $\rho$, the law of everything else the auditor sees does not depend on $a$. Its acceptance probability is a measurable function $\mathrm{acc}(m,\rho)$, the same for every member. The honest prover outputs $m=\rho+a\bmod1$, and completeness under every member gives $\int_0^1\mathrm{acc}(\rho+a\bmod1,\rho)\,d\rho\ge1-s$ for every $a$. Integrate over $a$. The map $(a,\rho)\mapsto(\rho+a\bmod1,\rho)$ preserves Lebesgue measure on the square, so Fubini's theorem gives

$$\int_0^1w(t)\,dt\ge1-s,\qquad w(t)=\int_0^1\mathrm{acc}(t,\rho)\,d\rho .$$

The prover that outputs $(t,\eta_0)$ has $m\in\{t\}$ and is accepted with probability $w(t)$, under every member. Since the average of $w$ is at least $1-s$, the set of $t$ with $w(t)\ge1-s-\kappa$ has positive measure, which proves the claim. $\square$

Proof of (e). For a fixed $U$, $\rho\sim\mu$ makes $\lfloor2^k\rho\rfloor$ uniform on $\{0,\dots,2^k-1\}$, so $p_\tau(T;\mathsf L_U)=2^{-k}\sum_i\Pr_\xi[d(\mathsf L(x;U_i,\xi),T)\le\tau]$, whose expectation over $U$ is $p_\tau(T;\mathsf L)$. Under the identification of $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ with $[0,1)$, $\mathsf L(x;u,\xi)$ with $u$ uniform has law $Q_x$. The spread is the same computation.

Fix a prover. A Train call at $x$ with seed $\rho$, passed as a value or a name, returns $\mathsf L(x;U_J,\xi')$ with prefix $J=\lfloor2^k\rho\rfloor$. Let $J_1,J_2,\dots,J_{K'}$ be the distinct prefixes of these calls in order of first use, with $K'\le q_T$. Calls at other inputs, queries to $R$ and Eval calls on the prover's own models do not involve $U$. By lazy sampling for the table, $U_{J_k}$ is uniform and independent of the prover's transcript before its first call with prefix $J_k$. Let $J^*$ be the prefix of $\rho^*=R(c,\eta)$ for the output nonce. If $J^*$ is not among $J_1,\dots,J_{K'}$, then $U_{J^*}$ is uniform and independent of the prover's whole transcript and output. Let $(\xi_{k,i})$ for $k\le q_T+1$ and $i\le r$ be independent with law $\pi$ and independent of everything else. Let the auditor's replays use $\xi_{k,i}$ when $J^*=J_k$, and $\xi_{q_T+1,i}$ when $J^*$ is new. As in (a) this gives the replays their law. A win forces some replay $\mathsf L(x;U_{J^*},\xi_{\cdot,i})$ within $\tau$ of $T$. So the win lies in the union of the events $F_{k,i}=\{k\le K',\ d(\mathsf L(x;U_{J_k},\xi_{k,i}),T)\le\tau\}$ for $k\le q_T$, and $F_{*,i}=\{J^*\text{ new},\ d(\mathsf L(x;U_{J^*},\xi_{q_T+1,i}),T)\le\tau\}$. In each event the table value is uniform, by lazy sampling, and the hardware randomness is independent of it, so each event has probability at most $p_\tau(T;\mathsf L)$. A union bound over the $(q_T+1)r$ events finishes. The prover's queries to $R$ do not enter, so the bound holds for every $q_R$. $\square$

Without the oracle. Without replay at an explicit seed, part (d) shows that an auditor cannot separate completeness from soundness. Every complete drawn-seed auditor accepts some target of reach zero with probability close to its completeness. With replay, parts (a) to (c) fix the steering probability up to the factors stated, in terms of the reach, the spread and the joint reach. These are properties of the law of trained models near $T$ and of the hardware randomness at one seed. Nobody can write them down for a transformer, and Train calls measure them for every learning function. Part (e) adds that the steering probability of a prover without the learning function grows with its training runs alone, and queries to $R$ do not enter. Criteria 1 to 3 hold.

Degenerate oracles. The prover holds the value $R(c,\eta)$, so under the realizable policy it already passes the auditor's seed to Train, and the seed-revealing policy adds nothing. Part (a) holds under both adversary-side degenerate oracles, so criterion 4 is checked through the auditor, an honest party whose Train calls the theorem designs (def-degenerate-oracles, item 3). In the coupled discipline the auditor is sound and complete as (a) and (b) state. In the drawn-seed discipline the statement fails, by (d). The theorem meets criterion 4 in the same way as thm-coupling-certificate. One more variant shows the role of $R$, though it is not a degenerate oracle. A prover that chooses the seed itself may pick a seed whose run lands in $T$. For $\mathsf L(x;\rho)=\rho$ on $[0,1)$, $T=[0,\eta']$ and $\tau=0$ it wins with probability one while $p_0(T)=\eta'$, so (a) fails without the random oracle (proven, by the example).

Prior work. Proof-of-learning (Jia et al., IEEE S&P 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05633) verifies a training trajectory by replaying segments within a tolerance. Zhang et al. (IEEE S&P 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09454) and Fang et al. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03567, search summary only, venue from memory EuroS&P 2023) construct spoofs that pass such checks. Fang et al. conclude that a provably robust verification needs further understanding of optimization in deep learning. Our audit replays the whole run from a seed derived through a random oracle, and part (a) proves a bound in terms of two quantities that Train calls measure, with no assumption on optimization. Work on verifiable training that controls hardware nondeterminism (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09603, search summary only) makes replays agree, which drives the spread to zero and allows a small $\tau$. Part (a) is the grinding argument for the Fiat and Shamir transform in the random-oracle model. Its content here is the reduction of steering to reach and spread, the matching attack, and the separation of (d). Novelty is open until the proof-of-learning literature is read in full.

### Black-box certifiability, revised [thm-black-box-certifiability]

Setting. One learning node trains $x_b$ under $H_b$, and the model reaches the adversary through the query channel of def-learning-oracle-model, as a name with label eval, a query set $U$ and an allowance $Q\ge q_E$. The query set $U\subseteq\mathcal U$ is finite, so the oracle refuses an Eval call on the served name at a query outside $U$. The output set $\mathcal Y$ is finite with $C\ge2$ elements. The evaluation map may be randomized, with fresh $\sigma$ per query. Let $\mathcal T_q$ be the set of deterministic strategies with $q$ queries, complete $C$-ary trees of depth $q$ whose internal nodes carry queries in $U$ and whose leaves carry bits, so

$$|\mathcal T_q|\le|U|^{(C^q-1)/(C-1)}\,2^{C^q}.$$

For a tree $\tau$ let $A(\tau)=\Pr_{H_1}[\tau=1]-\Pr_{H_0}[\tau=1]$, where the model is drawn from $Q_{x_b}$ and each query has fresh evaluation randomness. Let $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal T_q}A(\tau)$ and $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}=\mathrm{TV}(Q_{x_0},Q_{x_1})$.

Statement. Parts (a) to (d) are those of round 01, with $n$ calls at each input in place of $N$.

(o) Reduction. Under the realizable policy every adversary with $q_E$ Eval calls on the served name has advantage at most $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$, whatever its Train calls, and also when it knows $\mathsf L$.

(a) Certificate from both sides. With $n$ drawn-seed models at each input, opened by the experimenter, let $\hat A(\tau)$ be the frequency of $\tau=1$ over the models at $x_1$ minus that over the models at $x_0$, each tree run once per model with fresh evaluation randomness. For every learning function, with probability at least $1-\alpha$,

$$\max_{\tau\in\mathcal T_q}|\hat A(\tau)-A(\tau)|\le\epsilon_n:=\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(4|\mathcal T_q|/\alpha)}{n}},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\Big|\max_\tau\hat A(\tau)-\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)\Big|\le\epsilon_n .$$

(b) Exchange rate. The adversary under the realizable policy that spends $q_T=2n$ Train calls on shadow models, $n$ at each input, and plays $\hat\tau=\arg\max_\tau\hat A(\tau)$ with $q_E$ Eval calls on the served name has advantage at least $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)-2\sqrt{\ln(2|\mathcal T_{q_E}|)/n}$, for every learning function.

(c) White box. Let $\mathsf{ev}$ be deterministic and $Q_{x_0},Q_{x_1}$ atomless, and let $\varepsilon\ge0$ with $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(Q)+\varepsilon<1$. A drawn-seed procedure with $N$ Train calls that accepts the collapse claim $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}\le\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(Q)+\varepsilon$ with probability at most $\alpha$ whenever the claim is false, over every learning function, accepts with probability at most $\alpha$ under $\mathsf L$. A white-box test whose certified advantage exceeds a certified upper bound on $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(Q)+\varepsilon$ refutes the claim.

(d) Shared seeds. With deterministic $\mathsf{ev}$ and $F(m)=(\mathsf{ev}(m,u))_{u\in U}$, for every $q$,

$$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)\le\mathrm{TV}(F_*Q_{x_0},F_*Q_{x_1})\le\Pr_\Gamma[F(W_0)\neq F(W_1)]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{wb}}\le\beta,$$

both certifiable from coupled pairs as in thm-coupling-certificate (b).

Label. Proven, (o), (a), (b), (c), (d). Open, a matching lower bound for (b), and how loose (d) is against (a).

Proof of (o). Represent the randomness of the adversary's own calls in advance. The $k$-th own Train call uses an independent pair $(\rho'_k,\xi'_k)$ when it draws, and an independent $\xi'_k$ when it passes a seed. Its own Eval calls use independent $\sigma'_k$. Call the coins and these variables $\omega$. They are independent of the hypothesis and of the served model. Given $\omega$ the adversary is deterministic, and its next call on the served name is a function of the earlier answers on that name. Every answered Eval call on the served name is at a query in $U$, by the query channel. A refused call returns a fixed symbol that depends neither on the model nor on the hypothesis, so it can be removed from the interaction without changing the output. Padded to $q_E$ queries, the interaction with the served model is therefore a tree $\tau_\omega\in\mathcal T_{q_E}$, and the output is its leaf bit. The advantage is $\mathbb E_\omega A(\tau_\omega)\le\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$. Nothing in the argument restricts $\omega$, so it may contain the adversary's knowledge of $\mathsf L$. $\square$

The clause is needed. Without the query set, (o) fails by the counterexample of ch-query-universe (proven).

Proofs of (a) to (d). Unchanged, in round-01/builder.md, with $n$ in place of $N$.

Scope. Two limits. The certificate of (a) costs about $C^{q_E}\ln|U|$ runs, so it serves small $q_E$, and whether that cost is necessary is the open lower bound. The collapse certificate of (d) needs a small $\beta$. For learning functions of the stochastic gradient type, $\beta$ is at least the probability that record $j$ enters some batch, because an update that uses the record changes the parameters almost surely (heuristic), and (d) is then vacuous. With (c), a claim that $Q$ label queries reach what the head reveals can then be refuted and, for realistic learners, never certified (heuristic, through the second limit).

Without the oracle. A bound on what $q_E$ label queries reveal needs the law of the trained transformer's labels on $U$. With the oracle, (a) certifies $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}$ from both sides and (b) gives an attack, uniformly over learning functions, at a cost that depends on the learning function only through $C$, $|U|$ and $q_E$. Part (c) fixes the form any answer to Küpçü's question can take in the drawn-seed discipline. The statement that $Q$ label queries reach what the head reveals can be refuted and never certified on atomless model laws. Part (d) shows what shared seeds add, a certifiable collapse when the coupled divergence is small.

Degenerate oracles. Part (o) is an upper bound for every adversary, tested under the bottom and the top oracle. Under the bottom oracle it holds, for an adversary that knows $\mathsf L$ as well. Under the top oracle it fails. In the example of thm-coupling-certificate (c)(i), let $\mathsf{ev}(m,u)$ be the $u$-th binary digit of $m$. Both model laws are uniform, so $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q)=0$ for every $q$. A top adversary that opens $\rho$ computes the first digit under $H_0$, queries it once and wins with advantage one. For one fixed learning function the bottom oracle attains $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$ with no Train call, by def-degenerate-oracles (ii), so the exchange rate (b) has content for adversaries chosen before the learning function. Parts (a), (c) and (d) concern the experimenter. Part (c) is the limit of the drawn-seed discipline and part (d) the certificate of the coupled discipline.

Non-triviality, requested. Criteria 1 to 3 hold, as the challenger found in round 01. Criterion 4 holds because (o) fails under the top oracle. With the query set, (o) and (a) together certify the label-only leakage of an arbitrary trained model at budget $q_E$ from above, uniformly over learning functions and against adversaries that know $\mathsf L$. I ask for ACCEPT NONTRIVIAL.

### Reference access, part (c) for explicit seeds [thm-reference-access]

Statement of (c), revised. Suppose some law $Q$ on $\mathcal M$ has an atomless image $P=\kappa_*Q$. Let $q$ bound the adversary's Train calls at $x_0$ and $x_1$. For every adversary under the realizable policy, explicit seeds included, and every $\epsilon>0$, there is a learning function with deterministic kernels and $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)=1$ under which the adversary has advantage at most $\epsilon$. For every even $M$ and every $k\ge1$, a random member of the family $\mathsf L_{S,U}$ below gives expected advantage at most $2(q+1)(2q+1)/M+2q2^{-k}$. The family depends on the seed only through its first $k$ binary digits, so the statement holds on a $k$-bit seed space.

Label. Proven. The round-01 proof of (c) under its conditions (i) and (ii) stays valid and is subsumed. Parts (a) and (b) are unchanged.

Proof of (c). Identify $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ with $[0,1)$ under Lebesgue measure, by the realization fact, and let $\pi_k(\rho)=\lfloor2^k\rho\rfloor$. By Sierpiński's theorem cut $\mathcal V$ into $M$ cells of $P$-mass $1/M$, and for $|S|=M/2$ let $A_S$ be the union of the cells indexed by $S$. By the realization fact choose measurable maps $g$, $g^S_0$ and $g^S_1$ on $[0,1)$ such that, for $u$ uniform, $g(u)\sim Q$, $g^S_0(u)\sim Q(\cdot\mid\kappa^{-1}A_S)$ and $g^S_1(u)\sim Q(\cdot\mid\kappa^{-1}A^c_S)$. For independent uniform tables $U=(U_{b,i})_{b\in\{0,1\},\,i<2^k}$ let

$$\mathsf L_{S,U}(x_b;\rho)=g^S_b\big(U_{b,\pi_k(\rho)}\big),\qquad\mathsf L^\circ_U(x_b;\rho)=g\big(U_{b,\pi_k(\rho)}\big),$$

both constant at every other training input, with deterministic kernels. Every $\mathsf L_{S,U}$ puts its views at $x_0$ in $A_S$ and at $x_1$ in $A_S^c$, so $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)=1$.

Fix the adversary, and draw $S$ uniformly and $U$ independently. Under $H_b$ the honest seed $\rho^*$ is drawn by the oracle, and its prefix $J=\pi_k(\rho^*)$ is uniform and independent of $(S,U)$.
