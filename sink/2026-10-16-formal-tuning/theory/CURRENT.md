## Definitions

### Learning oracle [def-learning-oracle]

Setting. The data space $\mathcal Z$, the model space $\mathcal M$, the query space $\mathcal U$, the output space $\mathcal Y$, the set $\Lambda$ of learner names and the space $\mathcal X$ of training inputs are standard Borel spaces. A dataset is a finite multiset over $\mathcal Z$. A training input is $x=(\ell,m,D,\mathcal H)$, made of a learner name $\ell$, a starting model $m$, a dataset $D$ and a hypothesis class $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal M$ from a finite family. The learning function is a measurable map

$$\mathsf L:\mathcal X\times\mathcal R\times\Xi\to\mathcal M,\qquad(x;\rho,\xi)\mapsto\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi).$$

The seed $\rho$ has an atomless law $\mu$ on a standard Borel space $\mathcal R$, and the hardware randomness $\xi$ has a law $\pi$ on a standard Borel space $\Xi$. $\Pi_x$ is the law of $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ under $\mu\otimes\pi$. Training has deterministic kernels when $\Xi$ is a point. The evaluation map $\mathsf{ev}(m,u;\upsilon)\in\mathcal Y$, with $\upsilon$ of law $\nu$, is public.

The oracle. A learning oracle $\mathcal O$ for $(\mathsf L,\mu,\pi)$ stores models and seeds and names them separately for each caller. The $k$-th name a caller receives is the integer $k$, and each name a caller holds carries a label, open or eval. The oracle answers three calls.

1. $\mathsf{Train}(x,s)$. The starting model in $x$ is a model or a model name, the dataset is given by value, and $s$ is a seed, a seed name or $\bot$. On $\bot$ the oracle draws $\rho\sim\mu$ and stores it under a new name, and otherwise the run uses the seed that $s$ gives. The oracle draws $\xi\sim\pi$, stores $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ under a new name and returns the new names.
2. $\mathsf{Eval}(h,u)$, for a model name $h$, returns $\mathsf{ev}(m,u;\upsilon)$ for the stored model $m$ and a fresh $\upsilon\sim\nu$.
3. $\mathsf{Open}(h)$ returns the stored object.

A grant gives a caller a new name of a stored object, with a label, when a procedure or a policy directs it. A grant is not a call.

Fidelity. Every seed drawn on $\bot$, every $\xi$ and every $\upsilon$ is independent of all other randomness in the execution.

Access. $\mathsf{Train}$ and $\mathsf{Open}$ require label open on every name among their arguments, and $\mathsf{Eval}$ requires label eval or open. The oracle refuses any other call. A caller holds label open on every name that its own call returns, and it holds a name only in this way or through a grant.

Remarks. Fidelity of hardware randomness is a modelling assumption, heuristic for GPU training, where it assumes that the order of floating-point reductions in one run carries no information about another. The atomless seed law is an idealization that lets proofs realize arbitrary laws from the seed. thm-reference-access (c) holds on an $n_{\mathrm{bit}}$-bit seed space with an additive term of order $q\,2^{-n_{\mathrm{bit}}}$. Label eval gives black-box access to a model and label open white-box access. A value that a caller holds needs no name.

### Learning-oracle model [def-learning-oracle-model]

Procedure. A procedure is a dataflow graph $G$ whose nodes run in a fixed topological order. Each node has an owner and one of four operations. An input node holds a dataset, a seed or a public object, and a sampler of fresh records is one. A learning node calls $\mathsf{Train}(x_v,s_v)$, with $x_v$ formed from parent values and choices and $s_v=\bot$ unless the procedure feeds the node a seed node. An evaluation node calls $\mathsf{Eval}$. A general node computes a randomized function of its parents with randomness of its own, which the oracle does not hold. In $G^{\mathcal O}$ the calls go to $\mathcal O$, and the procedure grants each honest owner label open on the objects its node uses. In the real procedure $G$ the nodes compute $\mathsf L$ and $\mathsf{ev}$ directly with fresh randomness.

Channels. Each node value reaches a coalition $A$ through one of three channels. Through the plain channel $A$ receives the value. Through the query channel the procedure grants $A$ a name of the value with label eval and fixes a query set $U\subseteq\mathcal U$ and an allowance $Q$, and the oracle answers at most $Q$ Eval calls of $A$ on that name, each at a query in $U$. Through channel none $A$ receives nothing.

Adversary. A coalition $A$ acts through an adversary $\mathcal A$, an oracle algorithm that reaches $\mathsf L$ only through $\mathcal O$, computes $\mathsf{ev}$ itself and stops with probability one. It is unbounded, except in a statement that uses a computational assumption. There it runs in time $\mathsf t$ with unit cost per oracle call, and a reduction that implements $\mathcal O$ counts the time $\mathsf t_{\mathsf L}$ of each run of $\mathsf L$. Its choices are the node arguments that $A$ sets, the values and learner names of nodes that $A$ owns, and its queries. They also include the learner names of nodes whose training code $A$ supplies. A strategy is passive when the choices that enter procedure nodes are the functions of procedure values that the protocol prescribes, and active otherwise.

Schedule. For each node in order, the adversary receives the channel outputs of the nodes run so far, makes oracle calls and sets its choices for the node, and then the node runs. After the last node it makes further calls and stops. Its view is its transcript, made of the channel outputs, the answers to its calls and its coins.

Budget. $(q_T,q_E)$ bounds the adversary's Train calls and its Eval calls on names with label eval. Calls that the procedure requires of an owner in $A$ are not charged, and an Eval call on a name with label open is free.

Goals. To distinguish, the adversary faces two hypotheses $H_0,H_1$ on the inputs and outputs a bit, with advantage $\Pr[1\mid H_1]-\Pr[1\mid H_0]$. A privacy statement bounds $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)=\sup_E(P_0(E)-\gamma P_1(E))$, $\gamma\ge1$, over measurable sets $E$ of views, for the laws $P_b$ of the view under $H_b$. To steer, the adversary faces a node, a target set $T\subseteq\mathcal M$ and an acceptance event, and it succeeds when the node's value lies in $T$ and the event holds.

Remarks. A sum, a ciphertext or a noisy value is a general node released through the plain channel. A procedure in which an owner sees the iterates of training has one node per step. A statement about the budget takes one of three forms. An upper bound for every adversary is free of $q_T$ and may depend on $q_E$ on a served name. An attack or an estimator is one oracle algorithm whose guarantee holds for every learning function in a stated class. An impossibility says that some learning function in a stated class, or a random one from a stated family, defeats every oracle algorithm within the budget, and it may do so in a stated procedure.

Remark, transfer of lower bounds. A certifier sound for a target $\theta'\ge\theta$ is sound for $\theta$, since $\theta(\mathsf L)\le\theta'(\mathsf L)\le\hat t$ on the soundness event (proven). So a lower bound of the form $\Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\hat t\ge\vartheta]\ge p$ for every certifier sound for $\theta$ holds for every certifier sound for $\theta'$, and it reads as a failure when $\theta'(\mathsf L^\circ)=0$. A larger procedure gives such a $\theta'$ when, for every adversary of the stated procedure, some adversary of the larger one has a view whose image under one measurable map, the same under both hypotheses, has the law of the stated view under each hypothesis. Then $\theta'\ge\theta$ by data processing (proven). Honest clients whose updates enter a sum with client $j$'s do not meet this condition, and open problem 1 asks whether they lower the divergence.

Remark, inversion. For a win event $E$ of an inversion, for instance the views from which the adversary's guess lies within a tolerance of the record that $H_1$ removes, $P_0(E)\le e^\varepsilon P_1(E)+\mathrm H_{e^\varepsilon}(P_0\|P_1)$ by the definition of $\mathrm H_\gamma$ (proven). So every bound of the theory bounds the success of inversion under $H_0$ by $e^\varepsilon$ times its success under $H_1$, where the target is absent, plus the bound. Model extraction targets the model and has no pair of hypotheses, so no theorem of this theory covers it.

### Certifier [def-certifier]

An experimenter is a caller outside every procedure that reaches $\mathsf L$ only through $\mathcal O$. A caller works in the drawn-seed discipline when every Train call it makes has $s=\bot$ and it never opens a seed name, and in the coupled discipline otherwise. A target is a functional $\theta(\mathsf L)\in[0,1]$. A certifier is an experimenter that makes at most $N$ Train calls and outputs $\hat t\in[0,1]$. It is sound at level $\alpha$ over a class $\mathfrak L$ of learning functions when $\Pr_{\mathsf L}[\theta(\mathsf L)\le\hat t]\ge1-\alpha$ for every $\mathsf L\in\mathfrak L$. A lower certifier outputs $\underline t$ with $\Pr_{\mathsf L}[\underline t\le\theta(\mathsf L)]\ge1-\alpha$.

Remark. The main example of a target is the channel form. For training inputs $x_0\neq x_1$ and a channel $K$, a Markov kernel from $\mathcal M$ to a standard Borel view space $\mathcal V$, the view laws are $P_b=\int K(\cdot\mid m)\,\Pi_{x_b}(dm)$, and $\theta$ is $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ or $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$. A deterministic channel is a measurable map $\kappa:\mathcal M\to\mathcal V$. A statement that uses $n$ calls at each of two inputs has $N=2n$.

### Degenerate oracles [def-degenerate-oracles]

Fix a procedure $G$ and a coalition $A$. A policy directs grants to $A$ beyond those of the procedure.

1. The bottom oracle is the realizable policy, which grants nothing, with no bound on $q_T$.
2. The top oracle is the seed-revealing policy, which grants $A$ label open on the seed name of every honest learning node when the node runs.
3. For an experimenter, or an honest party whose Train calls a theorem designs such as an auditor, the bottom and the top case are the drawn-seed and the coupled discipline of def-certifier.

Criterion 4 tests a statement about adversaries under the bottom and the top oracle, and a statement about an experimenter or an auditor under the two disciplines.

Remark. Three facts fix the meaning. (i) By thm-perfect-substitution the bottom oracle is the real procedure against an adversary that samples $\mathsf L$. (ii) For one fixed learning function, an adversary that knows $\mathsf L$ is an oracle algorithm that hard-wires it, so the best success under the bottom oracle equals that of an adversary that knows $\mathsf L$. The clause "reaches $\mathsf L$ only through $\mathcal O$" therefore matters only in a statement that chooses the adversary before the learning function. (iii) When the hypotheses fix every honest dataset, the top oracle names honest data with the honest seed, and a theorem with drawn honest data names the policy it uses. The drawn-seed discipline gives an experimenter what a training service that draws and hides its own randomness gives, which is the case "only what it can already compute" for that actor (orchestrator ruling after round 02).

### Model bank [def-model-bank]

A design is a finite list $\mathcal B=((x_i,\rho_i))_{i\le k}$ of training inputs with seeds, each seed fixed or marked drawn, a drawn seed having law $\mu$ independently of everything else. The bank stores $m_i=\mathsf L(x_i;\rho_i,\xi_i)$, one run each with its own hardware randomness. The empirical oracle $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal B}$ answers $\mathsf{Train}(x,s)$ with the unused entry of least index $i$ such that $x_i=x$ and either $\rho_i$ is fixed and $s=\rho_i$ is passed by value, or $\rho_i$ is drawn and $s=\bot$, in which case it also returns a name for $\rho_i$. It marks the entry used and refuses a call that no unused entry matches. Eval and Open act as in def-learning-oracle. A coupled design draws its shared seeds from $\mu$ before the bank is built and lists them as fixed.

### Isolated client [def-isolated-client]

Fix a client $j$, candidate datasets $D^0_j\neq D^1_j$, a standard Borel space $\mathcal C$ of choices, a number $R\ge1$ of rounds, a dimension $d$, a noise scale $\sigma>0$, an initial state $w_0\in\mathcal M$, a measurable map $(c,D,w)\mapsto x(c,D,w)\in\mathcal X$ and measurable release maps $f_t:\mathcal C\times\mathcal M\to\mathbb R^d$. A choice holds the starting model and everything else that the node's input takes from outside the client, the learner name of the round included. A procedure $G$ isolates client $j$ behind Gaussian releases when client $j$ owns the input node of $D_j$ and the nodes of $G$ include, for $t\le R$, a learning node $v_t$ owned by $j$ and a general node $r_t$, with $v_1\prec r_1\prec\dots\prec v_R\prec r_R$, such that the following hold.

1. $v_t$ calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D_j,W_{t-1}),\bot)$, where $W_t$ is the value of $v_t$ and $W_0=w_0$. Here $c_t$ is the value of a parent of $v_t$ other than $v_{t-1}$ and the input node of $D_j$, or a choice of the coalition.
2. No node other than $v_1,\dots,v_R$ reads $D_j$, and the children of $v_t$ are among $r_t$ and $v_{t+1}$.
3. $r_t$ outputs $y_t=O_t+f_t(c_t,W_t)+Z_t$, where $Z_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_d)$ is randomness of $r_t$ and $O_t$ is a measurable function of the other parents of $r_t$ and of randomness of $r_t$ independent of $Z_t$.
4. The coalition owns no $v_t$ and no $r_t$, and the input node of $D_j$ and every $v_t$ reach it through channel none.

The client is stateless when $x(c,D,w)$ does not depend on $w$. The procedure may stop after a release, on a value that the coalition sets, and then no later node runs. Under $H_b$, $D_j=D^b_j$ and every other input is fixed, the same under both.

The coupled execution $\mathsf{Ex}_b$ runs $G$ under $H_b$ against a given strategy and, at each $v_t$ that runs, also computes the twin $W'_t=\mathsf L(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j,W'_{t-1});\rho_t,\xi'_t)$, with $W'_0=w_0$, the seed $\rho_t$ of $v_t$ and fresh $\xi'_t$. No node reads a twin. With the maximum over the rounds that run, the exceedance and its probability are

$$D^\star_b=\max_t\big\|f_t(c_t,W'_t)-f_t(c_t,W_t)\big\|,\qquad\epsilon_b(\Delta)=\Pr\big[D^\star_b>\Delta\big].$$

For $w^0,w^1\in\mathcal M$ the coupled tail of round $t$ is

$$\beta_t(c,w^0,w^1,\Delta)=\Pr\Big[\big\|f_t\big(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^1_j,w^1);\rho,\xi_1)\big)-f_t\big(c,\mathsf L(x(c,D^0_j,w^0);\rho,\xi_0)\big)\big\|>\Delta\Big],$$

with $\rho\sim\mu$ and $\xi_0,\xi_1\sim\pi$ independent, and it is written $\beta_t(c,\Delta)$ for a stateless client.

Remarks. An experimenter that holds every input and runs the strategy samples $\mathsf{Ex}_b$. At each $v_t$ it calls $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^b_j,W_{t-1}),\bot)$, which returns a seed name $s$, and then $\mathsf{Train}(x(c_t,D^{1-b}_j,W'_{t-1}),s)$, holding both states. It also makes the calls of the other nodes and the strategy's own calls, so a run costs at most $2R$ Train calls for client $j$ plus those. Local steps between two releases form one learning function. A guarantee under this definition is for the fixed pair $(D^0_j,D^1_j)$, and thm-record-level-certificate gives the record-level forms and their limits. A passive strategy also takes the protocol's stop. For a passive strategy the law of $D^\star_b$ depends only on the choices that enter procedure nodes, so $\epsilon_b$ is the same for every passive adversary. The experimenter then needs none of the strategy's own calls. The clause on the input node of $D_j$ is what step 1 of the proof of thm-dataflow-composition (b) needs. Without it a procedure may release the dataset, and the bound of that theorem fails. The exclusion in condition 1 serves the same step. A choice $c_t=W_{t-1}$ or $c_t=D_j$ lets the release means drift apart by $t$ times the per-round gap, and the bound of thm-dataflow-composition (b) then fails (round-06/challenger.md, proven, sketch).

## Open problems

Each item is a yes-or-no question with the class of learning functions, the discipline and the accuracy named. Each names the result it would extend, what is known and the obstacle. A certifier is uniformly accurate to $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ for a target when, at every learning function of the class, its output lies in $[\theta,\theta+\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}]$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$, with a number of Train calls fixed by the stated parameters alone.

1. Sum channel, for thm-dataflow-composition. Take $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $B_A=0$ and $n_h\ge1$ further honest clients, each isolated and stateless, whose clipped updates enter $O_t$. Is there a functional $\epsilon^\Sigma_0(\Delta)$ of $\mathsf L$, defined by Train calls, such that (i) $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)\le\delta_{\mathcal N}(\varepsilon;\sqrt R\Delta/\sigma)+\epsilon^\Sigma_0(\Delta)$ for every passive adversary, (ii) $\epsilon^\Sigma_0\le\epsilon_0$ at every learning function and $\epsilon^\Sigma_0<\epsilon_0$ at some, and (iii) some coupled-discipline certifier is uniformly accurate for $\epsilon^\Sigma_0(\Delta)$ over all learning functions, with Train calls fixed by $\alpha$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$, $R$ and $n_h$? Known. $\epsilon_0$ satisfies (i) and (iii), by thm-dataflow-composition (c)(ii). Obstacle. The coupled execution shares the other clients' seeds across the hypotheses, so their contributions cancel in the exceedance. Crediting them needs a coupling that moves their seeds, the seed maps of item 8.
2. Drawn-seed exponent, for thm-dataflow-composition (e). Known (proven). Let $d\ge6$ and $J_{\mathrm{cd}}=\min\{\lfloor2^{0.18d-1}\rfloor,\lfloor e^{B^2/(16\sigma^2)}\rfloor\}$. Over $\mathfrak L_B$, a drawn-seed certifier of $\theta$ sound at level $\alpha$ that outputs below $1-(1+\gamma)e^{-B^2/(16\sigma^2)}$ at a reference function with probability above $\alpha+\eta$ needs $N$ Train calls with $N(2N-1)>\eta J_{\mathrm{cd}}$. That is about $\sqrt{\eta/2}\min\{2^{0.09d-1/2},e^{B^2/(32\sigma^2)}\}$. In every dimension and for $\alpha\le1/2$, $O(e^{B^2/\sigma^2}\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-2}\ln(1/\alpha))$ drawn-seed Train calls give a uniformly accurate certifier. Question. Whenever $B^2/\sigma^2>d$, is some drawn-seed certifier of $\theta$ uniformly accurate over $\mathfrak L_B$ with $e^{O(d)}\,\mathrm{poly}(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}},\ln(1/\alpha))$ Train calls, with a universal constant in $O(d)$? Obstacle. The estimator of (e)(iii) has a variance governed by $e^{B^2/\sigma^2}$, and an estimator of the Gaussian-smoothed divergence whose variance is governed by $d$ alone is not known for laws on a ball of radius $B\gg\sigma\sqrt d$.
3. Shadow lower bound, for thm-black-box-certifiability (b). Known. $O((C^{q_E}(\ln|U|+1)+\ln(1/\alpha))/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}^2)$ shadow runs suffice to reach the label-only optimum within $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$. Question. Is there a family of learning functions on which every shadow-model attack that reaches $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{bb}}(q_E)$ within $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$ needs at least a constant times $C^{q_E}\ln|U|/\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}^2$ Train calls? Obstacle. A lower bound for learning decision trees of depth $q_E$ over $C$ outputs, which the oracle model does not supply.
4. Multi-round drawn seeds, for thm-dataflow-composition. In $G^{\mathrm{agg}}_R$ with $B_A=0$ and clip $B<\infty$, is there a drawn-seed certifier of $\mathrm H_\gamma(P_0\|P_1)$ for the protocol's strategy that is uniformly accurate over all learning functions with a number of Train calls fixed by $R$, $B/\sigma$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $\alpha$, and free of $d$? Known. Yes for $R=1$, by (e)(iii). Obstacle. Over $R$ adaptive rounds the release law is a mixture over trajectories, and importance weights over trajectories have variance of order $e^{RB^2/\sigma^2}$ (heuristic count). The problem concerns only the bottom experimenter, so its priority is low.
5. The price at a fixed ratio, for thm-bounded-deviation-transfer. Let $N^\star$ be as in its corollary. Fix $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak e/d$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$, $\alpha$, $\alpha'$ and $\eta$, and let $\mathfrak e\to\infty$ with $\ln R=o(\mathfrak e)$. Let $\mathsf L^\circ$ be the reference function of part (d). Let $J^{\mathrm{fb}}$ be the largest number of disjoint Borel sets such that, at $\mathsf L^\circ$, some $B_A$-deviating strategy without oracle calls puts $m_R$ in each with probability at least $1-\eta/2$. Do $\ln N^\star/\mathfrak e$ and $\ln J^{\mathrm{fb}}/\mathfrak e$ have the same limit? Known (proven). $N^\star\ge(1-\alpha-\alpha')J^{\mathrm{fb}}$, by the proof of part (d), which uses only that the cells are disjoint and that some strategy reaches each. The limit of $\ln N^\star/\mathfrak e$ is one along $\mathfrak e/d\to0$, by the corollary. At every fixed $\mathfrak a$, $(1+2\sqrt{\mathfrak a})^{-2}\le\liminf\le\limsup\le1$, by part (d) with $\mathfrak c=2\sqrt{\mathfrak a}/(1+2\sqrt{\mathfrak a})$ and part (b). thm-smoothed-choice-certificate (d) gives $\ln N^\star\ge\mathfrak e/4-O(1)$ for $\mathfrak a\le2/5$ and $\ln N^\star\ge(d/2)\ln(\mathfrak a\eta/4)-O(1)$ for every $\mathfrak a$. Known (heuristic). By Shannon's coding theorem, the limit of $\ln J^{\mathrm{fb}}/\mathfrak e$ is the capacity per unit of $\mathfrak e$ of the channel from the coalition's inputs to $m_R$ with the releases fed back. The coalition sees each release, so it can cancel noise that it has seen. Feedback does not raise the capacity of a memoryless channel. Here it helps because the decoder sees only the final sum $m_R$, while the coalition sees every release (round-06/challenger.md). Without feedback that capacity is $\ln(1+2\mathfrak a)/(2\mathfrak a)$. At $R=3$ a code that cancels $Z_1$ in the second input reaches $\ln(1+(\sqrt{\mathfrak a}+\sqrt{\mathfrak a-1})^2)/(2\mathfrak a)$, which is larger for $\mathfrak a>(1+\sqrt2)/2$ (round-05/challenger.md). As $R\to\infty$, a code that steers $m_R$ toward its codeword with inputs of norm $B_A$ beats the value without feedback at every ratio computed. It reaches about $0.60$ against $0.275$ at $\mathfrak a=4$ and $0.93$ against $0.55$ at $\mathfrak a=1$ (round-06/builder.md). Obstacle. The transfer pays the whole Rényi moment of the path, and the needles pay only for the cells that one decoder separates. Feedback lets the cells shrink below the noise of $m_R$, and no certifier is known that pays less than $e^{\mathfrak e}$ when the cells cannot be nearly orthogonal.
6. Threshold CKKS, for thm-dataflow-composition (a). (i) Does threshold CKKS with Gaussian smudging realize $\mathcal F$ against a coalition that follows $\Sigma$, under RLWE, with $\epsilon_\Sigma(\mathsf t)$ negligible in the security parameter for every polynomial $\mathsf t$? (ii) Does it realize $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{dev}}_{\mathcal Y}$ with the abort set against a deviating coalition, under RLWE, with negligible $\epsilon^{\mathrm{dev}}_\Sigma(\mathsf t)$, when it has six additions? They are a trusted key setup, proofs of plaintext knowledge for the inputs (with a Euclidean norm bound when thm-bounded-deviation-transfer is wanted), a broadcast of the input ciphertexts and the decryption shares, honest decryptors that recompute the aggregate and decrypt only it once per round, proofs of correct partial decryption, and agreement of the honest parties on the delivered value. Obstacle. (i) needs a simulator for partial decryptions with smudging that survives the key-recovery attacks of Li and Micciancio. (ii) also needs proofs of correct partial decryption for CKKS, and the search of round 03 found active security only for thresholdized BFV, BGV and FHEW.
7. The gap between $\tau$ and $\tau/2$, for thm-replay-audit-steer. Fix hardware randomness, a learning function, a closed target $T$ and $q_{\mathsf{RO}}$. Does some prover with $q_{\mathsf{RO}}$ queries to the random oracle always win with probability at least a universal constant times $\min\{1,q_{\mathsf{RO}}p_\tau(T)\}$? Known. Yes under deterministic kernels, with the constant $1-e^{-1}$. Obstacle. An attack that beats the threshold $\tau/2$ must predict where the auditor's replay lands, which depends on the joint law of run and replay near $T$ beyond the joint reach.
8. Seed maps, for thm-coupling-certificate (c)(ii). (i) Under deterministic kernels, with a deterministic channel $\kappa$ and atomless view laws, is $\inf_\psi\Pr[\kappa(W^\Gamma_0)\neq\kappa(W^\psi_1)]=\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$, the infimum over measurable $\psi$ with $\psi_*\mu=\mu$? (ii) Is there a certifier with explicit seeds that is uniformly accurate for $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ over all learning functions with deterministic kernels and atomless view laws, with Train calls fixed by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $\alpha$? The challenger of round 03 expects no for (ii), by a needle argument (heuristic). Obstacle. The finite construction glues measure isomorphisms between the level sets of a finite view, which does not extend to a continuum of level sets.
9. Finite seeds. Only thm-reference-access (c) is proven on an $n_{\mathrm{bit}}$-bit seed space. For each impossibility proven with an atomless seed (lem-atomless-two-point, thm-dataflow-composition (e), thm-record-level-certificate (e), thm-smoothed-choice-certificate (e), thm-bounded-deviation-transfer (e)), is there an additive term $\epsilon(n_{\mathrm{bit}},N)$ that tends to zero as $n_{\mathrm{bit}}\to\infty$ at fixed $N$, such that the statement holds on an $n_{\mathrm{bit}}$-bit seed space with that term? Obstacle. On $2^{n_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ seeds each conditional law must be approximated, with an error that depends on how the partitions of the proofs meet the prefixes of the seed.

Dropped. The Poisson-subsampling question of thm-bank-soundness (d). Subsampling is a node of the procedure, and no theorem of this theory uses it, so the question is out of scope. Closed in round 04. Active certification over continuous reachable choices, by thm-smoothed-choice-certificate. Closed in round 05. The constant of the price while $\mathfrak e\ll d$, by thm-bounded-deviation-transfer.
