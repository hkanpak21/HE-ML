# The steering attacker, round 04, towards the idea

The verdict first. From this aspect the idea has worked when one theorem holds for a declared trainer on real data. An attacker that acts, and must stay stealthy to an auditor who reads the trained model, can move the model's prediction at any input by at most the trainer's own uncertainty at that input, times a function of the stealth level. The trainer's uncertainty is the spread of the honest model over seeds, which the oracle measures by drawn runs, so the bound is in the trainer's own units and shrinks exactly where the trainer has learned. Hardness then comes from learning and from nothing planted, since the only place a stealthy backdoor can live is where the trainer is ignorant. Clause 14 (c) of the final statement is this theorem for the linear model. The statement to prove below carries it to every trainer whose honest law is Gaussian in function space, which covers linearized networks, and names the learning attacker's price for reaching the cap. The experiment runs a real network across the linearized and the feature-learning regimes and refutes the statement if a stealthy poison moves the trigger by more honest standard deviations than its trace in the model law allows.

# 1. What would count as the idea having worked

Halil's idea, read from this aspect, has four parts. A learning-constrained attacker should meet a bound near the cost of learning the main task, and the views he names include a signal the attacker plants and later observes (`halil-ideas.md` item 1). The oracle should count what the attacker asked for, runs, seeds and records (item 7). A result counts when the task becomes hard because of learning and not because of something planted (item 9). The result should be realized on models in use, across architectures and sizes, with what the attack needed stated (item 11), against the class practitioners run, which reads the model's outputs and its representation (item 12).

The theorem whose proof would show all four, stated exactly. Fix a declared trainer, a dataset $D$ of $n$ records, and a model $f(x;\theta)$. Write $P^\circ$ for the law of the honest final model over the trainer's randomness and $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ for the standard deviation of $f(x;\theta)$ under $P^\circ$, the trainer's uncertainty at $x$. Let an attacker act through any shift of clause 7, with any write budget, and let it be stealthy at level $\delta$ against every auditor of the final model. Then for every input $x$,
$$\big\lvert\mathbb E_{P'}f(x;\theta)-\mathbb E_{P^\circ}f(x;\theta)\big\rvert\le m(\delta)\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x),\qquad m(\delta)=2\Phi^{-1}\big((1+\delta)/2\big),$$
with equality attained by one shift, and an attacker that does not hold the trainer's tangent features at $x$ reaches a fraction $1-\epsilon$ of the cap only after $N\ge c\,p_{\mathrm{eff}}/\epsilon$ shadow runs, $p_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the effective dimension of the trainer's uncertainty.

Why this is the idea. The right side is the trainer's performance at $x$, in the trainer's units, which falls as $1/\sqrt n$ where the trainer has data and stays large where it has none, so the attacker's achievable steer is capped by what the trainer has not yet learned, which is item 1 in a form that is true. The shadow runs are what the oracle counts, item 7. Nothing is planted, and the hardness is the trainer's learning, item 9. Both sides are measured on a real model, item 11, and the auditor reads the outputs, item 12. The trigger of a backdoor must then sit where the trainer is uncertain, and the defence that follows, a test of the model's uncertainty at suspicious inputs, is a constraint honest parties already meet.

# 2. What the tools reach now, and what blocks the rest

Reached.

- The cap for the linear model with label poisoning, both auditors, with the trajectory auditor worth $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$ (proven, clause 14, `prop-label-poison-stealth-response`).
- The shift as one object for records, updates and values in transit, and the chain bound that prices its stealth from above, $\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{T\bar\kappa/(4\varsigma^2)}$ with $\bar\kappa$ measured along the honest trajectory (proven, clauses 7 and 10).
- The honest spread $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ from drawn runs and the response $D_T$ from coupled runs, both for every learning function (proven, clause 21, item 3).
- The public-target cap, success at most the honest rate plus $\delta$, for any auditor who evaluates the target event (proven, clause 22).
- The Bayesian reading. For noisy gradient descent on a quadratic with Hessian $H$ and weight decay $\lambda$, the stationary law is $\mathcal N(\theta^*,(\eta\varsigma^2/2)(H_\lambda(I-\eta H_\lambda/2))^{-1})$, and the posterior of the linear model with noise $s$ and prior $\tau^2I$ is $\mathcal N(\hat\theta,(s^2/n)H_\lambda^{-1})$ with $\lambda=s^2/(n\tau^2)$. At $\eta\varsigma^2/2=s^2/n$ the two agree up to the factor $(I-\eta H_\lambda/2)^{-1}$, so $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ is the posterior predictive standard deviation at $x$ (proven, two displays).
- The optimal stealthy shift. In the linear model the shift that attains the cap at $x$ is $\Delta^*=\Sigma_TR_T^{-1}\varphi(x)$, and per eigenvalue of $H_\lambda$ the operator $\Sigma_TR_T^{-1}$ is $\eta\varsigma^2(1+r^T)/(1+r)$, within a factor two of a constant, so $\Delta^*$ lies within a well-conditioned map of the feature vector $\varphi(x)$ of the target input (proven, the two eigenvalue formulas of round 01). A poisoner attains the cap by planting records whose aggregate feature shift points along the trigger's features, which is what deployed backdoor poisons do (empirical, from memory, not in the library).
- The secret-trigger version, $d^{1/4}\sqrt\delta$ for an isotropic frame (heuristic, clause 14).

Blocked.

- Gaussianity in function space. The cap uses that the honest and steered laws are Gaussian with one covariance, which holds when the model is linear in $\theta$ and the shift is constant. A trained network is linear in $\theta$ only in the linearized regime, wide width and short training, where the tangent features $\varphi(x)=\nabla_\theta f(x;\theta_0)$ stay fixed (Jacot, Gabriel and Hongler, NeurIPS 2018, and Lee and coauthors, NeurIPS 2019, from memory, unverified, not in the library). Under feature learning the law is not Gaussian and the shift of a poisoned record depends on the state (open).
- Mean against total variation. Without Gaussianity, a small total variation does not bound a mean shift relative to a spread, since a mass $\delta$ placed far away moves the mean by $\delta$ times the distance. The general cap must be stated for a quantile of the response or under a tail condition on $P^\circ$ (open).
- The auditor's baseline on real data. The cap is against an auditor who knows $P^\circ$. In federated learning the server has no honest dataset, so its baseline is the spread across the other clients, which is $P^\circ$ only when clients are exchangeable (heuristic). In an experiment the oracle supplies $P^\circ$ by drawn runs at the shadow-run price (proven, item 11).
- The secret trigger beyond isotropy, where $d$ must become an effective dimension of $\Sigma_T$, the participation ratio $(\mathrm{tr}\Sigma_T)^2/\mathrm{tr}\Sigma_T^2$ (heuristic).
- The response constant of real training, which the cap does not need but the write-budget price does (open, clause 18).
- The learning attacker's approach rate. Its constant rests on covariance concentration over $N$ shadow runs (`fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration`, proven) and on the conditioning of $R_T^{-1}$, which is bounded for $\lambda>0$ (proven) and open at $\lambda=0$ with $p>n$.

# 3. One statement to prove

### thm-stealth-uncertainty-cap-linearized: a stealthy steer moves a linearized trainer only where it is uncertain

status: open
uses: def-steering-attacker-price, prop-label-poison-stealth-response, thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter, thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views, fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration

Setting. A model $f(x;\theta)=f(x;\theta_0)+\langle\theta-\theta_0,\varphi(x)\rangle$ with fixed features $\varphi:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^p$, which is a linear model, a random-feature model or a network in its linearized regime. A dataset $D$ of $n$ pairs, the square loss with weight decay $\lambda\ge0$, Hessian $H_\lambda=\frac1n\sum_i\varphi(x_i)\varphi(x_i)^\top+\lambda I$ with $\mu=\lambda_{\min}(H_\lambda)>0$, and $T$ steps of noisy gradient descent at step $\eta\le1/\lambda_{\max}(H_\lambda)$ and noise $\varsigma>0$. A steering attacker of clause 7 whose shift is constant along the trajectory, which label poisoning gives with $\Delta=\frac1n\sum_{i\in S}(y'_i-y_i)\varphi(x_i)$, and which an update sender or a party in the middle gives when its alteration does not read the state. Write $\Sigma_T$ for the honest covariance of $\theta_T$, $R_T=\eta\sum_{t<T}(I-\eta H_\lambda)^t$, $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)^2=\varphi(x)^\top\Sigma_T\varphi(x)$, and $m(\delta)=2\Phi^{-1}((1+\delta)/2)$.

Statement.

(a) The honest and the steered final models are Gaussian with one covariance $\Sigma_T$ and means $R_T\Delta$ apart, and for every input $x$, under stealth $\delta$ against every auditor of the final model,
$$\lvert\mathbb E_{P'}f(x;\theta_T)-\mathbb E_{P^\circ}f(x;\theta_T)\rvert\le m(\delta)\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x),$$
and under stealth $\delta$ against every auditor of the trajectory the right side carries the further factor $\sqrt{2/(\eta\mu T)}$.

(b) Equality at $x$ holds for the shift $\Delta^*=c\,\Sigma_TR_T^{-1}\varphi(x)$, and $\Sigma_TR_T^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $\eta\varsigma^2(1+r^T)/(1+r)$ with $r=1-\eta\lambda_j$, all within the interval $[\eta\varsigma^2/2,\eta\varsigma^2]$, so the attaining shift lies within a factor two in every eigendirection of $c'\varphi(x)$.

(c) An attacker that does not hold $\varphi$ and estimates $\Sigma_T\varphi(x)$ from the empirical covariance of $(f(x_1),\dots,f(x_n),f(x))$ over $N$ drawn shadow runs reaches the fraction $1-C\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{eff}}/N}$ of the cap at $x$, with $p_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\mathrm{tr}\Sigma_T)^2/\mathrm{tr}\Sigma_T^2$ and $C$ depending on $\eta\lambda_{\max}(H_\lambda)$ alone.

(d) At $\eta\varsigma^2/2=s^2/n$ the honest law is the Bayesian posterior of the linear model up to the factor $(I-\eta H_\lambda/2)^{-1}$, so (a) reads that a stealthy steer moves the prediction at $x$ by at most $m(\delta)$ posterior standard deviations.

Plan of the proof. Step 1, the two chains are affine in $\theta$ with the same matrix $I-\eta H_\lambda$ and the same noise, so both laws are Gaussian with covariance $\Sigma_T$ and the means differ by $R_T\Delta$ (proven, the induction of round 01 with $H_\lambda$ in place of $A$). Step 2, total variation of two Gaussians with one covariance is $2\Phi(m/2)-1$ with $m$ the Mahalanobis distance, and the trajectory law is Markov with per-step Kullback divergence $\lVert\Delta\rVert^2/(2\varsigma^2)$ (proven, round 01). Step 3, Cauchy and Schwarz in the $\Sigma_T$ inner product give $\lvert\langle R_T\Delta,\varphi(x)\rangle\rvert\le m_T\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$, and $m_T\le m(\delta)$ under stealth (proven). Step 4, the per-eigenvalue bound $m_T^2\le(2/(\eta\mu T))m_{\mathrm{traj}}^2$ (proven, round 01). Step 5, equality in Cauchy and Schwarz at $\Sigma_T^{-1/2}R_T\Delta\parallel\Sigma_T^{1/2}\varphi(x)$ gives $\Delta^*$, and the eigenvalues of $\Sigma_TR_T^{-1}$ follow from the two formulas of round 01 (proven, with the interval by $r\in[0,1)$). Step 6, the empirical covariance of the honest predictions over $N$ runs estimates $\Phi\Sigma_T\Phi^\top$ with relative error $C\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{eff}}/N}$ in operator norm (proven given `fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration`), and the loss in the cap is the sine of the angle between the estimated and the true $\Sigma_T^{1/2}\varphi(x)$ (proven, a perturbation bound), with the constant through the conditioning of $R_T^{-1}$ on the span of the features, bounded by $\lambda_{\max}(H_\lambda)$ (proven for $\lambda>0$). Step 7, the two covariance displays of section 2 (proven).

The step I expect to fail first. Step 6, in its constant. The attacker observes predictions at the training inputs and at $x$, so it estimates $\Sigma_T$ on the span of $\{\varphi(x_i)\}$ and $\varphi(x)$ only, and when $\varphi(x)$ has a component outside the span of the training features, as an out-of-distribution trigger does, that component of $\Sigma_T\varphi(x)$ is seen through no honest prediction and the estimate misses it. The fraction of the cap then depends on how much of $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)^2$ lies outside the training span, which is exactly the part a backdoor exploits. I expect the clean rate to hold for in-span inputs and to need a second term for out-of-span ones, and the theorem may need to state the cap reached as a function of that split. The transfer to feature learning is the step that fails after the theorem is proven, and the experiment is built to find where.

# 4. One experiment the learning oracle can run

What it measures. The ratio $\varrho=\lvert\bar f'(\tau)-\bar f^\circ(\tau)\rvert/(m(\overline{\mathrm{TV}})\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(\tau))$ at a trigger $\tau$, where $\bar f'$ and $\bar f^\circ$ are the mean predictions over drawn poisoned and honest runs, $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(\tau)$ the honest spread, and $\overline{\mathrm{TV}}=\sqrt{T\bar\kappa/(4\varsigma^2)}$ the chain bound with $\bar\kappa$ the mean squared shift of the poisoned update measured along the honest trajectories (clause 10). Since $\overline{\mathrm{TV}}$ is an upper bound on the stealth level against every auditor, the statement predicts $\varrho\le1$ wherever it holds. Beside it the experiment records a lower bound on the stealth level, the advantage $\hat\delta$ of a classifier two-sample test on the final models, and the response read from coupled pairs.

Declared parameters. Data, MNIST digits 3 against 8 as a regression to $\pm1$, $d=784$, $n=4000$ records. Model, a two-layer ReLU network of width $w\in\{64,4096\}$ with a scalar output, the small width the feature-learning regime and the large width the linearized one, and beside them the linearized model with features $\varphi(x)=\nabla_\theta f(x;\theta_0)$ frozen at initialization as the control on which the theorem is proven. Trainer, full-batch gradient descent with injected Gaussian noise $\varsigma$ on the gradient, weight decay $\lambda=10^{-3}$, step $\eta=0.1$, $T\in\{500,5000\}$ steps, and $\varsigma$ set so that $\overline{\mathrm{TV}}\in\{0.05,0.2\}$ at each write budget. Trigger, an out-of-distribution input, a fixed random pattern of the input norm, and an in-distribution input, a held-out digit, for contrast. Runs, $N=200$ drawn runs on each side at each cell, and 20 coupled pairs per cell.

The attacker it runs. A label poisoner with write budget $k\in\{40,400\}$ that chooses its $k$ records and their new labels so that the aggregate feature shift $\Delta=\frac1n\sum_{i\in S}(y'_i-y_i)\varphi(x_i)$ points along $\varphi(\tau)$ at initialization, the attaining direction of (b), by solving the least-squares problem for the label changes on the $k$ records whose features correlate most with $\varphi(\tau)$, scaled to a deviation budget $B_A$ that sets $\overline{\mathrm{TV}}$. In the second arm the attacker holds no features and estimates the direction from $N'\in\{20,200\}$ shadow runs, as in (c), and the experiment records the fraction of the cap it reaches against $\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{eff}}/N'}$.

The outcome that refutes the statement. On the linearized control, $\varrho>1$ beyond the bootstrap band over the 200 runs at any cell, which is impossible if steps 1 to 5 are right, so it would mean an error in the proof or in the measurement of $\bar\kappa$. On the networks the statement is not proven, and $\varrho>1$ at the large width refutes its transfer to the linearized regime of a real network, which is the claim of section 1 at the scale the theorem reaches. The expected pattern, and not a refutation, is $\varrho\le1$ at width 4096 and $\varrho>1$ at width 64 after the longer training, which would locate the loss of the cap at feature learning and give the response constant of clause 18 its first measured value, as the ratio of the coupled displacement to $B_A$. A second refutation, of (c), is a learning attacker that reaches more than $1-C\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{eff}}/N'}$ of the cap at the out-of-distribution trigger from $N'=20$ runs, which would show that the out-of-span component of section 3 costs nothing. A third outcome is not a refutation but the end of the line. If at both widths $\varrho\ll1$ for the attaining poison, the cap is loose on real networks and the right side of the theorem must be sharpened before it is a price.
