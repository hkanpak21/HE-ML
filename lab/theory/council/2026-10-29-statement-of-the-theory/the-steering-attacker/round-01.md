# The steering attacker

The question of this aspect. How is an attacker that acts, by poisoning, planting or altering values in transit, priced, and does attacker-planted structure escape the planting objection?

The verdict in one paragraph. An attacker that acts is one object, an additive shift on the trainer's update stream, whatever it sets. Its stealth against an auditor who watches that stream is already priced in the booklet, by the push-to-noise budget of `thm-bounded-deviation-transfer`, and its effect is the trainer's response to the shift, a stability constant that plants nothing and that the oracle measures by coupled runs. A backdoor and a watermark are channels an attacker or a designer fixes, and the party that learns in them is the auditor, so Halil's conjecture of a learning price for them attaches to the wrong side. Attacker-planted structure escapes the planting objection, since the planting is a move of the game, but what it buys is an attack result, an undetectability theorem, never the defence theorem Halil wants. The published undetectable backdoors plant in the training coins or a frozen layer, where no response of training is involved, and they price nothing about learning. One exact instance, label poisoning of noisy gradient descent on a linear model, shows the whole shape. Stealth caps the trigger response at a few noise standard deviations when the direction is known, at order $d^{1/4}$ noise standard deviations when the attacker keeps it secret, and a trajectory auditor regains a factor $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$ over a final-model auditor.

# 1. Halil's idea

Terms, defined before use. A procedure is a dataflow graph whose learning nodes call $\mathsf{Train}$ and whose evaluation nodes call $\mathsf{Eval}$ (`def-learning-oracle-model`, Definition 3 of the definitions reading). A party's view is what reaches it through the channels of the procedure. A party's choices are the node arguments and node outputs it sets (`def-interface-choices`), of three kinds that matter here: a data argument, meaning records it inserts or alters, a model argument, meaning an update it sends in place of the prescribed one, and a value in transit, meaning an activation or a gradient it alters on an edge. A passive attacker has no choices beyond queries. A steering attacker has choices and the steer goal, which it meets when a node's value lands in a target set it fixed (`def-steer-goal`). Stealth is a distinguish game played by a second party, the auditor, who must not tell the steered execution from the honest one on a stated view (`def-steer-goal`, last clause, and `def-distinguish-goal`). A backdoor is a steering attacker whose target set holds the models that satisfy a predicate on queries carrying a trigger, a secret of the attacker. A watermark is a designer's steer of the model's output law so that a key holder detects it from outputs.

Halil's idea has four parts.

First, breadth. The attacker's choices belong to the setting. It can change training data, change activations in transit as a party in the middle of U-shaped split learning does, and poison models, and the attack model follows the scenario, with poisoning and model poisoning among them (`halil-ideas.md` item 3). The booklet's Definition 3 already admits the steer goal, so the frame is his.

Second, planted signals as channels. Among the views he wants bounded are a signal an attacker plants and later observes, a backdoor, and a watermark (item 1). For a learning-constrained attacker on such a view he conjectures a bound near the cost of learning the main task (item 1).

Third, the planting objection and its exception. Hardness planted by the theorist in synthetic Gaussian data, or carried by a frozen random layer holding a key, is borrowed and ugly (item 4, and `2026-10-27-what-you-want.md` section 2). If structure is planted in one world and not in the other and the attacker sees only labels or activations, training in between is post-processing, it only makes the task harder and adds nothing to what is known, so a worthwhile result is a task that becomes hard because of learning (item 9). The exception he grants is that a designer who hides a direction in a released model achieves something real (item 4, last sentence).

Fourth, units and honest behaviour. A steering attacker's cost has a coordinate the passive one lacks, the records or values it sets, beside queries, floating-point operations and seeds times datasets (item 8). Asking honest parties to plant structure is poison without a theorem, while honest noise is acceptable (item 10). Item 10 belongs to the aspect `defence-as-honest-constraint`, and this file touches it only where the honest noise is what gives stealth a price.

The idea in one sentence, as he would want it stated. Every attack on a learning procedure is a party with a view and choices, the steering attacker sets records, updates or values in transit so that the trained model lands in a target set while the honest parties' checks stay quiet, its price is the budget of choices and queries that achieves this, and when it plants, the planted structure is the attack and not a borrowed assumption.

# 2. Critique

## 2.1 A backdoor and a watermark are channels, and the learner in them sits on the other side

In a backdoor the attacker knows the trigger and learns nothing at inference. The party that must learn is the defender, who sees a model and an input and must decide whether the input triggers the model. Christiano, Hilton, Lecomte and Xu, ITCS 2025 (CHLX25, library note full-read), pose exactly this in Definition 3.1. Theorem 4.1 prices it statistically, with best confidence $\max\{1/2,1-\Theta(\mathrm{VC}(F)\varepsilon)\}$. Corollary 5.2 shows that efficient PAC learnability of the class implies efficient defendability, and Theorem 5.5 shows that polynomial-size circuits are not efficiently defendable under one-way functions and indistinguishability obfuscation. So the cost of learning the main task, in item 1, attaches to the defender, and there only as an upper bound, since learnable implies defendable, with no universal multiplier the other way (`fact-chlx25-defendability-characterization`, correction of 2026-10-26).

In a watermark the party that would learn is the user who detects or removes it without the key. Christ, Gunn and Zamir, COLT 2024 (CGZ24), Lemma 4, show that every sound watermark is detected by an unbounded user with polynomially many queries and time exponential in the key length, and undetectability for polynomial time rests on one-way functions (`fact-cgz24-unbounded-attacker-detects-watermarks`). The price is the key length, a cryptographic plug-in of the excluded kind, and it holds no unit of learning.

Item 1's conjecture is therefore ill-posed for the backdoor, since no learner sits on the attacker's side, and false for the watermark, since the price is the key.

## 2.2 Training is post-processing for one auditor and not for the others

The claim of item 9 is right for a distinguish goal when the auditor holds the data. The model is a function of the data, so the auditor's advantage from the model is at most its advantage from the data (data processing, proven, elementary). It fails in three ways.

The model-level auditor does not hold the data. Its price is the detection price of the planted structure through the trainer's filter, and this is the task that becomes hard because of learning, which the theory already has. Item 18 (a), `thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter`, bounds the trajectory's total variation from the null by $\sqrt{T\bar\kappa_f/(4\varsigma^2)}$, where $\bar\kappa_f$ is the mean squared shift of the per-example update along the null trajectory, and item 26 bounds every attacker of a statistical-query trainer's output by the statistical-query hardness of the test.

For the steer goal, training is the mechanism and not the post-processing. Planted records act only through the trainer's response to them, so steer success is a learning-theoretic quantity, the trainer's sensitivity to the records it was given.

The published undetectable backdoors plant nowhere in the data. Goldwasser, Kim, Vaikuntanathan and Zamir, FOCS 2022 (GKVZ22), Theorem 6.4, draw the random Fourier features from sparse Gaussian pancakes, a change of the training coins (`fact-gkvz22-secret-in-training-coins`). Bogdanov, Rosen and Vafa, ICML 2026 (BRV26), Theorem 2 with Theorem 5, plant the frozen compressing Gaussian layer. There is no training between the planting and the auditor, so post-processing does not describe them. His word for them, ugly, is right for a sharper reason than the one he gives. The planted law is not a response of training to anything, so these constructions carry no statement about learning. The sparse-PCA version of GKVZ22, Appendix A, Claim A.5, also fails at its printed parameters (`lem-sparse-pca-backdoor-width-price`).

## 2.3 The planting objection does not reach attacker-planted structure, and the escape yields attack results only

The objection of item 4 and of section 2 of the aim reading targets a defence theorem, a lower bound on the attacker, whose hardness is a problem the theorist planted in the data. When the attacker plants, the hardness is a lower bound on the auditor, which is an upper bound on the defence. That is an attack result, an undetectability theorem. The admissibility rule of `CONTEXT.md` (instructions of 2026-10-27) concerns the designer's modifications and is silent on the attacker's choices, which are moves of the game. So attacker-planted structure escapes the objection, legitimately, and the price it yields is the auditor's detection price for the planted law against the auditor's class. Feldman, Grigorescu, Reyzin, Vempala and Xiao, J. ACM 2017 (FGRVX17), Theorem 2.9, give $n^{\Omega(\log n)}$ queries to VSTAT$(n^{2-\delta}/k^2)$ for planted clique (`fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound`). Damian, Pillaud-Vivien, Lee and Bruna, COLT 2024 (DPLB24), Theorem 3.2 through Proposition 3.7, give $d^{k^*/2}$ for a planted direction with generative exponent $k^*$ (item 27). None of this gives the defence theorem Halil wants. A defence theorem on steering must bound the steerer, and the only currency the theory holds for that is stealth against an auditor together with the trainer's response.

## 2.4 The steering results already in the theory sit on the certifier's side

Three results concern an attacker that acts. Item 6, `thm-bounded-deviation-transfer`, says that a coalition whose additive inputs to the releases stay within $B_A$ of the prescribed ones raises the exceedance probability by a fixed function of the push-to-noise budget $\mathfrak e=(R-1)B_A^2/(2\sigma^2)$, and certifying it costs about $e^{\mathfrak e}$ runs, matched by a lower bound. `thm-active-choice-dichotomy` says that certifying against a coalition with admissible choices costs runs linear in the number of choices and is impossible for infinitely many. Item 9, `thm-replay-audit-steer`, says that a grinding prover passes an audit with probability at most $(q+1)n_{\mathrm{rep}}$ times the honest rate, through a random-oracle plug-in. None prices the steerer's success as a function of what it sets, and none has a learning-constrained steerer.

The literature recorded in the registers has the same gap. The null search `fact-no-sq-bound-against-poisoning` of 2026-10-13 found no statistical-query or low-degree lower bound against a poisoner. The certified-defence cluster states its bounds as a radius, a poisoned-sample count or an interface restriction (`fact-agentic-certified-cluster-no-bounded-adversary`). The one general poisoning theorem recorded is an attack. Mahloujifar, Mahmoody and Mohammed, ICML 2019 (MMM19), raise a bad event's probability from $\mu$ to $\mu^{1-pk/m}$ (`fact-kp-poisoning-multiplicative`, pointer the abstract, library note a sweep). Steinhardt, Koh and Liang, NeurIPS 2017 (SKL17), bound the loss under poisoning by a certificate over a feasible set of poisons (from memory, unverified, library note a sweep).

## 2.5 Where he is right and the theory has not used it

The frame of Definition 3 holds the steer goal, and the adversary's budget $(q_T,q_E)$ holds no coordinate for what it sets. The theory has never priced steer success.

The party in the middle of U-shaped split learning is steering as a means to inversion. Pasquini, Ateniese and Bernaschi, ACM CCS 2021 (PAB21, in the library as a sweep note, content from memory, unverified), have the server send crafted gradients that steer the client's encoder toward an invertible map and then invert the activations. Here the target set, the invertible encoders, is public to the client, who holds the encoder and tests it by training a decoder on its own records. The defence costs the client one $\mathsf{Train}$ call of the attacker's own class, a same-units statement of the kind he wants, and nothing in the theory states it.

His criterion, hard because of learning, is the right one for a defence theorem on steering, and the trainer's filter meets it. The stealth bound of item 18 depends on the shift the records cause in the trainer's update, not on the records.

# 3. A better formulation

Three objects. A definition that makes the steering attacker one object, an exact instance, and the secret-target clause.

### def-steering-attacker-price: the steering attacker, its shift and its price

status: open
uses: def-learning-oracle-model, def-interface-choices, def-steer-goal, def-distinguish-goal, def-certifier, thm-bounded-deviation-transfer

Fix a procedure in which a trainer runs $T$ steps $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t(g_t+\varsigma\xi_t)$, with $g_t$ the honest update at step $t$, $\xi_t\sim\mathcal N(0,I_p)$ independent, and $\varsigma>0$ the trainer's noise. A steering attacker is an adversary of Definition 3 whose choices are nonempty. Its three kinds of choice act on the update stream as one object. Setting $k$ of the $n$ records changes $g_t$ by $v_t=\nabla L_{D'}(\theta_t)-\nabla L_D(\theta_t)$. Sending an update changes $g_t$ by the difference from the prescribed one. Altering a value in transit changes $g_t$ by the Jacobian of the receiving node applied to the alteration. The shift of the attacker is the sequence $(v_t)_{t<T}$. Its write budget is $k$, its deviation budget is $B_A=\max_t\lVert v_t\rVert$, and its push-to-noise budget is $\mathfrak e=\sum_{t<T}\lVert v_t\rVert^2/(2\varsigma^2)\le TB_A^2/(2\varsigma^2)$, the quantity of `thm-bounded-deviation-transfer` with the update stream as the release.

The target set $\Gamma$ is a set of models. It is public when the auditor's class contains a member that evaluates the indicator of $\Gamma$ on its view, and secret otherwise. A backdoor's target set $\Gamma_\tau$, fixed by a trigger $\tau$ the auditor does not hold, is secret. The auditor is a distinguisher of class $\mathcal A$ with budget $\beta_{\mathrm{aud}}$ of reference samples, $\mathsf{Train}$ calls and queries, between the honest execution and the steered one, on a stated view: the trajectory $(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_T)$, the final model $\theta_T$, or outputs on honest queries. Success is $s=\Pr[\theta'_T\in\Gamma]$ under the steered execution and the honest rate is $s^\circ=\Pr[\theta_T\in\Gamma]$ under the honest one. Stealth $\delta$ means every auditor of $(\mathcal A,\beta_{\mathrm{aud}})$ has advantage at most $\delta$.

The steering price at $(s,\delta,\mathcal A,\beta_{\mathrm{aud}})$ over an instance class is the set of minimal budgets $(k,B_A,q_T,q_E)$ at which some steering attacker reaches success at least $s$ on every instance with stealth $\delta$. The response of the trainer is $D_T=\theta'_T-\theta_T$ under coupled runs, meaning the same seed and the same $\xi$, and the response constant $S$ is the least number with $\lVert D_T\rVert\le S\,B_A$ over the admissible shifts. The response constant is a stability constant of the trainer. Under $\mu$-strong convexity and $\eta\le1/L$ it is at most $1/\mu$, and under $L$-smoothness alone it is at most $((1+\eta L)^T-1)/L$ (proven, by the induction in the proof of the next object). For real training it is open.

Measurement. The write budget, the deviation budget, $T$, $\eta$ and $\varsigma$ are read off the procedure, and $\mathfrak e$ is computed from the shift. Success and the honest rate are frequencies over drawn runs. The response and the response constant are read off coupled runs, which need seed names (item 3). The auditor's advantage is bounded below by a classifier two-sample test on drawn runs of its view and above by the coupled certificate of item 3 when the view is a deterministic function of the model.

The other regime. The price is stated for every auditor of a class. Against the class of all auditors, stealth is total variation and the bounds below are statistical. Against a restricted class, statistical queries or a statistic, stealth is weaker and permits a larger shift, and the difference is the auditor's class price, open. Without the oracle the auditor cannot know the honest rate or the honest spread, and stealth has no baseline. With it, drawn runs supply both.

### prop-label-poison-stealth-response: label poisoning of noisy gradient descent, stealth caps the response

status: sketch
uses: def-steering-attacker-price, thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter, thm-bounded-deviation-transfer

Let the trainer run $T$ steps of full-batch noisy gradient descent on the square loss of a linear model over a dataset $D$ of $n$ pairs $(x_i,y_i)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R$:
$$\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\,(A\theta_t-b+\varsigma\xi_t),\qquad A=\tfrac1n\sum_i x_ix_i^\top,\qquad b=\tfrac1n\sum_i y_ix_i,$$
with $\eta\le1/\lambda_{\max}(A)$, $\mu=\lambda_{\min}(A)>0$ and $\theta_0$ fixed. A label poisoner sets the labels of $k$ records, which gives $D'$ with the same $A$ and a new $b'$. Write $\Delta=b'-b$, so $\lVert\Delta\rVert\le\frac1n\sum_{i\in S}\lvert y'_i-y_i\rvert\,\lVert x_i\rVert$ and the shift is $v_t=-\Delta$ at every step. Let $\Phi$ be the standard normal distribution function and $m(\delta)=2\Phi^{-1}((1+\delta)/2)$, so $m(\delta)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\delta+O(\delta^3)$. Let $\Sigma_T$ be the covariance of $\theta_T$ under the honest execution and $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)=\sqrt{x^\top\Sigma_Tx}$ the standard deviation of the honest prediction at $x$ over the training noise.

(a) Both views are Gaussian with equal covariance under the two executions, and the best auditor's advantage is $2\Phi(m/2)-1$, with
$$m_{\mathrm{traj}}^2=\frac{T\lVert\Delta\rVert^2}{\varsigma^2},\qquad m_T^2=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{\Delta_\lambda^2}{\varsigma^2}\cdot\frac{(1-r_\lambda^T)(2-\eta\lambda)}{\eta\lambda\,(1+r_\lambda^T)},$$
the sum over the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $A$, with $r_\lambda=1-\eta\lambda$ and $\Delta_\lambda$ the component of $\Delta$ along the eigenvector.

(b) $m_T^2\le\dfrac{2}{\eta\mu T}\,m_{\mathrm{traj}}^2$. The trajectory auditor's separation exceeds the final-model auditor's by at least the factor $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$.

(c) The response is deterministic, $D_T=A^{-1}(I-(I-\eta A)^T)\Delta$, with $\lVert D_T\rVert\le\lVert\Delta\rVert/\mu$, and for every input $x$,
$$\lvert\langle D_T,x\rangle\rvert\le m_T\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x).$$
Under stealth $\delta$ against the final-model auditor, $\lvert\langle D_T,x\rangle\rvert\le m(\delta)\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ for every $x$. Under stealth $\delta$ against the trajectory auditor, $\lvert\langle D_T,x\rangle\rvert\le m(\delta)\sqrt{2/(\eta\mu T)}\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ and $\lVert D_T\rVert\le\varsigma\,m(\delta)/(\mu\sqrt T)$.

(d) The write budget does not enter. Stealth binds the aggregate shift $\lVert\Delta\rVert$, which $k$ records of any $k\ge1$ can produce by label changes of total size $n\lVert\Delta\rVert$ at most. The stealthy steering price is a deviation budget, and the stealthy displacement of the final model is at most $m(\delta)\,\varsigma\sqrt{\eta/\mu}$ against the final-model auditor and $\varsigma\,m(\delta)/(\mu\sqrt T)$ against the trajectory auditor, whatever the write budget.

Sketch. The two chains are linear with the same matrix $I-\eta A$ and the same Gaussian noise, so each view is Gaussian with the same covariance under both executions, and the means differ by $(D_1,\dots,D_T)$ with $D_{t+1}=(I-\eta A)D_t+\eta\Delta$ (proven, induction). For two Gaussians of equal covariance the total variation is $2\Phi(m/2)-1$ and the Kullback divergence is $m^2/2$, with $m$ the Mahalanobis distance (proven, standard). The trajectory law is Markov, so its Kullback divergence is the sum over steps of the divergence between the step kernels along the honest trajectory, each a pair $\mathcal N(\cdot,\eta^2\varsigma^2I)$ with means $\eta\Delta$ apart, hence $T\lVert\Delta\rVert^2/(2\varsigma^2)$ and $m_{\mathrm{traj}}^2=T\lVert\Delta\rVert^2/\varsigma^2$ (proven). This is `thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter` (a) with $\bar\kappa_f=\lVert\Delta\rVert^2$, with the exact Gaussian formula in place of Pinsker. Along an eigendirection, $D_{T,\lambda}=(1-r_\lambda^T)\Delta_\lambda/\lambda$ and $\mathrm{Var}=\eta^2\varsigma^2(1-r_\lambda^{2T})/(1-r_\lambda^2)$, and the quotient with $1-r_\lambda^2=\eta\lambda(2-\eta\lambda)$ gives the formula of (a) (proven). Since $(1-r^T)/(1+r^T)\le1$ and $2-\eta\lambda\le2$, each term is at most $2\Delta_\lambda^2/(\eta\varsigma^2\lambda)\le2\Delta_\lambda^2/(\eta\varsigma^2\mu)$, which sums to $(2/(\eta\mu T))\,m_{\mathrm{traj}}^2$ (proven). Cauchy and Schwarz in the $\Sigma_T$ inner product give $\lvert\langle D_T,x\rangle\rvert=\lvert\langle\Sigma_T^{-1/2}D_T,\Sigma_T^{1/2}x\rangle\rvert\le m_T\,\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ (proven). Stealth $\delta$ against the best auditor on a view means $2\Phi(m/2)-1\le\delta$, so $m\le m(\delta)$, and (c) and (d) follow by substitution (proven). The bound $\lVert D_T\rVert\le\lVert\Delta\rVert\max_\lambda(1-r_\lambda^T)/\lambda\le\lVert\Delta\rVert/\mu$ is read off the eigendecomposition (proven). What is unchecked is only my arithmetic, which a second reader should redo, so the status is sketch.

The experiment. The experimenter knows $A$, $\eta$, $\varsigma$ and $T$, so $m_{\mathrm{traj}}$ and $m_T$ are computed. One coupled pair of runs reads $D_T$, since it is deterministic. Drawn runs give $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(x)$ and a classifier two-sample test on them gives a lower bound on the auditor's advantage to compare with $2\Phi(m/2)-1$. Every quantity of the statement is measured.

The other regime. The bound binds every auditor, so it is statistical. An auditor who does not know the honest law estimates it from the oracle at the shadow-run price of item 11, and an auditor of a restricted class detects less, which permits a larger shift. For a nonlinear model the analogue of the trajectory step is `thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter` (a) with $\bar\kappa_f$ the mean squared shift along the honest trajectory (proven there), and the analogue of the response step is the response constant $S$, at most $1/\mu$ under strong convexity and at most $((1+\eta L)^T-1)/L$ under smoothness alone, which is vacuous for $T\gg1/(\eta L)$ (proven by the same induction). The response constant of real training is open, and the oracle measures it by coupled runs (empirical). For feature poisoning the shift depends on $\theta$ and the laws are no longer Gaussian, so only the filter bound applies (open for the exact form).

### prop-secret-target-planted-detection: a secret target set makes the auditor a detector of the planted law

status: sketch
uses: def-steering-attacker-price, prop-label-poison-stealth-response, thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views, thm-shadow-price-is-generative-exponent, thm-bayes-release-loss-matched-price, fact-gkvz22-backdoor-scope, fact-chlx25-defendability-characterization

(a) Public target set. If a member of the auditor's class evaluates the indicator of $\Gamma$ on its view, every steering attacker with stealth $\delta$ has $s\le s^\circ+\delta$ (proven, the indicator is an auditor and its advantage is $s-s^\circ$). To decide at level $\alpha$ the auditor needs the honest rate within $a$, which costs $\lceil\ln(2/\alpha)/(2a^2)\rceil$ drawn runs at the honest input (proven, Hoeffding), the two-view case of `thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views` (a), sound for every learning function under drawn seeds (item 7). Seed grinding, choosing the seed until $\theta_T\in\Gamma$, is a steering attacker with zero shift and advantage $1-s^\circ$ against the indicator auditor, so it is caught without seed control (proven). `thm-replay-audit-steer` is the secret-target case with seed control, where the auditor replays because it cannot evaluate $\Gamma$.

(b) Secret target set. If no member of the auditor's class evaluates the indicator, the cap of (a) fails. GKVZ22 Theorem 6.4 gives, for random Fourier features under Hypothesis 3.7 (homogeneous CLWE) and Assumption 6.3 (an inverse-polynomial margin), success one on the trigger predicate with negligible white-box advantage for polynomial-time auditors (proven there, conditional). BRV26 Theorem 2 with Theorem 5 gives white-box total variation $o(1)$ against every auditor for the frozen compressing Gaussian layer, with the collision strength conditional on Assumption 2 (proven there). Both plant in the training coins or in a frozen layer. Their shift on the update stream is undefined, since the planted object is not a response of training to records, and the response constant of `def-steering-attacker-price` does not apply to them.

(c) The auditor's price is the detection price of the planted law. For a secret target set the auditor's advantage on a view is at most the total variation between the honest and the planted law of that view (proven, definition), so its price is the detection price of the planted law against its class. For a data-level statistical-query auditor and planted records whose law along a hidden direction has generative exponent $k^*$ relative to the honest Gaussian law, the price is of order $d^{k^*/2}$ up to the factor $\lambda_{k^*}^{-2}$ (DPLB24 Theorem 3.2 through Proposition 3.7, proven there, item 27), and a mixture with weight $\varepsilon=k/n$ scales $\lambda_{k^*}$ by $\varepsilon$ (heuristic). For a planted bipartite clique it is FGRVX17 Theorem 2.9 (proven there). For a model-level auditor the total variation is at most the data-level one (proven, data processing), and for a trajectory auditor of a noisy gradient trainer it is at most $\sqrt{T\bar\kappa_f/(4\varsigma^2)}$ with $\bar\kappa_f$ the mean squared shift the planted records cause (`thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter` (a), proven). The planting is the attacker's choice, so the rule of `CONTEXT.md` on the designer's modifications is silent on it, and the hardness so obtained is an upper bound on the auditor, an attack result. It never yields a defence theorem.

(d) An auditor that reads what the trainer reads sees every steer. If the auditor's class contains the statistics the trainer reads, the per-example update means, then every steer is by definition a shift of those statistics and the auditor detects it at the trainer's own query price plus the noise cost (proven, definition of the shift). A planted law that differs from the honest one only at degrees above the trainer's does not steer a correlational trainer, since a square-loss gradient trainer reads the data through correlational queries alone (item 16, `thm-bayes-release-loss-matched-price`, proven), and a planted law that differs at degree one or two is detected from the data at price of order $d$ (DPLB24 at $k^*\le2$, proven there). So against a correlational trainer, stealth at degree above two and steering are incompatible (proven, from the two cited results).

(e) What a secret direction buys, exactly, in the setting of `prop-label-poison-stealth-response`. Let the attacker draw the direction of $\Delta$ uniformly on the sphere, at radius $\rho$, which a write budget $k\ge d$ or a random choice of records realizes (heuristic), and let $A=\lambda I$. The steered final-model law is a mixture of Gaussians over the direction, and its chi-square divergence from the honest law is $\mathbb E\exp(m_T^2\langle u,u'\rangle)-1$ for independent uniform unit $u,u'$, which is $m_T^4/(2d)+O(m_T^8/d^2)$ (proven, second-moment method, with the Gaussian approximation of $\langle u,u'\rangle$ at variance $1/d$, heuristic in the error term). So the best auditor's advantage is at most $m_T^2/(2\sqrt{2d})$, and stealth $\delta$ permits $m_T$ up to order $d^{1/4}\sqrt\delta$, against $m(\delta)$ of order $\delta$ when the direction is known. The matching detection, by the chi-square statistic at $m_T$ of order $d^{1/4}$, is the classical boundary of Gaussian mean testing from one observation (Baraud, Bernoulli 2002, from memory, unverified). The trigger response at the attacker's best $\tau$ is then of order $d^{1/4}\sqrt\delta$ honest standard deviations, and the trajectory auditor cuts it by the factor $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$ of (b) of the previous object (proven given (e)). The hardness here is the secrecy of a random direction, a key of the attacker, and nothing is planted in the honest law.

The experiment for (e). Drawn runs under a fixed secret direction give $\mathrm{sd}^\circ(\tau)$ and the response at $\tau$. Drawn runs over fresh directions give the mixture, on which a classifier two-sample test lower-bounds the auditor's advantage and the chi-square statistic on $\Sigma_T^{-1/2}(\theta_T-\bar\theta^\circ)$ attains it when $m_T\gg d^{1/4}$.

The other regime. (a) and (c) bind every auditor. (b) is computational in GKVZ22 and statistical in BRV26. (d) is a statement about a trainer class, correlational, and an auditor class that contains it. (e) is statistical, and a statistical-query auditor on the final model reads $\theta_T$ through queries of tolerance $\tau_q$, which replaces $d^{1/4}$ by the degree-two price of a spiked mean, open in its exact form. # 5. The statement from this aspect

An attacker that acts is an additive shift on the trainer's update stream, whether it sets records, an update or a value in transit, and its two prices are the push-to-noise budget its shift costs against an auditor of that stream and the trainer's response to a unit shift, a stability constant the oracle measures by coupled runs. When the target set is public, stealth caps steering success at the honest rate, which the auditor buys with drawn runs at the shadow-run price. When the target set is secret, the auditor's task is detecting the planted law, its price is the detection exponent of that law against the auditor's class, and the planting is the attacker's move, so it borrows nothing and proves an attack, never a defence. In the linear model with noisy gradient descent the trigger response of a stealthy label poisoner is a few honest noise standard deviations when its direction is known and of order $d^{1/4}$ when it is secret, and a trajectory auditor cuts both by $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$. The published undetectable backdoors place the planted law in the training coins or a frozen layer, where the response of training is not defined, and they price nothing about learning.
