## Reply of the third launch

1. `learning-attacker-class`. Question: which class makes an attacker a learner, and what shape can a lower bound against it take? Idea: an attacker that must exploit the signal by learning, and not by an arbitrary function at a given signal level, meets a bound sharper than the geometric one, roughly the cost of learning the main task (`halil-ideas.md` item 1). The accepted contribution form restricts a family of attacks and bounds every member's success on a family of models, as the comparison-sort bound does (item 14; `2026-10-27-what-you-want.md` §1, attacks on learners come from learners). Why it matters: every critic, the council and the debate name this as the one gap, and none of the 63 objects defines the class (`2026-10-29-what-we-have.md` §1, item 2). Expected critique: the general form is refuted (booklet 5.11), the one instance (item 16) is a property of the channel since the Bayes release is correlationally identical to the data, and every bound that sees a class restricts what it reads and never how it trains, the AKMSS21 embedding holding only in a rounding model where memorization cannot be stated (item 26); the better form declares the class as architecture, optimizer, batch and precision, prices it in updates, and states the theorem as the secret channel's exponent at least the task's exponent under named conditions.

2. `white-box-stitching-class`. Question: can the white-box attacker be priced by restricting it to the linear readouts practitioners actually run? Idea: for an arbitrary attacker holding the weights there is no good bound, even in split learning. Practitioners plug the weights into other models through a linear projection or after PCA and stay near the model's own basis, so restricting or measuring that class suffices, and an expectation over seeds is a weak specification (`halil-ideas.md` item 12). Why it matters: white box is where every idealized class fails (`what-you-want` §5.2), the theory has no white-box price, and the debate's statistic class (item 32) is already this class for updates. Expected critique: a linear readout class is a sufficiency class, so its price is the degree-2 price of DPLB24 Proposition 2.6, within a constant of the full observer on generic laws and separated only when the second moment along the secret vanishes, a knife edge; and "near the model's basis" is a seed-dependent frame, capped by the short-seed dichotomy (item 20) and reported as nearly linear across seeds by model stitching (empirical, not in the library). Better form: a probe class of stated rank and degree, priced by the generative exponent of the channel restricted to the probe span.

3. `capacity-as-price`. Question: is the attacker's model capacity, relative to the target's, a coordinate of the price? Idea: realize one result across today's architectures, sizes, data and scenarios, and state what the attack needed as a number of queries or as a capacity of the attacker's model relative to the model attacked, read off agreement on held-out data; the attacker must match or exceed the target's capacity by a stated amount (`halil-ideas.md` item 11; the candidate units in item 8). Why it matters: it is the one unit he names that is realizable on real models without a law of the data (items 5 and 7), and the adapter gap (booklet 7.1, open) is its only hook in the theory. Expected critique: held-out agreement is a success level and not a budget, so capacity as he states it sits in the argument of the price rather than its value; item 34 shows the attacker's cost floor depends on the release's noise and query size and not on its parameter count, so capacity binds only through the sample complexity of a declared family. Better form: a two-coordinate budget, samples and attacker parameter count against the target's, measured by the oracle across sizes and proven first in the Gaussian linear model, where capacity is the dimension.

## Reply of the first launch

1. `learning-attacker-class`. Question. Which attacker class does the theory price, and how does that class enter a lower bound? Idea. Attacks on learning come from learning algorithms, so the attacker is a learner whose reconstruction problem takes the intermediate vectors as input (`CONTEXT.md`, framing of 2026-10-25, and `what-you-want.md` §1). A learning-constrained attacker meets a bound near the cost of learning the main task (`halil-ideas.md` item 1), and white-box attackers in practice read weights through a linear projection or PCA, so restricting or measuring that class suffices (items 12, 14). Why it matters. No object names a learner of a declared architecture as the attacker, and every lower bound binds all estimators or a reader class (`what-we-have.md` items 2, 32, section 8), so the aim has no statement yet. Critique. The main-task form is refuted (booklet `conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost`) and survives only for a Bayes release against a square-loss gradient learner (item 16) and for re-learning under nature's noise (item 34). Item 12 names the one class the theory can bound, the statistic class, so I will formulate the attacker as a reader of a declared statistic followed by any learner, priced by the reader's degree and sample count, with the gradient learner a case only inside the AKMSS21 rounding model.

2. `defence-as-honest-constraint`. Question. Which changes to the honest protocol count as a defence, and in what unit is a defence priced? Idea. A designer's modification is admissible when it programs an oracle with a value no party can tell from an honest sample (`CONTEXT.md`, instruction of 2026-10-27), and modifications of the data give expectations for measurability (`what-you-want.md` §1, §2). Asking honest clients to plant structure is poison unless a theorem backs it, while a constraint they already meet, such as adding noise, is acceptable (`halil-ideas.md` item 10). Why it matters. The programming rule and canaries are pending rulings (`what-we-have.md` decisions 5, 6), and the one exact defence, normalization at the optimum (item 33), restricts the honest learning function in the way he allows. Critique. "Already meet" describes practice and gives no criterion, and admissible programming changes no price (item 10), so it is never a defence. I will separate programming, a measurement device, from a restriction of the honest learning function, a defence, priced in the trainer's units by the utility it costs against the gap it opens for a named attacker class, as items 18 and 33 do.

3. `capacity-as-attacker-budget`. Question. Is the capacity of the attacker's model, relative to the model under attack, a coordinate of the price? Idea. Realize one result on models in use, across architectures, sizes, data and scenarios, then state what the attack needed, queries or a capacity of the attacker's model relative to the target, read off agreement on held-out data, the attacker matching or exceeding the target by a stated amount (`halil-ideas.md` item 11, with the units of item 8). Why it matters. It is the one proposal that reaches real architectures, where no law exists and only the measured cost of a declared learner remains (`what-we-have.md` section 6, last paragraph), so it fixes what the measured side of a price records. Critique. Parameter count is neither necessary nor sufficient, since PCA and the norm-weighted estimator share a capacity and one fails where the other succeeds (item 32), and one linear program inverts a ReLU client (item 14). The quantity that acts as capacity in every bound we hold is the degree of the statistic the attacker reads, the generative exponent of the channel (item 27), so I will define the capacity price as the least reader degree that succeeds, its sample price the dimension to half that degree, measured by fitting readers of rising degree, with item 34 as the exact case where matching capacity means the trainer's own class.

## Reply of the second launch

1. `learning-attacker-class`. Which restriction on the attacker lets a lower bound see it as a learner? Halil holds that a learning-constrained attacker meets a sharper bound than the geometric one, near the cost of learning the main task (`halil-ideas.md` item 1). Practitioners read open weights through linear projections, PCA and stitching, and restricting or measuring that class suffices (item 12), and the theorem to aim at bounds every member of a restricted attack family on a family of models (item 14). It matters because no result prices a learning attacker as a learner (`what-we-have.md` items 2 and 32, section 8), and the plan he agreed to rests on this theorem. I expect to argue that every bound that sees a class restricts what the attacker reads, a statistic or statistical queries, never the fact that it trains, so item 12 names the one class with a proven side, while the conjecture of item 1 is refuted in general (booklet 5.11) and holds for the Bayes release alone (item 16). The better object fixes the attacker as a statistic read, a hypothesis class fitted and an optimizer, prices the first two, and leaves the optimizer unpriced outside AKMSS21.

2. `defence-priced-in-trainer-units`. Which change to honest behaviour counts as a defence, and in what unit is it priced? Halil admits a designer's modification as a random oracle may return a fixed value that no party can tell from a fresh sample (`CONTEXT.md`, instructions of 2026-10-27). Honest clients adding noise is acceptable, and honest clients planting structure is poison without a theorem (`halil-ideas.md` item 10). It matters because a security statement without a priced defence is a catalogue of attacks, and decision 5 of `what-we-have.md` waits on this reading. I expect to argue that the indistinguishability reading preserves every price (item 10), so under it nothing is a defence, while the two defences the theory holds, Gaussian releases and gradient normalization (items 6 and 33), change honest behaviour detectably. The better formulation separates a measurement rule, programming that preserves prices, from a defence rule that charges the defence in the trainer's units, its excess risk or its extra samples, so both sides of a statement carry a price.

3. `the-steering-attacker`. How is an attacker that acts, by poisoning, planting or altering values in transit, priced, and does attacker-planted structure escape the planting objection? Halil lets the attacker change training data or activations in transit, as a party in the middle of U-shaped split learning, with the attack model following the scenario (`halil-ideas.md` item 3), and he lists backdoors and watermarks among the views he wants bounded (item 1). It matters because Definition 3 admits steering, item 9 is the only steering result, and every price to date is for a passive observer, while the settings he names are active. I expect to argue that a backdoor and a watermark are channels the attacker plants, so the learner of item 1 is the auditor and the roles flip. The hidden-direction machinery he rejects in item 4 then prices the detection of a planted signal legitimately, since the attacker plants it, and his conjecture becomes the generative exponent of the planted channel (item 27) read against the auditor's class.
