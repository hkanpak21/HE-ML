# The statement of the theory, final

Integrated from the draft and the two reviews. Clauses 1 to 21 keep the draft's numbering, clauses 22 to 26 are the additions the reviewers asked for, and the objective opens the statement. Each clause is a definition, an assumption, a claim with its status in the four words, or a line of scope. Items refer to `readings/2026-10-29-what-we-have.md`. The section Dissent lists every amendment not taken.

## Statement

Objective. The theory prices an adversary against a learning procedure in the units of the procedure's own training, proves the prices by reduction to learning problems with known thresholds, with or without the oracle, and measures the same prices by experiment, the oracle supplying the runs (`CONTEXT.md`). Its headline target is a natural procedure in which a declared attacker's price exceeds the trainer's by a margin that is named and measured, with item 15 the one instance, at $\Theta(1+n\rho^2)$ under the Gaussian mechanism (proven), and the matched-budget form of clause 17 the next candidate (open).

1. Definition. A learning function $\mathsf L(x;\rho,\xi)$ maps a training input, a seed and hardware randomness to a model, served by the learning oracle through $\mathsf{Train}$, $\mathsf{Eval}$ and $\mathsf{Open}$ in a named mode that reuses seeds and a drawn mode that cannot. A procedure is a dataflow graph with declared channels, a party is a view, choices and a goal, and a certifier bounds leakage from $\mathsf{Train}$ calls.

2. Claim, proven. The oracle is realizable, an unbounded attacker gains nothing from it, and a certifier sees it through the seed name alone, coupled runs certifying leakage for every learning function (items 1 to 8).

3. Definition. A declared learner is a model family of $p$ parameters, a loss, an optimizer with batch $b$, steps $T$ and precision $\rho$ or injected noise $\varsigma$, and a readout fixed in advance. The trainer is a declared learner. The attacker and the auditor are declared learners when a statement says so, and otherwise members of a declared class, every observer, the best test, a statistic, statistical queries, or a learner trained on runs, and stealth is stated against that class. A learning attacker's budget is $(n,T,b,p)$ with its $\mathsf{Train}$ calls and queries beside it, $p$ a coordinate and not a price. Its price exists only on instances whose hidden frame is drawn per instance with positive entropy, since on a fixed instance a readout fixed in advance may be the Bayes decoder. A party that acts adds its write and deviation budgets.

4. Definition. A price is the set of minimal budgets at which some member of a class reaches a success level, and a stealth level when it acts, on every instance of a class of record laws. Comparisons run along one coordinate or in exponent, and the exponent alone is no unit (proven, item 28). A price is known within a factor when a declared attack's measured budget lies within that factor of a proven lower side for a class containing it.

5. Definition. A programming fixes an oracle's values at finitely many points to recorded draws and is admissible when no member of the class tells the two setups apart. A defence is a change to the procedure with three properties. Every randomness it introduces has a public law, is drawn fresh at each application of the defence's kernel, independent of all other randomness, and is read by that application alone and by no other node, party or later application, so per-step and per-query noise pass and a random layer reused across the records of one run is a kept key. It runs at every execution. It keeps the trainer in its declared class, the family and the loss unchanged, and may change the trainer's update, as clipping and normalization do. Its utility price is the budget ratio of each honest party that pays, in that party's coordinates at a stated risk. Its security price against a class on a task is the ratio of the class's prices along one coordinate, in named units, and on a steer task the inverse ratio of the stealthy response caps with the shift chosen after the defence. A defence is admissible when the lower side of each security price holds over an instance class fixed before the defence and over every choice of a steerer within its budget.

6. Claim, proven. An admissible programming moves every member's success by at most $\varepsilon$ at every budget, so it measures and is never a defence, and a defence is never an admissible programming (item 10). An in-distribution canary that replaces an honest record, a recorded seed and recorded noise program. An added, out-of-distribution or random-label canary changes the law of the released model, programs nothing, and measures a record outside the honest law (decision 6). Release noise, clipping, normalization and a predictive release defend.

7. Definition. A steering attacker sets records, sends an update, alters a value in transit or chooses a seed. The first three are a shift $v_t(\theta)$ on the trainer's update stream, with deviation budget $\sup_{t,\theta}\|v_t(\theta)\|$ and push-to-noise budget $\mathbb E\sum_t\|v_t(\theta_t)\|^2/(2\varsigma^2)$ along the honest trajectory. A chosen seed is a choice of the noise path with zero shift, so the chain bound of clause 10 says nothing about it and the public-target cap of clause 22 prices it. Stealth $\delta$ against an auditor class is advantage at most $\delta$, the auditor buying its baseline from drawn runs, and the response constant bounds the coupled displacement of the final model per unit of deviation.

8. Claim, proven for the simulation, a reading of the register for the count. No lower bound binds an attacker because it learns, since fine-precision SGD on an engineered network simulates any PAC learner (Abbe, Kamath, Malach, Sandon and Srebro, NeurIPS 2021, Theorem 1a). Every bound the theory holds sees a learning attacker through one of three handles: what it reads, a statistic, statistical queries in the worst-rounding model (Theorem 1c) or correlational queries under the square loss, the noise of a Gaussian update chain, the trainer's or the attacker's own, the latter the analyst's handle that the attacker in practice does not supply, and an assumption on every efficient algorithm. An attacker that acts is bound beside these through the auditor's view of the target event.

9. Assumption. Two games, never mixed. Statistical-query and noisy-chain bounds are unconditional, polynomial-time bounds with the record-level view are conditional on named lattice hardness (Halil, 2026-10-25).

10. Claim, proven. One lemma, three uses. Under two laws the trajectory laws of a Gaussian update chain differ in total variation by at most $\sqrt{T\bar\kappa/(4\varsigma^2)}$, $\bar\kappa$ the mean squared shift of the per-example update mean along the null trajectory (item 18 (a)). It bounds the trainer as a filter, the noisy gradient attacker in steps, and the steerer's stealth.

11. Claim, proven and empirical. Shadow runs cost $\Theta(k/a^2)$, membership against the Gaussian mechanism $\Theta(1+n\rho^2)$ times the trainer's price, and the full observer's update price is bracketed within a factor 86, the one price with a measured and a proven side in one unit (items 11, 15, 29 to 31). Every computational price rests on structure in the record law along a hidden direction or a hidden subset, and in a Gaussian frame family the degree of a channel is the generative exponent with the secret as label (proven, items 20 to 28).

12. Claim, proven in the Gaussian linear model and for correlational classes, open beyond. The attacker pays what the trainer paid when its observations are the trainer's data in law and its secret is the trainer's target as a learning problem, for every class at the Bayes release with nature's noise and inputs, for correlational classes under any centred noise, and for the trained release within the factor $1/\kappa$ (items 16, 34). Each condition is needed. A proper function of the target can cost $d$ times less, an exact release is relearned from $d$ queries, and for another secret neither inequality holds (booklet 5.11).

13. Claim, proven for the chain bound and the kernel eigenvalues up to degree three, heuristic above. Under the decoding hypothesis, that no bounded frame-free readout decodes weakly at the frame-averaged law, which holds at generative exponent two with margin $O(d^{-1/2})$, a noisy gradient attacker regressing the secret on shadow pairs needs $T\ge(1-2\delta_m)^2\varsigma^2/\bar\kappa$ steps to decode weakly at every frame. Here $\delta_m$ is the total variation between $m$ test pairs from the frame mixture and $m$ pairs from the frame-averaged law, with $m\ge192/c_0^2$ at weak-decoding level $c_0$, of order $\sqrt{m\lambda_{k^*}^2d^{-k^*/2}}$ with $k^*$ the generative exponent of the channel with the secret as label and $\lambda_{k^*}$ its first Hermite coefficient. And $\bar\kappa\le G_\nabla^2\sum_{k\ge l}a_k^2\nu_k$, with $l\ge k^*$ the information exponent, $a_k$ the Hermite coefficients of the conditional mean of the secret given the view, $G_\nabla^2$ the family's squared gradient norm in $L^2(\gamma_d)$, and $\nu_k$ the top eigenvalue of the centred degree-$k$ Haar kernel for normalized Hermite polynomials, $\nu_1=1/d$, $\nu_2=2/(d(d+2))$, $\nu_3=3/(d(d+2))$ (proven), $\nu_k=\Theta(d^{-\lceil(k+1)/2\rceil})$ for $k\ge4$ (heuristic). The price in steps is of order $\varsigma^2d^{\lceil(l+1)/2\rceil}/G_\nabla^2$, with a constant set by $a_l$ and, when $l$ is even, by $a_{l+1}$ as well, since $\nu_l$ and $\nu_{l+1}$ then share an order, and capacity lowers it by at most the factor $p$.

14. Claim, proven in the linear model for a known direction, heuristic for a secret one. Write $m(\delta)=2\Phi^{-1}((1+\delta)/2)$, which is $\sqrt{2\pi}\,\delta$ to first order. A stealthy label poisoner of noisy gradient descent moves the trigger response by at most $m(\delta)$ honest standard deviations with a known direction (proven) and by order $d^{1/4}\sqrt\delta$ with a secret one (heuristic, the Gaussian mean-testing boundary from one observation), a trajectory auditor regains $\sqrt{\eta\mu T/2}$ once $\eta\mu T\ge2$ (proven), and the response constant is $1/\mu$ under strong convexity, $((1+\eta L)^T-1)/L$ under smoothness (proven), open for real training.

15. Claim, proven by the definition of the shift for the equivalence, in exponent for Gaussian records against the product null for the prices, heuristic for a structured honest law. Every steer is a shift of the statistics the trainer reads, its per-example update means, beyond the trainer's own sampling noise at its budget, so an auditor whose class holds those statistics at the trainer's tolerance detects every steer, and stealth against that auditor is impossible wherever steering happens. Against a statistical-query auditor, a planted law at Hermite degree $k$ along a hidden direction costs of order $d^{k/2}$ to detect (DPLB24 Theorems 3.2 and 4.1), which is the correlational trainer's own price at that degree (from memory, unverified), so a steer at degree $k$ is stealthy against an auditor of budget below $d^{k/2}$ exactly when the trainer's update has a degree-$k$ component that resolves the shift at the trainer's budget, which a linear model never has above degree two and a network has at every degree. Attacker-planted structure yields attack results and never a defence theorem, and the published undetectable backdoors plant in training coins or a frozen layer (GKVZ22 Theorem 6.4, BRV26 Theorems 2 and 5).

16. Claim, proven. Three defences bind every observer, Gaussian releases, noisy gradients and normalization, and a release with nature's noise binds every power-constrained observer, costing the attacker $\Theta(n/(\kappa d))$ against that class and nothing against the unconstrained one, and the consumer $k$ answers per prediction for excess $s^2/k$ (items 6, 18, 33, 34). Against the noisy gradient class a defence's price under $\Pi'$ is at least $(1-2\delta_m)^2\varsigma^2/\bar\kappa^{\Pi'}$, so its security price has lower side $\bar\kappa^{\Pi}/\bar\kappa^{\Pi'}$ when the undefended attacker attains its bound (proven given clause 13).

17. Claim, heuristic. Honest Gaussian noise $\varsigma$ on client updates multiplies the trainer's rounds by $1+b\varsigma^2/\mu_2$ and every passive observer's update price by $(1+b\varsigma^2/\mu_2)^2$, both in exponent of $\varsigma$ and with constants that depend on the label law at fixed batch, since the full observer's per-update chi-square under the Gaussian surrogate given $W$, $\frac12\mathbb E[W^4T(W)^2/(W^2+\varsigma^2)^2]$ to second order in the spike, and the spiked value $\frac12c_2^2/(\mu_2+\varsigma^2)^2$ have a ratio that moves with $\varsigma$ (heuristic, the surrogate is not the update law). On the steer task the same noise leaves the stealthy trigger response at $m(\delta)$ honest standard deviations, so its security price is one in units of the honest spread, and the honest spread grows linearly in $\varsigma$, so in absolute units a stealthy steerer's displacement grows linearly in $\varsigma$ and the security price on the steer task is $\varsigma_0/\varsigma$ against a reference noise $\varsigma_0$ (proven in the linear instance). A defence's steer-task price names its units, and the absolute unit is the one the consumer meets at the trigger.

18. Open. A bound for a batch-one gradient attacker at fine precision without injected noise outside the single-index family, the core. A natural learning problem yielding a one-way function. A defence priced against a learner class at a matched budget. The response constant of real training.

19. Scope. Planted hardness for a defence theorem does not count, the ban lives in the instance class, and the attacker may plant. A cheap cryptographic plug-in does not count, a natural one-way function would, and the seed generator is Halil's decision 4.

20. Scope. A real seed is a short key and caps every seed-blind bound at the number of seeds, seed-blind results hold for SGD and fail for Adam, and items 21 to 23 hold in exact arithmetic (proven, items 13 and 20 to 23). The white-box attacker is bound only by statements that bind every observer or that restrict the trainer, Gaussian releases, noisy gradients, normalization and the statistical-query filter among them (proven, items 6, 18, 26, 33), since every bound that sees a class through what it reads fails once the attacker holds the representation, and no computational bound sees its class (a reading of the register). A backdoor and a watermark are channels whose learner is the auditor or the user, a watermark's price being its key length (CHLX25 Definition 3.1 and Theorem 4.1, CGZ24 Lemma 4). The natural-data oracle is parked and homomorphic encryption set aside.

21. Claim, proven by the definitions. The oracle supplies the runs: drawn runs give baselines, coupled runs the response and the leakage certificate, the two seed modes $\bar\kappa$ along the attacker's trajectory, and both procedures a defence's two prices. A measured failure of a declared attack is never a lower bound (item 2). On real data the proven side survives in the bounds that quantify over every learning function or every law, the needle and shadow-run bounds, the public-target cap and the chain bound with $\bar\kappa$ measured, and elsewhere only the measured side exists.

22. Claim, proven. When the auditor's class evaluates the indicator of the target set, every steering attacker with stealth $\delta$ has success at most the honest rate plus $\delta$, and the auditor buys the honest rate within $a$ at confidence $1-\alpha$ from $\lceil\ln(2/\alpha)/(2a^2)\rceil$ drawn runs at the honest input, sound for every learning function (items 7, 11). A chosen seed is a steering attacker with zero shift and advantage $1-s^\circ$ against that auditor, so a public target needs no seed control, and the replay audit is the secret-target case with seed control (item 9). When the target set is secret the cap fails (GKVZ22 Theorem 6.4, BRV26 Theorems 2 and 5), the auditor's task is to detect the planted law, and its price is the detection price of that law against its class, the generative exponent for a planted direction (item 27) and FGRVX17 Theorem 2.9 for a planted clique, an attack result and never a defence theorem.

23. Claim, proven. A coalition that deviates its additive inputs to Gaussian releases by at most $B_A$ raises the coupled tail to a fixed function of the honest one, coupled runs certify it with about $e^{\mathfrak e}$ runs, $\mathfrak e=(R-1)B_A^2/(2\sigma^2)$, and every certifier sound for every learning function needs $e^{(1-o(1))\mathfrak e}$ $\mathsf{Train}$ calls (item 6). Certifying against a coalition with admissible choices costs runs linear in the number of choices and is impossible for infinitely many (`thm-active-choice-dichotomy`). The push-to-noise budget of clause 7 is this $\mathfrak e$ with the update stream as the release, so the steerer's stealth and the certifier's cost are one quantity.

24. Claim, proven. No bound on the trainer's output alone filters. A trainer that outputs one bit, or an $\varepsilon$-differentially private bit, hands a planted parity to the identity attacker, so no bound on the entropy, the mutual information or the central privacy loss of the output composes with a statistical-query lower bound. A filter restricts the trainer while it trains, its access, its state or its trajectory. Under Gaussian noise the attacker's advantage is at most $\sqrt{TC^2\bar\gamma/\sigma^2+\mathcal I/2}$ with $\mathcal I=2TC^2/(b\sigma^2)$ the trajectory's information about the sample, and the second term cannot be dropped, since one noisy step into a hashed table memorizes every record at $\mathcal I=\Theta(d^2\log d)$ (item 18). Hence a defence acts on the learning function or the dataflow during training and never on the released output alone, and the honest filter is a theorem only at $\sigma^2\gtrsim TC^2/b$, a regime deployed training avoids, so at deployed noise the noise defence has a measured side and no proven side.

25. Claim, proven given the simulation of local randomizers by statistical queries (Kasiviswanathan, Lee, Nissim, Raskhodnikova and Smith, FOCS 2008, `fact-ldp-equals-sq`, recorded and not re-read). A trainer that reads each record through a local randomizer is a statistical-query learner, so by item 26 every statistical-query-hard secret stays hidden from every attacker of its output, white box included. Client-side noise on records is a defence by composition, with security price the statistical-query price of the secret and utility price the statistical-query price of the task at the randomizer's tolerance, both open in their constants. It is the one defence whose proven side binds every attacker without the regime condition of clause 24, and no object states it as a defence.

26. Open. The factor within which a price counts as known. Along a family indexed by $d$ a price is known in exponent when the logarithms of the measured and the proven budgets have a ratio tending to one, and at a fixed dimension the factor is stated with the result, 86 being the only instance. No rule says when a measured side on real data, where no law is stated, meets a proven side on a law the experimenter built. Until one exists a result on real data is the measured price of a declared attack and nothing more.

## Dissent

Every amendment of the two reviews is taken except the two below. Where the two reviewers amended one clause differently, clauses 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 13, 15, 17 and 20, the two amendments are merged and both are taken, with the exceptions recorded here.

- Defence reviewer, clause 5. Its replacement keeps "leaves the trainer's declared components unchanged". Not taken, since clipping and normalization change the optimizer and clause 6 calls them defences, which the steering reviewer showed. The final clause keeps the trainer in its declared class, the family and the loss unchanged, and lets the update rule change. The defence reviewer's grain at the application of the kernel, its admissibility clause and its payer clause are taken.

- Defence reviewer, clause 13. Its replacement defines $\delta_m$ as the mean total variation between $m$ test pairs at one frame and $m$ pairs from the frame-averaged law, and names the DPLB24 coefficient $\zeta_{k^*}$. Not taken. The per-frame total variation is $\Theta(\lambda_{k^*})$ at every frame and does not decay in $d$, as the steering reviewer's round 02 showed, and the quantity the second-moment bound controls is the total variation of the frame mixture, which the final clause uses. DPLB24 writes $\lambda_k$ for the coefficient and $\zeta_k$ for the function, so the Haar eigenvalues are renamed $\nu_k$ instead, as the steering reviewer proposed. The defence reviewer's decoding hypothesis, its test-pair count $m\ge192/c_0^2$, its generative exponent in $\delta_m$ and its constant through $a_l$ and $a_{l+1}$ are taken.

No amendment was rejected outright, and no clause was dropped.
