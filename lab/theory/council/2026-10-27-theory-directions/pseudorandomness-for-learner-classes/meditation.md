# Pseudorandomness for learner classes

## The question

Can one notion carry learnability, security and measurement? The notion is a family of joint laws that a learner class at a budget cannot tell from the product of its marginals. The test has three parts. Is the notion more than statistical dimension, moment matching or pseudorandom functions? Does its gap object, the learning one-way function, describe the extraction and seed-separation results? What handle on gradient learners of a fixed architecture exists beyond the embedding in SQ?

## Meditation

A party faces a family $\{\Pi_s\}_{s\in S}$ of laws of a pair $(U,W)$, with a prior $\pi$ on $S$. For the trainer $U$ is the input $X$ and $W$ the label $Y$. For the attacker $U$ is the secret and $W$ its view, and shadow runs, which are Train calls, supply its samples of the pair. The reference is the product of the marginals, $\Pi_s^\circ=\Pi_{s,U}\otimes\Pi_{s,W}$. In sample access with an atomless law of $U$, draws from $\Pi^\circ_s$ have the law of the answers of a lazily sampled random function at fresh inputs, because no input repeats (proven). With chosen inputs and deterministic labels the reference is the random function itself (proven, the same argument). The random-oracle reference is therefore the product of marginals, and pseudorandomness for a class is independence as that class sees it. The product must be taken per instance. A reference with other marginals lets a test of one marginal pass for learning (proven, by any family whose label marginal differs from the reference's).

Item 10 of the reading of 26 October is already an instance. Its price of $\Theta(k/a^2)$ shadow runs is the minimax cost of learning a law on $k$ points to total variation $a$ (proven, a standard rate recalled from memory). The uninformed attacker of `def-uniform-security-profile` is a learner of its own view law.

"Told apart" has two readings. Distinguishing asks for a bit that separates $\Pi_s$ from $\Pi_s^\circ$. Weak learning asks for a predictor of $W$ from $U$ that beats the best constant, which is the best predictor under the reference. Weak learning gives distinguishing at the cost of one more statistic (proven). The converse depends on the loss. In log loss a statistic $\phi$ with gap $g$ yields the tilted law $\nu(y)e^{\lambda\phi(x,y)}/Z_\lambda(x)$, which gains $2g^2$ nats over the marginal $\nu$ (proven, by Hoeffding's lemma). In square loss only correlational statistics $y\,g(x)$ count. The law $Y=\langle u,X\rangle\xi$ from the refutation of `conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost` has $E[Y\mid X]=0$. No learner of any class gains on it in square loss, yet an SQ test detects it at generative exponent 2 (proven). For binary labels every statistic is correlational and the readings agree (proven). The distance between distinguishing and square-loss learning is the distance between the generative and the information exponent. For classes closed under hybrids, distinguishing equals predicting by Yao's next-bit theorem, at a loss of a factor $n$ in advantage (from memory, unverified). "Learnable exactly when not pseudorandom" is thus a known equivalence in log loss and false in square loss. For low-degree learners, detection and estimation have different thresholds (from memory, Schramm and Wein, whose library note SW22 is unread). The registered SQ gap between detection at $d^{k^*/2}$ and estimation at $d^{k^*/2+1}$ is of the same kind (`fact-generative-exponent-measures-detection`).

In each class of interest the notion is a known invariant (proven, `prop-class-pseudorandomness-collapse`). Unbounded learners meet the total variation of the mixture, SQ learners the statistical dimension of FGRVX17, and degree-$k$ single-sample tests the moment matching that defines $k^*$. Polynomial-time learners meet pseudorandom functions, weak ones under random examples (from memory). At the level of existence, universal learnability is the absence of one-way functions (`fact-pessiland-learning`). The family is pseudorandom for a class exactly when the constant predictor is outcome-indistinguishable for it (from memory, Dwork, Kim, Reingold, Rothblum and Yona). Its log-loss dual is V-information (`fact-xu-usable-information-definition`), and in circuits it is conditional pseudoentropy (from memory, Vadhan and Zheng). The notion separates SQ from polynomial time (`prop-blind-lll-label-recovery`), correlational from full SQ, and coarse from fine gradient learners (`fact-fine-precision-sgd-simulates-pac`). Each of these separations is already known (heuristic, assuming the To-read list holds no surprise). The single-party definition adds no content.

Content could live only in the comparison of two parties on one setup. In a learning one-way setup the trainer reaches its target from data, and the release attains that target. The view of the release is pseudorandom for an attacker class containing the trainer's, at a larger budget, for a target fixed by the trainer's target. Each condition blocks a trivialization. A constant release is pseudorandom, a weaker attacker class gives a gap of classes alone, and a target the trainer never needed is privacy, the topic trainer-as-filter. Comparing two tasks with a restricted-learner attacker is occupied in cryptographic units (`fact-restricted-class-attacker-occupied`), so only the learning units are open.

Extraction of a Bayes release is the case where the attacker's target is the trainer's own. One identity settles it. Let $R_\sigma$ be the law of $(X,m_P(X)+\sigma\xi)$ with $m_P(x)=E[Y\mid X=x]$. Then $E_{R_\sigma}[Y g(X)]=E_P[Y g(X)]$ for every $g$, so the release answers every correlational query as the data do. The square-loss gradient field of every architecture is the same under both laws (proven). A loss-matched learner pays exactly the same to extract as to train. An attacker class containing the trainer's can run the trainer on the answers. So no learning one-way setup of extraction type exists for square-loss population gradient trainers, with or without injected noise (proven).

Item 20 is a learning one-way setup only between an SQ trainer that reads $y^2$ and an SQ extractor. The extractor's exponent never exceeds the information exponent of the training law, $k^*(m_P)\le k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)$ (proven). $P_\natural$ attains the bound with $k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P_\natural)=4$. The extractor of item 20 pays what a correlational trainer of $P_\natural$ would pay (proven for the exponents, the trainer's correlational lower bound recalled from memory). Item 15 fails the third condition. The trainer targets labels, which do not involve the pancake direction. The separation is between a seed-known and a seed-blind attacker of one class, so the seed acts as a trapdoor. Without it the hardness is borrowed from the record law, or for Gaussian records it is statistical (`thm-blind-orbit-limit`). Neither is a gap of learner classes. The object worth keeping is a pair of prices of one class on two channels from one secret, the second produced by the trainer. "One-way" misleads, since extraction is re-learning and in the loss-matched world never costs more.

The SQ embedding of gradient learners (`fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd`) forgets the architecture. A finer handle keeps it. Let $\psi_\theta(x,y)$ be the per-example update and $\Delta_s(\theta)$ the gap of its mean between $\Pi_s$ and the reference. Noisy population gradient descent at noise scale $\varsigma$ moves its trajectory by at most $\sqrt{T\bar\kappa_f/(4\varsigma^2)}$ in average total variation, with $\bar\kappa_f=\sup_\theta E_s\|\Delta_s(\theta)\|^2$ (proven, `thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter`). The architecture enters only through its update class $\{\psi_\theta\}$. The bound binds every reader of the trajectory, so it is a filter theorem relative to the architecture, finer than `prop-learner-side-sq-transfer`. Sparse parities are computed by $k$ ReLU units, yet $\bar\kappa_f\le G^2/\binom nk$. So cheap to compute and hard to learn holds inside one architecture, unconditionally, at a price in steps proportional to $\varsigma^2\binom nk$ (proven, the argument of Shalev-Shwartz, Shamir and Shammah as I recall it). At a point where the model is the best constant, a gradient gap of norm $\tau$ gives one step of gain $\tau^2/(2\beta)$ for a $\beta$-smooth risk (proven). The price of the class lies between the gap at the start and the gap along the null trajectory. Closing that interval is the dynamics of information exponents, the open core (open).

Symmetry is a second handle. An $H$-equivariant learner has the same risk at $s$ as at every $hs$, so at every secret it pays at least the Bayes price of a secret drawn uniformly from the orbit (proven). Gradient descent from an isotropic Gaussian start is $O(d)$-equivariant (`fact-orthogonal-equivariance-of-training`). For it every fixed secret behaves as a random one on its orbit. This is the exact sense in which a random oracle enters a gradient learner. It holds at every precision in exact arithmetic, and coordinate-wise rounding or Adam break it.

Mini-batch noise is a channel that the update class misses (heuristic, assuming the Gaussian surrogate of a batch mean). The covariance of a batch update is a second-degree statistic of the data law. In square loss it contains $E[Y^2\,\cdot\,]$, which no correlational query sees. On $Y=\langle u,X\rangle\xi$ with a linear model the population drift equals the null's exactly, while the update covariance carries the spike $2uu^\top$ (proven, a two-line computation in Next). Gradient learners escape their update class here, and the escape closes only when injected noise dominates the batch covariance. This reading of the threshold $b\rho^2$ of AKMSS21 is heuristic.

Halil's thesis reads as follows here. A learner of time $t$ outputs a predictor of time at most $t$, so easy to learn implies cheap to compute (proven, trivially). In this frame simplicity of computation enters only by shrinking $\{\psi_\theta\}$, which enlarges the set of pseudorandom families. Simplicity restricts learning. That transformers learn real tasks becomes the claim that real tasks are not pseudorandom for their update class, an empirical statement that no theorem of the frame supplies (heuristic).

## Claims

### def-class-pseudorandom-channel: pseudorandomness of a channel family for a learner class
status: proven
uses: def-uniform-security-profile, thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views

Statement. Let $\{\Pi_s\}_{s\in S}$ be laws of $(U,W)$ on $\mathcal U\times\mathcal W$, $\pi$ a prior on $S$, and $\Pi_s^\circ=\Pi_{s,U}\otimes\Pi_{s,W}$. For a class $\mathcal A$ of oracle algorithms with budget $\beta$ in a named access mode (samples, statistical queries, chosen inputs, Train calls), put
$$\mathrm{Adv}_{\mathcal A,\beta}=\sup_{A\in\mathcal A_\beta}\Big|E_{s\sim\pi}\Pr[A^{\Pi_s}=1]-E_{s\sim\pi}\Pr[A^{\Pi_s^\circ}=1]\Big|.$$
The family is $(\mathcal A,\beta,\epsilon)$-pseudorandom when $\mathrm{Adv}_{\mathcal A,\beta}\le\epsilon$.
(a) If $\Pi_{s,U}$ is atomless, $n$ samples of $\Pi_s^\circ$ have the law of $n$ answers $(U_i,O(U_i))$ of a lazily sampled random function $O$ with values i.i.d. $\Pi_{s,W}$, at inputs $U_i$ i.i.d. $\Pi_{s,U}$.
(b) A learning one-way setup is a trainer class $\mathcal T$ at budget $\beta_T$, an attacker class $\mathcal A\supseteq\mathcal T$ at budget $\beta_A>\beta_T$, a family of data laws, a release map and a channel from release to view, such that (i) some $T\in\mathcal T_{\beta_T}$ reaches the trainer's target, (ii) the release attains it, and (iii) the view family is $(\mathcal A,\beta_A,\epsilon)$-pseudorandom for an attacker target that is a function of the trainer's target.

Proof. (a) The inputs are almost surely distinct, so the lazily sampled values are i.i.d. $\Pi_{s,W}$ and independent of the inputs. (b) is a definition. Dropping (ii) admits a constant release, dropping $\mathcal A\supseteq\mathcal T$ admits a gap of classes alone, and dropping the link in (iii) admits targets the trainer never needed.

### prop-distinguishing-versus-weak-learning: distinguishing equals log-loss learning, not square-loss learning
status: proven
uses: conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost, fact-xu-usable-information-definition, fact-generative-exponent-sq-recovery-bound

Statement. Let the family $\{P_s\}$ of laws of $(X,Y)$ have common marginals $\mu$ and $\nu$, known to the learner, and $P^\circ=\mu\otimes\nu$.
(a) For a loss $\ell\in[0,1]$ with baseline $\ell^\circ=\inf_c E_\nu\ell(c,Y)$, every $h$ has $E_{P^\circ}\ell(h(X),Y)\ge\ell^\circ$. A learner whose output has risk at most $\ell^\circ-\epsilon$ under $P_s$ with probability $1-\delta$ becomes, with one more query of tolerance $\epsilon/3$, a distinguisher of advantage at least $1-\delta$.
(b) If a deterministic SQ algorithm with $q$ queries in $[0,1]$ of tolerance $\tau$ outputs 1 on $P_s$ against every valid oracle and 0 on $P^\circ$, then $q$ queries of tolerance $\tau/4$ produce a conditional law $\hat p$ with $E_{P_s}\log(\hat p(Y\mid X)/\nu(Y))\ge\tau^2/2$.
(c) In square loss with $\mathrm{Var}\,Y<\infty$, a gain $\epsilon$ over the best constant forces $\mathrm{Cov}_{P_s}(Y,h(X))\ge\epsilon/2$, and $\mathrm{Cov}_{P_s}(Y,g(X))\ge\tau$ with $|g|\le1$ yields a gain of $\tau^2$. Every such covariance vanishes under $P^\circ$.
(d) For $Y=\langle u,X\rangle\xi$ with $X\sim N(0,I_d)$ and $\xi$ an independent sign, no predictor of any class gains in square loss, while the SQ detection price is that of generative exponent 2. For $\mathcal Y=\{\pm1\}$ the readings (a) to (c) coincide.
(e) The supremum of the log-loss gain over a predictive family containing $\nu$ is the V-information of that family.

Proof. (a) Under $P^\circ$ the risk of $h$ is $E_\mu E_\nu\ell(h(x),Y)\ge\ell^\circ$. Accept when the answer is at most $\ell^\circ-\epsilon/2$. (b) Answer each query with its $P^\circ$ value, computable from $\mu$ and $\nu$. This path is fixed. If no query on it had gap above $\tau$ under $P_s$, these answers would be valid for $P_s$ and the output would be 0. Query the $q$ path queries at tolerance $\tau/4$ and keep one whose estimate departs from its $P^\circ$ value by more than $3\tau/4$. Its gap $g$ is at least $\tau/2$, made positive by replacing $\phi$ with $1-\phi$. Hoeffding's lemma gives $\log E_\nu e^{\lambda\phi(x,Y)}\le\lambda E_\nu\phi(x,Y)+\lambda^2/8$, so the tilt gains at least $\lambda g-\lambda^2/8$, which is $2g^2\ge\tau^2/2$ at $\lambda=4g$. (c) The gain of $h$ is $2\,\mathrm{Cov}(Y,h)-E(h-EY)^2$. Take $h=EY+\tau(g-Eg)$. (d) $E[Y\mid X]=0$ and $Y^2=\langle u,X\rangle^2$, the exponent-2 law of the refutation of `conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost`. A binary-label statistic is $\phi(x,-1)+\frac{1+y}{2}(\phi(x,1)-\phi(x,-1))$, correlational. (e) The definitions registered in `fact-xu-usable-information-definition`.

### prop-class-pseudorandomness-collapse: the notion is a known invariant in each class
status: proven
uses: prop-learner-side-sq-transfer, fact-sq-low-degree-equivalence, fact-generative-exponent-sq-recovery-bound

Statement. Take the notation of `def-class-pseudorandom-channel` with common marginals and reference $P^\circ$.
(a) For unbounded algorithms with $n$ samples, $\mathrm{Adv}=\mathrm{TV}(E_sP_s^{\otimes n},(P^\circ)^{\otimes n})\le\frac12\big(E_{s,s'}(1+\chi_{P^\circ}(P_s,P_{s'}))^n-1\big)^{1/2}$, where $\chi_{P^\circ}(P,P')=E_{P^\circ}[(\frac{dP}{dP^\circ}-1)(\frac{dP'}{dP^\circ}-1)]$ and $s,s'$ are independent draws of $\pi$.
(b) For SQ algorithms with $q$ queries to VSTAT$(t)$, pseudorandomness is SQ hardness of the decision problem $B(\{P_s\},P^\circ)$. It is bounded through the statistical dimension with average correlation as in `prop-learner-side-sq-transfer`, and nearly equivalent to low-degree hardness under the condition of `fact-sq-low-degree-equivalence`.
(c) For a Gaussian single-index family with direction $u$ and the tests $T(y)q(x)$, $T$ square-integrable and $q$ a polynomial of degree below $k$, the family is exactly pseudorandom (tolerance zero) if and only if $k\le k^*(P)$.

Proof. (a) The likelihood-ratio test is optimal, and the bound is the second-moment computation with $\mathrm{TV}\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2}$. (b) is the definition read against FGRVX17 Theorems 3.7 and 3.13 as registered. (c) Write $X=Zu+X_\perp$ with $Z=\langle u,X\rangle$. Then $E[T(Y)q(X)]=E[T(Y)\tilde q(Z)]$ with $\tilde q(z)=E\,q(zu+X_\perp)$ of degree below $k$, and $E[T(Y)h_j(Z)]=0$ for all $T$ and $1\le j<k^*$ is the definition of $k^*$. For $k>k^*$ the test $q=h_{k^*}(\langle u,\cdot\rangle)$ with a suitable $T$ separates.

### thm-bayes-release-loss-matched-price: a Bayes release costs a loss-matched learner exactly what its data cost
status: proven
uses: thm-extraction-exponent-price, fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd

Statement. Let $P$ be a law of $(X,Y)$ with $EY^2<\infty$, $m_P(x)=E[Y\mid X=x]$, and $R_\sigma$ the law of $(X,m_P(X)+\sigma\xi)$, where $\xi$ is independent of $X$ with mean zero and unit variance.
(a) $E_{R_\sigma}g(X)=E_Pg(X)$ and $E_{R_\sigma}[Yg(X)]=E_P[Yg(X)]$ for every bounded $g$, and $m_{R_\sigma}=m_P$. A correlational oracle of tolerance $\tau$ for one law is one for the other.
(b) For every model $f_\theta$ with $E\|f_\theta(X)\nabla_\theta f_\theta(X)\|$ and $E\|Y\nabla_\theta f_\theta(X)\|$ finite, the square-loss population gradients under $P$ and $R_\sigma$ coincide at every $\theta$.
(c) For every family $\{P_s\}$, every correlational algorithm and every square-loss population gradient method, with or without data-independent noise, reaches excess risk $\varepsilon$ from $P_s$ exactly when it reaches fidelity error $E(h-m_{P_s})^2\le\varepsilon$ from $R_{\sigma,s}$, at the same budget.
(d) For $\mathcal Y=\{\pm1\}$, a release that answers with a sign of mean $m_P(x)$ has answer law $P$, so every class pays the same.

Proof. (a) The tower property and $E\xi=0$ with $\xi$ independent of $X$. (b) The gradient is $E[(f_\theta(X)-Y)\nabla_\theta f_\theta(X)]$, linear in $Y$ given $X$. (c) The excess square risk of $h$ under $P$ is $E(h-m_P)^2$, which is the fidelity error, and the oracles or gradient fields coincide by (a) and (b). (d) A sign of mean $m_P(x)$ has the conditional law of $Y$ given $X=x$. Finite batches break the identity only through $E[Y^2\mid X]$ against $m_P^2+\sigma^2$, a statistic outside the correlational class.

### prop-extraction-gap-information-exponent: the extraction gap of item 20 is the information exponent of the label law
status: proven
uses: thm-extraction-exponent-price, lem-level-set-moment-balance, fact-generative-exponent-post-processing-monotone, thm-blind-split-seed-separation, thm-blind-orbit-limit

Statement. Let $P$ be a Gaussian single-index law with direction $u$, $Z=\langle u,X\rangle$, $EY^2<\infty$, $h_k$ the normalized Hermite polynomials, and $k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)=\min\{k\ge1\mid E[Yh_k(Z)]\ne0\}$.
(a) $k^*(P)\le k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)$ and $k^*(Q_{m_P,\sigma})\le k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)$.
(b) In `thm-extraction-exponent-price` the SQ extractor's exponent $k^*(m_P)/2$ is at most $k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)/2$, and its ratio to the SQ trainer's exponent is at most $k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P)/k^*(P)$.
(c) $P_\natural$ attains the bound. $E[m_\natural(Z)h_j(Z)]=0$ for $j=1,2,3$ and $E[m_\natural(Z)h_4(Z)]=-1/\sqrt{48\pi}$, so $k_{\mathrm{IE}}(P_\natural)=k^*(m_\natural)=4=2k^*(P_\natural)$.
(d) The setup of `thm-blind-split-seed-separation` violates condition (iii) of `def-class-pseudorandom-channel` (b), since the trainer's target does not determine the pancake direction.

Proof. (a) If $E[h_k(Z)\mid Y]=0$ almost surely then $E[Yh_k(Z)]=0$, so the first $k$ with $E[Yh_k(Z)]\ne0$ is at least $k^*(P)$. For $Y'=m_P(Z)+\sigma\xi$, $E[Y'h_k(Z)]=E[m_P(Z)h_k(Z)]=E[Yh_k(Z)]$, and the same implication applies. (b) follows from (a) and the exponents of `thm-extraction-exponent-price`. (c) $E[\lvert Z\rvert^pe^{-Z^2/2}]=\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})/\sqrt{2\pi}$. Evenness of $m_\natural$ removes odd $j$. For $j=2$ the value is proportional to $\Gamma(2)-\Gamma(1)=0$. For $j=4$ it is $(\Gamma(3)-6\Gamma(2)+3\Gamma(1))/\sqrt{48\pi}$. A quadrature check matched both values to ten digits (empirical, a scratch computation). $k^*(m_\natural)=4$ is `lem-level-set-moment-balance` and $k^*(P_\natural)=2$ is part (d) of `thm-extraction-exponent-price`. (d) by inspection of the protocol class.

### thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter: noisy gradient training sees a family only through its update class
status: proven
uses: fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd, fact-fine-precision-sgd-simulates-pac, prop-learner-side-sq-transfer, fact-sgd-parity-near-sq-limit

Statement. Fix a model $f_\theta$ with $\theta\in\mathbb R^p$, a per-example update $\psi_\theta(x,y)\in\mathbb R^p$, a family $\{P_s\}$ with common marginals, $\Delta_s(\theta)=E_{P_s}\psi_\theta-E_{P^\circ}\psi_\theta$ and $\bar\kappa_f=\sup_\theta E_{s\sim\pi}\|\Delta_s(\theta)\|_2^2$. Noisy population gradient descent draws $\theta_0$ from a law free of the data and sets $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t(E_P\psi_{\theta_t}+\zeta_t)$, with fixed $\eta_t>0$ and $\zeta_t\sim N(0,\varsigma^2I_p)$ i.i.d.
(a) $E_{s\sim\pi}\mathrm{TV}\big(\mathcal L(\theta_{0:T}\mid P^\circ),\mathcal L(\theta_{0:T}\mid P_s)\big)\le\sqrt{T\bar\kappa_f/(4\varsigma^2)}$.
(b) Every map of $\theta_{0:T}$, unbounded and white box, has average advantage at most this bound in telling $P_s$ from $P^\circ$.
(c) Let $X$ be uniform on $\{\pm1\}^n$, $Y=\chi_S(X)$ with $S$ uniform over $k$-sets, $P^\circ$ with $Y$ uniform and independent, and $\|\psi_\theta\|_2\le G$. Then $\bar\kappa_f\le G^2/\binom nk$, so average advantage $1/3$ needs $T\ge4\varsigma^2\binom nk/(9G^2)$. Every $\chi_S$ is computed by one hidden layer of $k$ ReLU units.
(d) Let $\psi=\nabla_\theta\ell$, let $f_{\theta^\circ}$ be the constant minimizing $E_\nu\ell(c,Y)$, with minimum $\ell^\circ$ and $\nabla L_{P^\circ}(\theta^\circ)=0$, and let $L_{P_s}$ be $\beta$-smooth. Then $L_{P_s}(\theta^\circ-\beta^{-1}\nabla L_{P_s}(\theta^\circ))\le\ell^\circ-\|\Delta_s(\theta^\circ)\|^2/(2\beta)$.

Proof. (a) The initial laws agree. The kernels are Gaussian with covariance $\eta_t^2\varsigma^2I$ and means apart by $\eta_t\Delta_s(\theta_t)$. The chain rule gives $\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal L(\cdot\mid P^\circ)\,\|\,\mathcal L(\cdot\mid P_s))=\sum_{t<T}E^\circ\|\Delta_s(\theta_t)\|^2/(2\varsigma^2)$, the expectation over the null trajectory, whose law is free of $s$. Averaging over $s$ inside gives at most $T\bar\kappa_f/(2\varsigma^2)$, and Pinsker with Jensen finish. (b) Data processing for total variation, and convexity for the mixture. (c) For binary $y$, $\psi_\theta=a_\theta(x)+y\,b_\theta(x)$ with $\|b_\theta\|\le G$, and $\Delta_S(\theta)=\hat b_\theta(S)$, the vector of Fourier coefficients at $S$. Parseval gives $\sum_{|S|=k}\|\hat b_\theta(S)\|^2\le E\|b_\theta(X)\|^2\le G^2$. With $r=\sum_{i\in S}(1-x_i)/2$, the network $1+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_j\mathrm{ReLU}(r-j)$ with $c_0=-2$ and $c_j=4(-1)^{j+1}$ equals $(-1)^r=\chi_S(x)$ on $r\in\{0,\dots,k\}$. (d) $L_{P_s}(\theta^\circ)=\ell^\circ$ because $Y$ has law $\nu$ under $P_s$, $\nabla L_{P_s}(\theta^\circ)=\Delta_s(\theta^\circ)$, and the descent lemma applies.

### prop-equivariant-learner-orbit-prior: an equivariant learner faces a random secret on the orbit
status: proven
uses: fact-orthogonal-equivariance-of-training, lem-equivariant-view-coupling, thm-blind-orbit-limit

Statement. Let a compact group $H$ with Haar law $\lambda$ act on $\mathcal X$, on $S$ with $P_{hs}=(h\times\mathrm{id})_\#P_s$, and on predictors by $(h\cdot g)(x)=g(h^{-1}x)$. Let a learner $A$ on $n$ samples satisfy $A(hD)\stackrel{d}{=}h\cdot A(D)$, and let $r(g,s)=E_{P_s}\ell(g(X),Y)$. Then for every $s$, $E\,r(A(D_s),s)=\int E\,r(A(D_{hs}),hs)\,\lambda(dh)$, the Bayes risk of $A$ under the orbit prior of $s$. So at every secret an equivariant learner pays at least the Bayes price of a secret drawn from $\lambda$ on its orbit.

Proof. $D_{hs}\stackrel{d}{=}hD_s$, hence $A(D_{hs})\stackrel{d}{=}h\cdot A(D_s)$, and $r(h\cdot g,hs)=E_{P_s}\ell(g(h^{-1}hX),Y)=r(g,s)$. The risk is constant on the orbit and equals its average. Gradient descent from an isotropic Gaussian start is $O(d)$-equivariant (`fact-orthogonal-equivariance-of-training`), so a sparse direction costs it what a uniform one costs. `thm-blind-orbit-limit` is the same averaging for the seed-blind server. Equality with the best learner's Bayes risk is the Hunt-Stein theorem, recalled from memory.

## Pushbacks on the frame

1. Drop the single-party definition as a contribution. It is outcome indistinguishability of the constant predictor, its log-loss side is V-information, the equivalence with prediction is Yao's, and for SQ it is FGRVX17. Keep it as notation that gives the parties one type, and run the proposed search before any sentence calls it new.
2. "A target is learnable exactly when it is not pseudorandom" is false in square loss (`prop-distinguishing-versus-weak-learning` (d)). Name the loss, or use log loss.
3. "A secret is safe exactly when its channel is pseudorandom" holds in one direction. Recovery implies detection only through a search-to-decision reduction, which the theory has for rotation-equivariant families (`lem-agreement-detection`) and lacks in general (`prob-search-to-decision-converse`). Detection and recovery prices differ (`fact-generative-exponent-measures-detection`). A secret on one record is a two-point problem, where class restrictions are weak.
4. Drop the name learning one-way function. Item 20 is not one for gradient trainers, and its gap is the distance between the information and the generative exponent of the label law. Item 15 is a trapdoor whose hardness is borrowed or statistical.
5. A fixed architecture is not a class closed under composition, and class-relative notions lose post-processing without closure (`fact-mprv09-class-relative-cdp-price`, `fact-capacity-bounded-dp-function-class`, `fact-xu-data-processing-fails`). The update class with a noise scale composes along the trainer. Along an attacker of another architecture it composes only through SQ.
6. The auditor is not a party of the same type. Its target is a distance from the reference, an estimation problem, and items 8 and 9 already price it.
7. The trainer as a filter fails on update channels. A trainer whose drift learns nothing still leaks through its batch noise (heuristic, the statement of Next).
8. The random oracle is canonical only as the product of marginals. For a representation channel the structureless record law, the NGCA null, is a modelling choice that each result must state.

Run one-pass SGD from $\theta_0=0$ with batch $b\ge1$, step $\eta\le1/d$ and injected noise $N(0,\varsigma^2I_d)$ on the linear model $f_\theta(x)=\langle\theta,x\rangle$ in square loss. The per-example update $(\langle\theta,x\rangle-y)x$ has mean $\theta$ under both $P_u$ and the reference, and covariance $(1+\|\theta\|^2)I+\theta\theta^\top+2uu^\top$ under $P_u$ against $(1+\|\theta\|^2)I+\theta\theta^\top$ under the reference. The claim has two parts. (a) The population drift is free of $u$, so noisy population gradient descent releases a model whose law is free of $u$, by `thm-noisy-gradient-query-filter` with $\bar\kappa_f=0$. (b) An observer of the first $T$ updates recovers $u$ to correlation $1/2$ with probability $1-o(1)$ once $T\ge C\,d\log d\,(1+b\varsigma^2)^2$, for every $b$, and no estimator from the updates reaches correlation bounded away from zero when $T\le c\,d\,(1+b\varsigma^2)^2$. This would show that a gradient trainer which learns nothing about a secret leaks it through its batch noise, and it would read the threshold $b\rho^2$ of AKMSS21 as the point where a second-degree channel closes.
