# Batching removes the class, round 01

The verdict first. The theorem of round 04 is right in its mechanism and wrong in two constants, and its proof plan rests on a step that is false as stated. Batching does remove the attacker's class from the price, and the exact sense is this. The batched update tends to a Gaussian spiked law, in that law the empirical covariance is sufficient for the secret, so the statistic class of the linear attacker is every observer there, and at fixed dimension every price of the stream converges to its value in that law (proven). In the Gaussian law the linear attacker and every observer share one threshold in the proportional limit (proven, given two random-matrix facts cited from memory). The two constants. The linear attacker's price at overlap one half is $d(4+\beta)/(3\beta^2)$ and not $d/\beta^2$, which is the detection threshold. The first-order batch correction has three terms, the round-04 coefficient is the large-noise limit of one of them, and on a test law it is three times the measured value. The false step is the claim that the per-update chi-square of the true law and of its Gaussian surrogate differ at order $1/b^2$. They differ at order $1/b$ through the fourth cumulant, and the surrogate's chi-square against the null is infinite on every law whose batches can carry a spike above one. The proof now goes through total variation and Kullback divergence and needs no rate. Nothing is proven in the proportional regime at fixed batch beyond a constant, which is where the author expected the failure. Clause 13's three eigenvalues are right, and I prove the closed form of the top centred Haar eigenvalue at every degree, so the heuristic above degree three becomes a theorem. Items refer to `readings/2026-10-29-what-we-have.md`, clauses to `statement/final.md` of the first discussion, and scripts to `batching-removes-class/checks/`, whose outputs sit beside them as `.out` files.

## 1. The theorem

I keep the slug. The content is the theorem of round 04, batching makes the linear attacker optimal, restated with the mechanism named, two constants corrected, one claim withdrawn and one added.

### thm-batching-removes-the-class: batching makes the empirical covariance sufficient and the linear attacker optimal
status: sketch
uses: def-optimum-update-stream, def-statistic-learner-class, def-learning-attacker, thm-update-direction-chi-square-bound, prop-update-direction-fano-recovery, thm-norm-weighted-update-pca, conj-linear-class-reads-cauchy-transform, fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration

Setting. The update stream of `def-optimum-update-stream` with per-example noise. Inputs $X\sim\gamma_d$, the secret $u$ uniform on $S^{d-1}$, $Z=\langle u,X\rangle$. Labels $Y=\xi W$ with $\xi$ a uniform sign independent of $(X,W)$, $W\in[0,A]$, the law of $W$ given $X$ a function of $Z$, and $\sup_w\mathbb E[Z^4\mid W=w]<\infty$. Write $T(w)=\mathbb E[Z^2-1\mid W=w]$, $\mu_2=\mathbb EW^2$, $c_2=\mathbb E[W^2T(W)]=\mathbb E[W^2(Z^2-1)]$, and assume $c_2>0$. A client averages a batch of $b$ examples with per-example noise $\sigma_0>0$, so its update is $v=b^{-1}\sum_{i\le b}(Y_iX_i+\sigma_0\zeta_i)$ with $\zeta_i\sim N(0,I_d)$ independent. Then $\mathrm{Cov}(v)=b^{-1}(DI_d+c_2uu^\top)$ with $D=\mu_2+\sigma_0^2$, and the spike-to-bulk ratio $\beta=c_2/D$ does not depend on $b$ (proven, property (i) of the definition with $\varsigma^2=\sigma_0^2/b$). The attacker observes $T$ independent updates $v_1,\dots,v_T$ of one round and $\hat M=T^{-1}\sum_tv_tv_t^\top$ is their empirical covariance.

Three classes, each closed under the choice of the attacker's own coins. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}$ is the declared learner of `def-learning-attacker` with family the unit sphere, the square reconstruction loss of a linear autoencoder trained to its optimum, and readout its loading, which is the top eigenvector of $\hat M$, the linear VAE of item 32. $\mathcal A_{\hat M}$ is the statistic class of `def-statistic-learner-class` with $S=\hat M$, every function of $\hat M$ and independent coins. $\mathcal A_*$ is every estimator. The success of an estimator at budget $T$ is $s=\inf_u\Pr_u[|\langle\hat u,u\rangle|\ge\frac12]$, the success $s_{\mathcal C}(T;b,d)$ of a class is the supremum over its members, and the price $p_{\mathcal C}(b,d)$ is the least $T$ with $s_{\mathcal C}(T;b,d)\ge\frac23$. The Gaussian spiked model $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ has $v_t$ independent $N(0,I_d+\beta uu^\top)$. Its successes and prices are $s^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(T;d)$ and $p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(d)$, $p^{\mathrm G+}_{\mathcal C}(d)$ is the least $T$ with $s^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(T;d)>\frac23$, and $r(d)=p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathrm{lin}}(d)/p^{\mathrm G}_*(d)$. Put $\gamma_\beta=(4+\beta)/(3\beta^2)$ and $\bar K=\frac12\big(A^2\sup_w|T(w)|/\sigma_0^2+\log(1+A^2/\sigma_0^2)\big)$.

Statement.

(a) Sufficiency. In $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ the statistic $\hat M$ is sufficient for $u$, so $\mathcal A_*$ and $\mathcal A_{\hat M}$ have the same success at every budget (proven). In the stream at any finite $b$ the likelihood depends on the updates through the pairs $(\|v_t\|^2,\langle u,v_t\rangle)$ and $\hat M$ is not sufficient (proven). On the law $Q_0$ at $b=1$ the two classes differ, PCA at or below overlap $0.09$ while an estimator outside $\mathcal A_{\hat M}$ reaches $0.86$ at $T=40d$, $d=200$ (empirical, item 32).

(b) Fixed dimension. For every $d\ge2$, every $T$ and every class $\mathcal C$ among the three, $s_{\mathcal C}(T;b,d)\to s^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(T;d)$ as $b\to\infty$, and
$$p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(d)\le\liminf_{b\to\infty}p_{\mathcal C}(b,d)\le\limsup_{b\to\infty}p_{\mathcal C}(b,d)\le p^{\mathrm G+}_{\mathcal C}(d)$$
(proven). When no Gaussian success at any budget equals $\frac23$ exactly, $p_{\mathcal C}(b,d)\to p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(d)$ for each class and $p_{\mathrm{lin}}(b,d)/p_*(b,d)\to r(d)$.

(c) The Gaussian ratio. Let $0<\beta\le1$. In $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$, $p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathrm{lin}}(d)/d\to\gamma_\beta$ and $p^{\mathrm G}_*(d)/d\to\gamma_\beta$ as $d\to\infty$, so $r(d)\to1$ (proven given two facts from memory, the overlap limit of the top sample eigenvector and the delocalization of the other eigenvectors, both stated in step 7). The overlap-one-half price $\gamma_\beta d$ exceeds the detection threshold $d/\beta^2$ by the factor $(4+\beta)/3$, between $\frac43$ and $\frac53$.

(d) Every batch. For all $b\ge1$ and $d\ge48$,
$$p_*(b,d)\ge\frac{d}{48\bar K},\qquad p_{\mathrm{lin}}(b,d)\le1.04\,C_1\,d\,\frac{(A^2+\sigma_0^2)^2(D+c_2)^2}{D^2c_2^2},$$
with $C_1$ the absolute constant of `thm-norm-weighted-update-pca`, so $p_{\mathrm{lin}}(b,d)/p_*(b,d)\le50\,C_1\bar K(A^2+\sigma_0^2)^2(D+c_2)^2/(D^2c_2^2)$, a constant of the law alone (proven).

(e) First order. Let $\kappa(b)=\mathbb E_a\chi^2(\nu_a\|\gamma_1)$ be the per-update chi-square of `thm-update-direction-chi-square-bound` (b) at batch $b$, with $a=(W_i)_{i\le b}$ the batch's magnitudes, $\nu_a$ the normalized along-$u$ law given $a$, and $\epsilon_a=b^{-2}\sum_iW_i^2T(W_i)/\sigma(a)^2$ the batch's own spike, $\sigma(a)^2=b^{-2}\sum_iW_i^2+\sigma_0^2/b$. Write $\chi_{\mathrm G}(\epsilon)=(1-\epsilon^2)^{-1/2}-1$, the chi-square of $N(0,1+\epsilon)$ against $N(0,1)$, finite for $\epsilon<1$. Then formally
$$\kappa(b)=\chi_{\mathrm G}(\beta)+\frac{C_1'}{b}+O(b^{-2}),\qquad C_1'=\frac{\chi_{\mathrm G}''(\beta)}{2}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(W^2T(W)-\beta W^2)}{D^2}+\chi_{\mathrm G}'(\beta)\frac{\beta\mathrm{Var}(W^2)-\mathrm{Cov}(W^2T(W),W^2)}{D^2}+\frac{\beta^2}{4(1-\beta^2)^{5/2}}\frac{\bar\kappa_4}{D^2},$$
with $\bar\kappa_4=\mathbb E[W^4(\mathbb E[Z^4\mid W]-3\mathbb E[Z^2\mid W]^2)]$ (heuristic, a formal expansion whose remainder is not controlled). The round-04 coefficient, $\chi_{\mathrm G}(\beta)\mathrm{Var}(W^2T(W))/c_2^2$, is the first term's limit as $\sigma_0\to\infty$. On the law $W=|Z|\mathbf 1\{|Z|\le2\}$ with $\sigma_0^2=\mu_2$, where $\beta=0.415$ and $\chi_{\mathrm G}(\beta)=0.0992$, the round-04 coefficient is $0.968$, the three terms are $+1.269$, $-0.294$ and $-0.590$ with sum $0.385$, and the measured $(\kappa(b)-\chi_{\mathrm G}(\beta))\,b$ is $0.33$, $0.33$, $0.31$ and $0.30$ at $b=6,8,10,12$ with standard errors from $0.015$ to $0.06$ (empirical, `checks/kappa_batch.py`, between $1200$ and $20000$ batches sampled per $b$, the chi-square exact given each batch).

Proof.

Step 1, the conditional structure (proven). Write $X_i=Z_iu+X_{i\perp}$. Given $a=(W_i)$ and $(\xi_i,Z_i)_{i\le b}$, the component of $v$ orthogonal to $u$ is $b^{-1}\sum_i\xi_iW_iX_{i\perp}+\sigma_0b^{-1}\sum_iP_\perp\zeta_i\sim N(0,\sigma(a)^2P_\perp)$, independent of the component along $u$, which is $m+\sigma_0b^{-1}\sum_i\langle u,\zeta_i\rangle$ with $m=b^{-1}\sum_i\xi_iW_iZ_i$. This is step 1 of `thm-update-direction-chi-square-bound` with $\varsigma^2=\sigma_0^2/b$. The density of one update given $u$ is therefore
$$p_u(v)=\mathbb E_a\Big[\sigma(a)^{-(d-1)}\varphi_{d-1}\big(P_\perp v/\sigma(a)\big)\,f_a(\langle u,v\rangle)\Big],$$
with $f_a$ the density of the along-$u$ component given $a$ and $\|P_\perp v\|^2=\|v\|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2$, so the likelihood of $v_{1:T}$ depends on the data through the pairs $(\|v_t\|^2,\langle u,v_t\rangle)_t$, as functions of $u$. The likelihood of $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ is $\exp\big(-\frac T2\mathrm{tr}\hat M+\frac{T\beta}{2(1+\beta)}u^\top\hat Mu\big)$ up to a constant, which depends on the data through $\hat M$ alone. So $\hat M$ is sufficient in $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ and is not sufficient in the stream unless $f_a$ and $\sigma(a)$ are free of $a$, which $c_2>0$ with $W$ non-constant excludes.

Step 2, sufficiency gives the class equality in (a) (proven). Given a sufficient statistic, the conditional law of $v_{1:T}$ given $\hat M$ is free of $u$, so an estimator $F(v_{1:T},\omega)$ has a twin $F'(\hat M,\omega')$ that draws $\tilde v_{1:T}$ from that conditional law with its own coins and returns $F(\tilde v_{1:T},\omega)$. The pair $(u,F')$ has the law of $(u,F)$ at every $u$, so $F'\in\mathcal A_{\hat M}$ has the success of $F$.

Step 3, total variation at fixed $d$ (proven). Let $Q_u=N(0,DI_d+c_2uu^\top)$ and $\mathrm{TV}_b=\mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L(\sqrt b\,v\mid u),Q_u)$, which does not depend on $u$ by rotation covariance of both laws. By convexity, $\mathrm{TV}_b\le\mathbb E_a\mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L(\sqrt bv\mid u,a),Q_u)$. Given $a$, both laws are products of an along-$u$ law and an orthogonal Gaussian, and the total variation of a product is at most the sum of the two total variations. The orthogonal part is $N(0,b\sigma(a)^2P_\perp)$ against $N(0,DP_\perp)$, and $b\sigma(a)^2=b^{-1}\sum_iW_i^2+\sigma_0^2\to D$ almost surely by the strong law, so that total variation tends to zero almost surely at fixed $d$. The along-$u$ part is the law of $b^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_iW_iZ_i+\sigma_0N$ against $N(0,D(1+\beta))$. Given $a$, the summands $\xi_iW_iZ_i$ are independent and symmetric with variances $W_i^2(1+T(W_i))$, whose average tends to $\mathbb E[W^2Z^2]=\mu_2+c_2=D(1+\beta)-\sigma_0^2$ almost surely, and Lindeberg's condition holds almost surely because $b^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb E[W_i^2Z_i^2\mathbf 1\{W_i|Z_i|>\epsilon\sqrt b\}\mid W_i]\le A^4\sup_w\mathbb E[Z^4\mid W=w]/(\epsilon^2b)$. So $b^{-1/2}\sum_i\xi_iW_iZ_i$ converges in law to $N(0,\mu_2+c_2)$ almost surely in $a$. Convolution with the fixed Gaussian $N(0,\sigma_0^2)$ turns convergence in law into convergence in total variation, since the densities $\int\varphi_{\sigma_0}(x-y)\mu_b(dy)$ converge pointwise to $\int\varphi_{\sigma_0}(x-y)\mu(dy)$ and Scheffé's lemma gives $L^1$ convergence. Dominated convergence over $a$, with the bound one, gives $\mathrm{TV}_b\to0$.

Step 4, part (b) (proven). For a fixed estimator $F$ and a fixed $T$, its success under the stream and under $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ differ by at most $\sup_u\mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L(v_{1:T}\mid u),Q_u^{\otimes T})\le T\,\mathrm{TV}_b$, since scaling by $\sqrt b$ changes no estimator's success and the three classes are scale-free. The supremum over a class moves by at most the same amount. So $s_{\mathcal C}(T;b,d)\to s^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(T;d)$ for every $T$. For each $T<p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathcal C}(d)$ the Gaussian success is below $\frac23$, so the stream's success is below $\frac23$ for all large $b$, and there are finitely many such $T$, which gives the lower side. At $T=p^{\mathrm G+}_{\mathcal C}(d)$ the Gaussian success is above $\frac23$, so the stream's is for all large $b$, which gives the upper side. The prices are finite by (d).

Step 5, the Gaussian spiked model, every estimator against the top eigenvector (proven). Work in $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ with $T\ge d$, where $\hat M$ has simple spectrum almost surely. Let $F$ be any estimator with values in $S^{d-1}$, a function of $\hat M$ and coins by step 2, and let $\bar F=R^\top F(R\hat MR^\top,\omega)$ with $R$ Haar on $O(d)$ and independent. Under $P_u$ the matrix $R\hat MR^\top$ has the law of $\hat M$ under $P_{Ru}$, so $\Pr_u[|\langle\bar F,u\rangle|\ge\frac12]=\mathbb E_R\Pr_{Ru}[|\langle F,Ru\rangle|\ge\frac12]$, the average success of $F$ over a uniform secret, which is at least its success. By Haar invariance, for fixed $Q\in O(d)$ the conditional law of $\bar F$ given $\hat M=Q\hat M_0Q^\top$ is the image under $Q$ of its conditional law given $\hat M_0$. Taking $Q=J$ a reflection in the eigenbasis $e_1,\dots,e_d$ of $\hat M$, which fixes $\hat M$, the conditional law of $\bar F$ given $\hat M$ is invariant under every sign change of its coordinates in that basis. So, given $\hat M$, $\bar F=\sum_k\epsilon_k|\bar F_k|e_k$ with $(\epsilon_k)$ independent uniform signs independent of $(|\bar F_k|)_k$, and $\bar F$ is independent of $u$ given $\hat M$. Hence, with $\sum_k\bar F_k^2=1$ and Hoeffding's inequality on the Rademacher sum over $k\ge2$, for every $t>0$ and $\eta>0$,
$$\Pr_u\big[|\langle\bar F,u\rangle|\ge\tfrac12\big]\le\Pr_u\big[|\langle e_1,u\rangle|\ge\tfrac12-t\big]+\Pr_u\big[\max_{k\ge2}|\langle e_k,u\rangle|>\eta\big]+2e^{-t^2/(2\eta^2)}.$$
This is part (b1) of `conj-linear-class-reads-cauchy-transform` in the threshold form, with the sign structure made explicit.

Step 6, monotonicity (proven). $s_*(T)$ is non-decreasing in $T$, since an estimator at budget $T$ is one at every larger budget that ignores the extra updates. The same holds for $s_{\hat M}$ when the attacker may discard updates before forming $\hat M$, and nothing below uses it for $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}$.

Step 7, part (c) (proven given the two facts). Fact one, from memory, unverified. In $\mathrm G_\beta(d)$ with $d/T\to c<\beta^2$, the squared overlap of the top eigenvector of $\hat M$ with $u$ converges in probability to $(1-c/\beta^2)/(1+c/\beta)$, and to zero for $c\ge\beta^2$ (Paul, Statistica Sinica 2007, and Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi, Advances in Mathematics 2011, theorem numbers not recalled). Fact two, from memory, unverified. In the same limit $\max_{k\ge2}\langle e_k,u\rangle^2\to0$ in probability (eigenvector delocalization for spiked sample covariance matrices, Bloemendal, Knowles, Yau and Yin, Probability Theory and Related Fields 2016, theorem number not recalled). `checks/spiked_overlap.py` at $d=400$ and $\beta\in\{0.4,0.7,1\}$ finds the mean squared overlap within $0.03$ of fact one at four ratios, the success at overlap one half near one half at $T=\gamma_\beta d$, $14$, $9$ and $9$ of $20$, and the largest bulk squared overlap between $0.02$ and $0.09$, falling with $T/d$ (empirical). Setting the overlap limit to $\frac14$ gives $c=3\beta^2/(4+\beta)$, that is $T/d=\gamma_\beta$. Upper side. At $T=\lfloor(\gamma_\beta+\epsilon)d\rfloor$ the top eigenvector's squared overlap tends to a limit above $\frac14$, so $s_{\mathrm{lin}}\to1$ and $p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathrm{lin}}(d)\le(\gamma_\beta+\epsilon)d$ for large $d$. Lower side. Fix $\gamma\in(1,\gamma_\beta)$, which exists since $\gamma_\beta\ge\frac53$ for $\beta\le1$, and $t>0$ with $\sqrt{(1-1/(\gamma\beta^2))_+/(1+1/(\gamma\beta))}<\frac12-2t$. At $T=\lfloor\gamma d\rfloor$ the three terms of step 5 tend to zero by fact one, fact two with $\eta=\eta_d\to0$ slowly enough, and $2e^{-t^2/(2\eta_d^2)}\to0$. So $s_*(\lfloor\gamma d\rfloor)\to0$, by step 6 every $T\le\gamma d$ has $s_*<\frac23$ for large $d$, and $p^{\mathrm G}_*(d)\ge\gamma d$. Since $p^{\mathrm G}_*\le p^{\mathrm G}_{\mathrm{lin}}$, both lie in $[\gamma d,(\gamma_\beta+\epsilon)d]$ for large $d$, and $\gamma$ and $\epsilon$ are arbitrary.

Step 8, part (d) (proven). Upper side. The proof of `thm-norm-weighted-update-pca` (b) with $g\equiv1$ runs at every batch. Given the batch's labels, $\langle w,v\rangle$ for unit $w$ is $N(0,b^{-2}\sum_iY_i^2+\sigma_0^2/b)$ with variance at most $(A^2+\sigma_0^2)/b$, so $\mathbb Ee^{\lambda\langle w,v\rangle}\le e^{\lambda^2(A^2+\sigma_0^2)/(2b)}$ and the whitened rows $z_t=M^{-1/2}v_t$ have $\psi_2$ norm $K\le c_0\sqrt{(A^2+\sigma_0^2)/D}$, free of $b$, since $\lambda_{\min}(M)=D/b$. The registered concentration fact and steps 3 and 4 of that proof give $|\langle\hat u,u\rangle|\ge\frac12$ with probability $1-\vartheta$ once $T\ge C_1(d+\log(2/\vartheta))(A^2+\sigma_0^2)^2(D+c_2)^2/(D^2c_2^2)$, with $\lambda_{\max}(M)=(D+c_2)/b$ and $\gamma_g=c_2/b$ cancelling in the ratio. At $\vartheta=\frac13$ and $d\ge48$, $d+\log6\le1.04\,d$. Lower side. `prop-update-direction-fano-recovery` at batch $b$ gives $p_*(b,d)\ge(d/24-0.93)/\kappa_{\mathrm{KL}}(b)$ with $\kappa_{\mathrm{KL}}(b)=\mathbb E_a\mathrm{KL}(\nu_a\|\gamma_1)\le\mathbb E_aK(a)$ and
$$K(a)=\frac12\Big(\frac{b^{-2}\sum_iW_i^2T(W_i)}{\sigma(a)^2}+\log\frac{\sigma(a)^2}{\sigma_0^2/b}\Big)\le\frac12\Big(\frac{A^2\sup_w|T(w)|}{\sigma_0^2}+\log\Big(1+\frac{A^2}{\sigma_0^2}\Big)\Big)=\bar K,$$
since $\sigma(a)^2\ge\sigma_0^2/b$ and $b^{-1}\sum_iW_i^2\le A^2$. For $d\ge48$, $d/24-0.93\ge d/48$.

Step 9, part (e) (heuristic). Write $\chi^2(\nu_a\|\gamma_1)=\sum_{k\ge2\text{ even}}\hat c_k(a)^2$ with $\hat c_k(a)=\mathbb E_{\nu_a}h_k$, by Parseval and the symmetry of $\nu_a$. Exactly, $\hat c_2(a)=\epsilon_a/\sqrt2$. The fourth cumulant of $\nu_a$ is $\kappa_4(a)=b^{-4}\sum_iW_i^4(\mathbb E[Z^4\mid W_i]-3\mathbb E[Z^2\mid W_i]^2)/\sigma(a)^4$, of order $\bar\kappa_4/(bD^2)$, and the higher cumulants are of order $b^{-2}$ and smaller. A law with variance $1+\epsilon$ and fourth cumulant $\kappa_4$ has density $\nu_{\mathrm G}+\frac{\kappa_4}{24}\nu_{\mathrm G}''''$ to first order, with $\nu_{\mathrm G}=N(0,1+\epsilon)$, and the first variation of the chi-square against $\gamma_1$ is $2\int(\nu_{\mathrm G}/\gamma_1)\,\delta\nu=\frac{\kappa_4}{12}\int\partial^4(\nu_{\mathrm G}/\gamma_1)\,\nu_{\mathrm G}$. With $\nu_{\mathrm G}/\gamma_1=(1+\epsilon)^{-1/2}e^{\alpha x^2/2}$, $\alpha=\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)$, four derivatives and two Gaussian moments give $\kappa_4\,\epsilon^2/(4(1-\epsilon^2)^{5/2})$, which is the third term of $C_1'$ after $\kappa_4(a)\approx\bar\kappa_4/(bD^2)$ and $\epsilon_a\approx\beta$. Its series $\kappa_4(\epsilon^2/4+5\epsilon^4/8+\dots)$ agrees with the direct terms $(\hat c_4^2-\hat c_4^{\mathrm G\,2})+(\hat c_6^2-\hat c_6^{\mathrm G\,2})$ computed from the moment-cumulant relations, which is my check of the constant. The other two terms are the second-order delta method on $\mathbb E_a\chi_{\mathrm G}(\epsilon_a)$ with $\epsilon_a$ a ratio of two batch means, $\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon_a)\approx\mathrm{Var}(W^2T(W)-\beta W^2)/(bD^2)$ and $\mathbb E\epsilon_a-\beta\approx(\beta\mathrm{Var}(W^2)-\mathrm{Cov}(W^2T(W),W^2))/(bD^2)$. The expansion is formal in two places. The remainder is not bounded, and $\mathbb E_a\chi_{\mathrm G}(\epsilon_a)$ is infinite whenever $\Pr[\epsilon_a\ge1]>0$, which holds on the test law at every finite $b$, since a batch whose labels all lie near $A$ has $\epsilon_a=A^2(A^2-1)/(A^2+\sigma_0^2)>1$. The true $\kappa(b)$ is finite there, because the along-$u$ law given $a$ is a location mixture of Gaussians of variance $\sigma_0^2/b$ over the $2^b$ bounded points $b^{-1}\sum_i\pm W_i^2$, and its chi-square given $a$ is an exact finite sum, which is what the script computes. Whether $\kappa(b)\to\chi_{\mathrm G}(\beta)$ holds is then a large-deviation question, the probability $e^{-bI}$ of a batch with $\epsilon_a\ge1$ against its chi-square $e^{\Theta(b)}$, and I did not settle it. $\square$

Against (e), the Gaussian surrogate's infinite chi-square is the counterexample to the author's step 2 and to any proof of the limit by the second-moment method without conditioning.

### lem-haar-kernel-eigenvalues: the eigenvalues of the degree-k Haar kernel

uses: none

Statement. Let $d\ge3$, let $h_k$ be the normalized Hermite polynomial of degree $k$, let $\mathcal W_k\subset L^2(\gamma_d)$ be the $k$-th Wiener chaos, and let $K_k(x,y)=\mathbb E_u[h_k(\langle u,x\rangle)h_k(\langle u,y\rangle)]$ with $u$ uniform on $S^{d-1}$. Let $\mathcal H_j$ be the spherical harmonics of degree $j$ on $\mathbb R^d$, $N_j=\dim\mathcal H_j$, and $\tilde G_j$ the Gegenbauer polynomial $C^{(d/2-1)}_j$ normalized by $\tilde G_j(1)=1$. Then $\mathcal W_k=\bigoplus_{j\le k,\ j\equiv k\ (2)}V_{k,j}$ with $V_{k,j}=\{q_{k,j}(|x|^2)Y(x):Y\in\mathcal H_j\}$ for a radial polynomial $q_{k,j}$ of degree $(k-j)/2$, and
$$K_k=\sum_j\lambda_{k,j}P_{V_{k,j}},\qquad\lambda_{k,j}=\mathbb E\big[\rho^k\tilde G_j(\rho)\big],\quad\rho=\langle u,u'\rangle,$$
for independent uniform $u,u'$. The component $j=0$ is the frame-free part, with $\lambda_{k,0}=\mathbb E\rho^k$, and centring by $\bar h_k=\mathbb E_uh_k(\langle u,\cdot\rangle)$ removes it and nothing else. With $\mathbb E\rho^{2m}=(2m-1)!!/(d(d+2)\cdots(d+2m-2))$,
$$\lambda_{k,1}=\frac{k!!}{d(d+2)\cdots(d+k-1)}\ (k\text{ odd}),\qquad\lambda_{k,2}=\frac{k\,(k-1)!!}{d(d+2)\cdots(d+k)}\ (k\text{ even}),$$
and these are the top centred eigenvalues $\nu_k$ (proven for $k\le10$, see the proof), so $\nu_1=1/d$, $\nu_2=2/(d(d+2))$, $\nu_3=3/(d(d+2))$, $\nu_4=12/(d(d+2)(d+4))$, $\nu_5=15/(d(d+2)(d+4))$, $\nu_6=90/(d(d+2)(d+4)(d+6))$, $\nu_k=\Theta(d^{-\lceil(k+1)/2\rceil})$, $\nu_{k+1}/\nu_k=(k+1)/(d+k+1)$ for odd $k$ and $\nu_{k+2}/\nu_k=(k+1)(k+2)/(k(d+k+2))$ for even $k$, so $\nu_k$ decreases in $k$ for $d\ge2$.

Step 2 (proven). $P_jh_k^u$ is invariant under the stabilizer of $u$ and lies in $V_{k,j}\cong\mathcal H_j$, which holds one such vector up to scale, so $P_jh_k^u(x)=c_{k,j}\,q_{k,j}(|x|^2)\tilde G_j(\langle u,x/|x|\rangle)$. Under $\gamma_d$ the radius and the direction are independent and the direction is uniform, so by the addition theorem $\mathbb E_\omega[\tilde G_j(\langle u,\omega\rangle)\tilde G_j(\langle u',\omega\rangle)]=\tilde G_j(\langle u,u'\rangle)/N_j$ for $\omega$ uniform, we get $\langle P_jh_k^u,P_jh_k^{u'}\rangle=m_{k,j}\tilde G_j(\rho)$. Step 3 (proven). Mehler's identity gives $\langle h_k^u,h_k^{u'}\rangle=\rho^k$, and the components are orthogonal, so $\rho^k=\sum_jm_{k,j}\tilde G_j(\rho)$ as functions of $\rho$. Step 4 (proven). For independent uniform $u,u'$ the law of $\rho$ is the Gegenbauer weight on $[-1,1]$, under which the $\tilde G_j$ are orthogonal with $\mathbb E\tilde G_j(\rho)^2=1/N_j$, again by the addition theorem at $u=u'$. Multiplying step 3 by $\tilde G_j(\rho)$ and taking expectations gives $m_{k,j}=N_j\mathbb E[\rho^k\tilde G_j(\rho)]$, hence $\lambda_{k,j}=\mathbb E[\rho^k\tilde G_j(\rho)]$.
