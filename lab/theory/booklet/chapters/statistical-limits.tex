\chapter{Statistical limits}\label{ch:statistical-limits}
Filled from the thinking space \texttt{statistical-limits}. It asks what an
adversary with unlimited computation achieves from the information a learning
procedure releases, how many samples, training runs or queries it needs, and which
of these limits are known, with their exact hypotheses. Three rounds on 25 October
2026 (discussants astra in place of argon, astra, argon) ended with every object
of the first three sections accepted and no blocking challenge. Three further rounds
on the same day, all with sol, opened milestone 2 and ended the same way. They
added the orbit of a seed-blind server, with the statistical price of reaching it, and
the price of membership when the attacker may call $\Train$. The prices are stated in
the trainer's units, so they can be set against the computational prices of
\cref{ch:computational-limits}.

\section{The profile of an unbounded adversary}

\begin{definition}[Uniform security profile]\blabel{def-uniform-security-profile}\status{accepted}
Fix a procedure, a coalition, a channel interface, a game and a policy, bottom or
top oracle. An instance fixes the learning function, the seed and hardware laws, the
data and a reference law. A budget $\beta=(m,N)$ allows $m$ reference samples and $N$
$\Train$ calls. The \emph{informed optimum} of an instance is the best success of
any finite-budget adversary that may depend on the instance. Over a class $\mathfrak J$
of instances, the \emph{uniform regret} at budget $\beta$ is the least, over adversaries
of that budget chosen before the instance, of the largest gap to the informed optimum
over the class. The \emph{attained price} at accuracy $a$ is the set of minimal budgets
at which one adversary has gap at most $a$ on every instance, finite by Dickson's
lemma. The \emph{threshold price} of a criterion, such as the two conditional
probabilities of HSU25, is the set of minimal budgets at which one adversary meets it
on every instance.
\notation{$g_{\mathfrak J,\mathsf P}(\beta)$}{uniform regret of class $\mathfrak J$ under policy $\mathsf P$ at budget $\beta$}
\usesstatement{def-learning-oracle-model,def-degenerate-oracles}
\usesproof{thm-coupling-certificate,thm-black-box-certifiability,fact-hsu-membership-sample-gap}
\source{On one instance the regret is zero as an infimum, since an adversary that
knows the instance simulates every finite-budget adversary. On the plain channel the
optimum is the total variation under the bottom oracle and the top divergence under
the top oracle, attained with no call. Every positive regret is therefore a price of
not knowing which member of the class is the instance. For a bounded adversary the
profile is not degenerate on a singleton (\cref{ch:computational-limits}).}
\fullproof{def-uniform-security-profile}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{definition}

\section{The training-run price of an unbounded attacker}

\begin{lemma}[A scramble of seed cells makes chosen seeds fresh]\blabel{lem-scrambled-seed-urn}\status{accepted}
Partition the seed interval into $M$ cells, and let a learning function return at
$x_0$ the label that a uniformly random bijection $\pi$ of the cells, composed with a
labelling of law $p$, assigns to the seed's cell. Fix a learning setting shared across
the compared laws. For any adversary with at most $N<M$ $\Train$ calls and any
$\mathsf{Eval}$ and $\mathsf{Open}$ calls, with chosen inputs, seed values, names and draws,
its transcript law is within total variation $N^2/(2(M-N))$ of the lazy oracle that
answers each new cell with a fresh draw of $p$. Two lazy oracles built from $p$ and
$p'$ give transcripts within Kullback--Leibler divergence $N\,\mathrm{KL}(p\|p')$.
\usesstatement{def-learning-oracle}
\source{The statistical form of the switch from a random permutation to a random
function. The proof is a hybrid bound integrated along the paths of the scrambled run,
since the index of the $t$-th new-cell call varies with the path.}
\fullproof{lem-scrambled-seed-urn}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Shadow-run price on finite views]\blabel{thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views}\status{accepted}
One learning node trains $x_0$ or $x_1$, and its model reaches the adversary only
through a deterministic map onto $k\ge4$ views. Let $k'=2\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. (a) For
every learning function, $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ drawn-seed calls at each input give a
plug-in test within $2\sqrt{k/\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ of the total variation. (b) Under
the bottom oracle every adversary with at most $N$ calls has, on some learning
function, a gap of at least $\min\{\frac18,\frac1{32}\sqrt{3k'/N}\}$ to the optimum,
and under the top oracle the same against the top optimum. (c) The same holds over
learning functions with deterministic kernels, up to any $\zeta>0$, through
\cref{lem-scrambled-seed-urn}. (d) Under the top oracle with deterministic kernels one
call at the honest seed reaches the top optimum. (e) So under the bottom oracle a
regret at most $a<\frac18$ everywhere costs at least $3k'/(1024a^2)$ $\Train$ calls, and
$2\lceil4k/a^2\rceil$ drawn-seed calls suffice. (f) For label-only access with $C$
outputs and allowance $q_E$ the bounds hold with $k=C^{q_E}$, and the known upper and
lower bounds differ by a factor of order $\ln|U|/(C-1)+1$ in the query set $U$.
\usesstatement{def-uniform-security-profile,def-degenerate-oracles}
\usesproof{lem-scrambled-seed-urn,thm-coupling-certificate,thm-black-box-certifiability}
\source{Chosen, opened and reused seeds and calls after the challenge do not beat the
passive rate under the bottom oracle, while the honest seed collapses the price to one
call under deterministic kernels. The passive rate is classical (Daniely, Sabato,
Ben-David and Shalev-Shwartz, JMLR 2015, Theorem 5, as the discussants cite it, not
read). The oracle part has no published statement known to the space, after one query.
Without the oracle, \texttt{thm-perfect-substitution} makes (b) and (c) lower bounds on
the shadow training runs of every attack that treats training as a black box. The
integrator re-derived (a) to (f) and the lemma on 2026-10-25.}
\fullproof{thm-shadow-run-price-finite-views}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{In the trainer's units.} The trainer spends one training run. An attacker
that must come within $a$ of the best advantage on every learning function with $k$
views spends of order $k/a^2$ runs. LiRA's 64 to 256 runs per benchmark
(\cref{fact-lira-priced-in-training-runs}) sit below this worst case because its
Gaussian model restricts the class. The thousands of shadow runs of the learned
reconstructor, and its failure without the initialization
(\cref{fact-balle-reconnn-priced-in-shadow-runs}), point the way part (d) does, in a
different game. This paragraph is heuristic.

\section{Halil's reconstruction example, statistically}

\begin{proposition}[The label sandwich of an unbounded reconstructor]\blabel{prop-reconstruction-label-sandwich}\status{accepted}
Let $(X,Y,Z)$ be a record in a Polish metric space, a finite label and a
representation. Let $\bar\kappa(\eta)$ be the label-weighted best chance of a fixed
centre falling within $\eta$ of $X$ given $Y$, $S^\star(\eta)$ the best chance of any
reconstructor of $Z$, and $\varepsilon^\star$ the Bayes error of predicting $Y$ from
$Z$. (1) Floor. $S^\star\ge\bar\kappa-\varepsilon^\star$, attained by decoding the
predicted label to its class centre. (2) Ceiling. If $S^\star\ge\bar\kappa$ then
$\mathrm{kl}(S^\star\|\bar\kappa)\le I(X;Z\mid Y)$, hence
$S^\star\le\bar\kappa+\sqrt{I(X;Z\mid Y)/2}$. (3) For a Gaussian release $Z=f(X)+B$ the
conditional mutual information is at most the label average of
$\frac12\ln\det(I+\Sigma_{f\mid y}\Sigma_B^{-1})$. (4) Both ends are tight. $Z=Y$ gives
$S^\star=\bar\kappa$, and an injective representation gives $S^\star=1$.
\notation{$\bar\kappa(\eta)$}{label-weighted best ball mass of the record given its label}
\usesstatement{fact-balle-reconstruction-robustness-definition}
\usesproof{fact-pac-privacy-mutual-information-bound,fact-pac-privacy-gaussian-release-bound}
\source{The floor is the lower bound of Halil's example in the statistical regime. The
reconstructor recovers at least the class prior of the label the head predicts, less
the head's error. Only the gain above $\bar\kappa$ needs information beyond the label.
For a deterministic injective representation the statistical baseline is total, so the
content of the example lies in the computational regime
(\cref{thm-reconstruction-from-conditional-mean}), or in noisy, quantized or
many-to-one representations. The ceiling is XD23 Theorem 1 with side knowledge.}
\fullproof{prop-reconstruction-label-sandwich}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\section{The orbit of a seed-blind server}

\begin{theorem}[An unbounded seed-blind server learns the records only up to the symmetry of the record law]\blabel{thm-blind-orbit-limit}\status{accepted}
Take a protocol of the class $\mathcal P_H$ of \cref{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game},
frozen or trained, on records $x_1,\dots,x_N$ i.i.d.\ from a law $D$ with invariant
side data, and let $G_D=\{Q\in H:Q_\#D=D\}$ be the symmetry group of the record law. A
seed-blind server is any measurable map of the view. (a) For every $Q\in G_D$ the view
with the records $x_i$ has the law of the view with the records $Qx_i$. (b) For every
server output $t_{1:N}$ and every $\epsilon>0$, the probability that every $t_i$ lies
within $\epsilon$ of $x_i$ is at most the mean over $x_1$ of the largest Haar mass, over
centres $y$, of $\{Q\in G_D:\lVert y-Qx_1\rVert\le\epsilon\}$. (c) For Gaussian records
and orthogonally equivariant training from a Gaussian initialization, $G_D=O(d)$, and
no server places even the first record within $\sqrt d/(2\sqrt2)$ except with
probability $\frac12e^{-d/16}+2e^{-0.31d}$. (d) The Gram matrix of the records is the
maximal invariant of the diagonal action of $O(d)$, so (a) leaves it open to learn, and
the two statements below give servers that approach it.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling}
\source{The Haar average of (b) turns the view coupling into a bound for every server,
and (c) is a spherical cap with the Chernoff bound $\Pr[\lVert g\rVert^2\le d/4]\le e^{-0.318d}$.
The integrator re-derived (a) to (d) on 2026-10-26. The theorem is the statistical form
of the remark of \cref{ch:computational-limits} that Gaussian records are learned only
up to a common rotation, and it is total. For the pancake law the symmetry group is
$\{Q:Qv=\pm v\}$, so the hidden coordinate may be learned and its price is
computational (\cref{thm-blind-split-seed-separation}). Council of 2026-10-27, a
correction and no change of status. The theorem is a result for SGD, since Adam is
equivariant under signed permutations only, which have invariants of degree four beyond
the Gram matrix (the meditator of degree-of-a-channel, both critics, sol citing Li,
Zhang and Arora).}
\fullproof{thm-blind-orbit-limit}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-03/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Empirical whitening returns the affine shape of the records]\blabel{prop-whitening-orbit-sample-price}\status{accepted}
Let a frozen client $z=Wx+b$ have $W$ of full column rank, both unknown to the server,
and let $N\ge d+1$ records have an invertible empirical covariance $S$. The server
centres the representations, whitens them with their empirical covariance and expresses
them in an orthonormal basis of its range. (i) Its output is $Pw_{1:N}$ with
$w_i=S^{-1/2}(x_i-\bar x)$ the empirically whitened records and $P\in O(d)$ depending
on the client, so its $O(d)$-class is a function of the affine shape of the record tuple,
whatever $W$ and $b$ are. (ii) For records of mean zero and identity covariance the
orbit error $\mathcal E_N=\min_{Q\in O(d)}\frac1N\sum_i\lVert Qu_i-x_i\rVert^2$ equals
$\sum_{k\le d}(1-\sqrt{\lambda_k(S)})^2+\lVert\bar x\rVert^2\le\lVert S-I_d\rVert_F^2+\lVert\bar x\rVert^2$.
(iii) For Gaussian records
$\mathbb E\,\mathcal E_N\le\frac{(N-1)d^2}{N^2}+\frac{2d}N\le\frac{d^2+2d}N$, so a
relative orbit error $\epsilon^2$ costs at most $(d+2)/\epsilon^2$ representations.
\notation{$\mathcal E_N$}{orbit error of a reconstruction of $N$ records, the Procrustes residual per record}
\usesstatement{thm-blind-orbit-limit}
\usesproof{lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records}
\source{The polar decomposition of $W$ and the Wishart moments
$\mathbb E\lVert g\rVert^4=d^2+2d$ and $\mathbb E\langle g_j,g_l\rangle^2=d$. The
equality in (ii) is sol's, from round 04, since the cross moment of the whitened and the
centred records is $S^{1/2}$. The heuristic refinement $d^2/(4N)$ of round 03 was
withdrawn in round 05. A seed-known server recovers every record exactly, so for a
frozen linear client the seed is worth the orbit plus an error of order $d^2/N$ per
record.}
\fullproof{prop-whitening-orbit-sample-price}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-03/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-05/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[The statistical price of blind reconstruction up to rotation is $d^2/N$]\blabel{thm-blind-orbit-price}\status{accepted}
Fix $O\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ with orthonormal columns and let the class $\mathcal W$
hold the frozen linear clients $z=OR^{-1}x$ with $R$ symmetric and eigenvalues in
$[\frac34,\frac54]$, with no side data and Gaussian records. There are absolute
constants $c,C$ such that for $d\ge5$ and $N\ge Cd$: (1) every server has, at some
client of $\mathcal W$, $\Pr[\mathcal E_N>c\,d^2/N]>\frac12$, so
$\sup_{\mathcal W}\mathbb E\,\mathcal E_N\ge\frac c2d^2/N$, and (2) empirical whitening
has $\mathbb E\,\mathcal E_N\le(d^2+2d)/N$ at every client of full column rank. So the
minimax orbit price is $\Theta(d^2/N)$, and $\Theta(d/\epsilon^2)$ representations are
necessary and sufficient for a relative orbit error $\epsilon^2$.
\usesstatement{prop-whitening-orbit-sample-price}
\usesproof{prop-reconstruction-label-sandwich,fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration}
\source{Any reconstruction gives an estimate of the inverse square root $R$ of the
representations' covariance, through the $\sqrt2$-Lipschitz bound
$\lVert\,\lvert A\rvert-\lvert B\rvert\,\rVert_F\le\sqrt2\lVert A-B\rVert_F$ of the
matrix absolute value, and a conditioned Gaussian prior on $R$ makes the ceiling of
\cref{prop-reconstruction-label-sandwich} a Fano bound, with $I(R;Y)\le D/4$, a
small-ball mass $2e^{-D}$ and $0.32+2e^{-4}<\frac12$ for $D=d(d+1)/2\ge15$. The
integrator re-derived the seven steps on 2026-10-26. In this class the positive-definite
$R$ is identifiable from the covariance of the representations, so the records are
identifiable in their own coordinates, and the rotation stays hidden only under the
orthogonally invariant initialization of \cref{thm-blind-orbit-limit} (sol's
correction of round 04, applied in round 05). The lower bound holds for unbounded
servers and the upper bound is attained by an efficient one, so the price is the same in
both regimes. The council of 2026-10-27 names this theorem with
\cref{thm-blind-orbit-limit} as a result for SGD, since Adam is equivariant under
signed permutations only (both critics). The remark changes no status.}
\fullproof{thm-blind-orbit-price}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-05/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[A frozen Gaussian ReLU client is identified up to rotation by its second moment]\blabel{prop-relu-client-second-moment-identifies-weights}\status{accepted}
Let a frozen client be $z=\mathrm{ReLU}(Wx)$ with $W\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ of rank
$d$ and rows nonzero and pairwise non-parallel, on Gaussian records, and let
$\Gamma=\mathbb E[zz^\top]$. (i) $\Gamma_{jj}=\lVert w_j\rVert^2/2$ and
$\Gamma_{jk}=\lVert w_j\rVert\lVert w_k\rVert\kappa(\theta_{jk})$ with
$\kappa(\theta)=(\sin\theta+(\pi-\theta)\cos\theta)/(2\pi)$ strictly decreasing on
$[0,\pi]$. (ii) $\Gamma$ determines $WW^\top$, hence $W$ up to right multiplication by
an element of $O(d)$. (iii) If $W$ has i.i.d.\ Gaussian entries and $m\ge2.1d$, a
server that knows $\Gamma$ factors it, solves the layer on the positive coordinates and
returns $Q^\top x_i$ for every record, for one $Q\in O(d)$, in polynomial time with
probability $1-Ne^{-\Omega(d)}$. The orbit limit of \cref{thm-blind-orbit-limit} is
attained at the population level.
\usesstatement{thm-blind-orbit-limit}
\usesproof{fact-lei-random-expansive-layers-invertible}
\source{The kernel is the first-order arc-cosine kernel of Cho and Saul (NeurIPS 2009,
as sol names it, not in the library), at half their normalization, and
$\kappa'(\theta)=-(\pi-\theta)\sin\theta/(2\pi)$. The same identity serves the auditor
of a planted layer in \cref{prop-gram-sufficiency-rotation-invariant}. The sample price,
with $\Gamma$ estimated from $N$ representations, is open
(\slug{prob-relu-blind-inversion-sample-price}). For non-Gaussian isotropic laws an
approximate kernel identity needs a hypothesis on the law.}
\fullproof{prop-relu-client-second-moment-identifies-weights}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-03/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\section{Train access and the price of membership}

\begin{theorem}[Train calls replace reference samples in the membership price of noisy mean estimation]\blabel{thm-train-access-price-frontier}\status{accepted}
Let the population be $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$, both unknown, and the learning function
the noisy mean of HSU25, $\frac1n\sum_{x\in D}x+\rho\Sigma^{1/2}\xi$, with the noise
calibrated to the population covariance. The attacker of
\cref{fact-hsu-membership-sample-gap} receives the release, a target and $m$ reference
samples, and may make $N$ $\Train$ calls on datasets of its choice under the bottom
oracle. Let $d^\star=n+n^2\rho^2$. (a) If $d>c_1d^\star$ and $m+2N\le c_2d^\star$, no
attack meets the HSU25 criterion, since two reference samples simulate one call. (b)
With identity covariance, no attack meets it for $d<c(n+n^2\rho^2+n^2/m)$, whatever
$N$ is. (c) For $d\ge2100\,d^\star$, $m-1\ge\min\{n,1/\rho^2\}$ and $N\ge C_0d$, the
attack that estimates $\Sigma$ from $N$ calls on one fixed dataset and compares the
target with a held-out reference sample in the estimated metric meets the criterion.
(d) For $\rho=n^{-a}$ with $0<a<\frac12$ and $d=\kappa d^\star$, with no $\Train$ call
$\Theta(n^{2-2a})$ reference samples are necessary and sufficient, with $\Train$ access
$\Theta(n^{2a})$ reference samples are, bought with $O(n^{2-2a})$ calls, and in samples
plus calls the price is $\Theta(n^{2-2a})$ either way.
\usesstatement{fact-hsu-membership-sample-gap,def-uniform-security-profile}
\usesproof{fact-hsu-gap-prices-covariance,fact-subgaussian-sample-covariance-concentration}
\source{The simulation of a call by two reference samples,
$(Y-Y')/\sqrt2\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$, HSU25 Theorems 3.1, 3.3 and 4.1 as the space
read them, and two Chebyshev bounds (0.05 and 0.49) for the attack of (c). The
integrator re-derived (a) to (d) on 2026-10-26. The attack of (c) runs in polynomial
time, so the frontier is also the price of efficient attackers. For constant $\rho$ and
$d\gtrsim n^2$ sol's attack of round 03 needs no reference sample, so outside the regime
of (d) the sample price can vanish. The modelling hypothesis, noise calibrated to the
population covariance so that $\Train$ calls leak it, is a decision for Halil.}
\fullproof{thm-train-access-price-frontier}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-03/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-05/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[In noisy mean estimation the attacker pays a factor $1+n\rho^2$ over the trainer]\blabel{thm-same-units-gap-gaussian-mechanism}\status{accepted}
In the setting of \cref{thm-train-access-price-frontier}, with $\rho<1$ and
$d=\kappa d^\star$, a meter of one unit per reference sample and per $\Train$ call, the
trainer's calibration free and its error the Mahalanobis risk, the trainer spends $n$
samples and one run. Every membership attack meeting the HSU25 criterion spends more
than $\frac{c_2}2d^\star$ units, and a polynomial-time attack spends at most
$C_\kappa d^\star$. So the attacker's price exceeds the trainer's by at least the factor
$\frac{c_2}4(1+n\rho^2)$. With $\rho=n^{-a}$ and $0<a<\frac12$, the trainer's sample
exponent is $1$ and the attacker's exponent in combined units is $2-2a$, while its
exponent in samples alone, with $\Train$ access, is $2a$.
\usesstatement{thm-train-access-price-frontier}
\usesproof{fact-hsu-membership-sample-gap}
\source{Parts (a), (c) and (d) of the frontier. The separation in reference samples alone
is HSU25's (Theorems 3.1 and 3.3, Remark 3.4), and this theorem adds $\Train$ access and
the combined meter. It sharpens \cref{conj-same-units-gap} in one direction, for a
natural procedure, and does not settle that conjecture's own form, a low-rank adapter
against gradient-trained membership adversaries. The lower bound holds for unbounded
attackers and the upper bound is attained by an efficient one, and the margin grows
with the noise, which also costs the trainer accuracy.}
\fullproof{thm-same-units-gap-gaussian-mechanism}{lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-03/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/statistical-limits/round-04/builder.md}
\end{theorem}
