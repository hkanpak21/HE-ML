\chapter{Mappings onto hard problems}\label{ch:hard-problem-mappings}
Filled from the thinking space \texttt{hard-problem-mappings}. Halil answered, on
hardness that comes from a data law, that mappings onto hard problems from the
literature are wanted. The space asks which attack tasks map onto such problems, with
the prices on both sides, and fixes the form of a mapping. Three rounds on 25 and 26
October 2026, all with sol, ended with every object below accepted and no blocking
challenge. The space's one clean mapping is learning-based extraction of a single-index
predictor, where the extractor's statistical-query exponent is twice the trainer's. It
also corrects one literature mapping, the sparse-PCA backdoor of GKVZ22, and reduces
the detection of a planted weight law to testing its rows. No result of this chapter
uses the learning oracle.

\section{The form of a mapping}

\begin{definition}[Mapping between an attack task and a hard problem]\blabel{def-hard-problem-mapping}\status{accepted}
A \emph{mapping} fixes nine things for a family indexed by a dimension $d$. (1) The
procedure, with the cost meter of \cref{def-seed-access-modes} and the trainer's budget
vector. (2) The attack task, a game with a view, a success functional, a baseline and
the advantage as their difference. (3) The hard problem $\Pi$, a distributional decision
or search problem with a cited set $B_\Pi(a)$ of budgets at which no solver of the
stated regime reaches advantage $a$. (4) The direction. $\Pi\lhd\mathcal T$ when a
reduction simulates the game without the secret, counts every training run it
simulates and maps the attacker's output to a solution, and $\mathcal T\lhd\Pi$ when a
solver becomes an attacker. (5) The price maps, $p_A$ from the attacker's budget and
advantage to the solver's, monotone with loss $\eta$, and $p_P$ with a named learning
target. (6) The regime with its accounting. Population SQ with recorded VSTAT
parameters, sample-based with counted samples, low-degree with the composed degree
stated, or polynomial time under a named assumption with the overhead stated. One
mapping lives in one regime, and a VSTAT parameter compared with a sample count is
labelled heuristic. (7) The exponent ratio, defined only for named scalar resources
with polynomial prices on both sides. (8) The carrier of the hardness, declared
metadata with no order. The record law, the honest randomness of the procedure, the
choice of a trainer who plants, or a key-dependent change the designer makes for
security. A mapping whose only hard instance is of the last kind does not count. (9) An
experiment with its measured budgets and training runs. Read forward, a mapping
transfers thresholds (\cref{lem-mapping-transfer}).
\usesstatement{def-seed-access-modes,def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\source{The round file tabulates thirteen instances, one row per mapping and regime,
from \cref{thm-blind-split-seed-separation} to
\cref{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}. The carrier rule counts mappings
carried by the record law, by the honest randomness of a standard procedure and by the
choice of a trainer who plants, and excludes only a key-dependent change the designer
makes for security. Whether this reading of the rule on cryptographic plug-ins is
Halil's is a decision for him. Council of 2026-10-27, on item (7), a remark and no change
of status. The price exponent at one degree depends on the geometry of the hidden
index, $d^{k/2}$ in a Gaussian frame and $d^k$ in a subset frame, so the exponent alone
is no unit (\slug{prop-overlap-grading-carries-price}, fable ACCEPT, sol REVISE).}
\fullproof{def-hard-problem-mapping}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-02/builder.md}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A mapping transfers thresholds]\blabel{lem-mapping-transfer}\status{accepted}
Let $\Pi\lhd\mathcal T$ in regime $R$ with price map $p_A$ and loss $\eta$, and let
$B_\Pi(a)$ be the cited set of solver budgets at which no $R$-solver reaches advantage
$a$. Every $R$-attacker with advantage at least $a+\eta$ has a budget $b_A$ with
$p_A(b_A)\notin B_\Pi(a)$. If $\mathcal T\lhd\Pi$ with price map $q$ and loss $\eta'$, a
solver with budget $b$ and advantage $a$ gives an attacker with budget $q(b)$ and
advantage $a-\eta'$.
\usesstatement{def-hard-problem-mapping}
\source{The definition read forward. The lemma holds in each regime separately and
never across two.}
\fullproof{lem-mapping-transfer}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[An SQ bound on testing binds every attacker of a one-bit learner]\blabel{prop-learner-side-sq-transfer}\status{accepted}
Let a procedure reach its records only through $m$ calls to 1-STAT, each an adaptively
chosen Boolean function of one fresh record, with coins independent of the record law,
and let any randomized map, unbounded and white-box, read its output. Let $B$ be the
problem of telling a law $D_0$ from the members of a family $\mathfrak D$. (a) If every
SQ algorithm that solves $B$ with probability $\alpha-\delta$ needs more than $q$
queries to VSTAT$(t)$, then every procedure-attacker pair that solves $B$ with
probability $\alpha$ has $m\ge\min\{q,\delta^2t\}$, for $\delta\in(0,\frac14]$. (b)
With $\mathrm{SDA}(B,\bar\gamma)=d$ and $\alpha>\frac12$,
$m\ge\min\{d(2\alpha-1)/2,(2\alpha-1)^2/(48\bar\gamma)\}$.
\usesstatement{lem-mapping-transfer}
\usesproof{lem-sq-learner-post-processing,fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\source{The composition of the procedure and the attacker is a one-bit sampling
algorithm, which FGRVX17 Theorem 3.13 simulates by $m$ queries to VSTAT$(m/\delta^2)$,
and Theorem 3.7 gives (b). FGRVX17 Theorem 3.16 prints $(2\alpha+1)^2$ where its proof
gives $(2\alpha-1)^2$, found by sol and checked in the PDF by the builder.
\cref{lem-sq-learner-post-processing} is the planted-biclique instance. The bound is
unconditional and binds unbounded attackers, because the restriction is on the
procedure, and it says nothing about procedures that read their records in full.
Council of 2026-10-27, a remark and no change of status. The transfer to SGD learners
through \cref{fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd} holds only in the worst-rounding model

\section{Extraction priced by the released link}

\begin{lemma}[The level sets of $\lvert z\rvert\phi(z)$ carry the Gaussian's first two moments]\blabel{lem-level-set-moment-balance}\status{accepted}
Let $Z$ be standard Gaussian and $G(r)=r\phi(r)$ for $r\ge0$. For $t\in(0,G(1))$ let
$a<1<b$ solve $G(r)=t$, and put $A=1-a^2$ and $B=b^2-1$. Conditionally on
$G(\lvert Z\rvert)=t$, $Z$ takes the values $\pm a$ with total probability $B/(A+B)$
and $\pm b$ with total probability $A/(A+B)$, signs equally likely. Hence
$\mathbb E[Z\mid t]=\mathbb E[Z^3\mid t]=0$, $\mathbb E[Z^2\mid t]=1$ and
$\mathbb E[Z^4\mid t]=1+AB$, so the conditional Hermite moments of degrees one to three
vanish and $\mathbb E[h_4(Z)\mid t]=(AB-2)/\sqrt{24}$. The link
$m_\natural(z)=\lvert z\rvert e^{-z^2/2}$ has generative exponent four.
\source{The identity $\frac{d}{dr}(r\phi(r))=\phi(r)(1-r^2)$ is the whole argument.
The Jacobian weights of a level set are $1/(1-a^2)$ and $1/(b^2-1)$, which balance the
second moment exactly. The value agrees with DPLB24 Example 2.7 (ii) and Corollary 2.8.
sol confirmed it line by line in round 00 and the integrator re-derived it on
2026-10-26.}
\fullproof{lem-level-set-moment-balance}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Extraction costs the generative exponent of the released link]\blabel{thm-extraction-exponent-price}\status{accepted}
A trainer releases $\hat f(x)=m(\langle w,x\rangle)$ for a known measurable link
$m:\mathbb R\to[-M,M]$, not almost everywhere constant, and a unit $w$. The extractor
sees answers $\hat f(x)+\sigma\xi$ at random Gaussian inputs, through VSTAT$(n)$ in the
SQ game or as $n$ answered pairs in the sample game, and outputs $g$ with values in
$[-1,1]$. It has weak fidelity $c_0$ when
$\mathrm{Cov}(g(x),\hat f(x))\ge c_0\,\mathrm{sd}(m(Z))$. Let $k=k^*(m)$. (a) For fixed
$m$, $\sigma$, $c_0$ and $r$, every SQ extractor with at most $d^r$ queries that reaches
weak fidelity $c_0$ with probability $\frac23$ for uniform $w$ needs $n\ge c\,d^{k/2}$,
for $d$ large. (b) Given the denoiser of DPLB24 Theorem 4.1 for the noisy answers,
$O(d^{k/2}/\tilde\lambda_k^2+d/(\tilde\lambda_k^2\epsilon^2))$ answered pairs, plus
$n_s$ pairs to fix the sign when $m$ is not even, give normalized error $\eta$ with
probability $1-2e^{-d^{c'}}-\delta_s$. (c) If the training labels are a channel of the
regression function $m_P(Z)$, then $k^*(m_P)\le k^*(P)$, so extracting the Bayes
predictor never needs a larger exponent than recovering the direction from labels. (d)
For the label law $P_\natural$ of $(Z,Z\varepsilon)$ with
$\Pr[\varepsilon=1\mid Z]=(1+\mathrm{sgn}(Z)e^{-Z^2/2})/2$, the trainer recovers the
direction from $O(d(1+\epsilon^{-2}))$ records at exponent two, the Bayes link is
$m_\natural$ of exponent four, and extracting the release needs VSTAT$(c\,d^2)$ while
$O(d^2)$ answered pairs suffice.
\usesstatement{lem-level-set-moment-balance,def-hard-problem-mapping}
\usesproof{fact-generative-exponent-post-processing-monotone,fact-generative-exponent-sq-recovery-bound,fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\source{A fidelity-to-detection reduction with a Haar rotation and three clipped
queries, the code of DPLB24 Lemma E.3, the correlation series of its Lemma 3.1, and
FGRVX17 Lemma 3.10 with Theorem 3.7, which sol verified step by step in round 01. The
integrator re-derived (a) to (d) on 2026-10-26. The trainer-to-extractor comparison
sets a sample count against a VSTAT parameter, so as a sample separation it is
heuristic, and chosen inputs break it, since the release then gives $w$ coordinate by
coordinate. The cost of computing the denoiser in (b) is not charged and is open.
Council of 2026-10-27, a correction and no change of status. Against a square-loss
population gradient trainer the gap vanishes, since extracting a Bayes release costs
what training costs (\slug{thm-bayes-release-loss-matched-price}, fable ACCEPT, sol
REVISE). The gap exists only against a trainer that reads squared labels, and the
information exponent of the label law bounds it
(\slug{prop-extraction-gap-information-exponent}, fable ACCEPT, sol REVISE). The
degree-four link is a knife edge. Every even link strictly monotone on the half line
has generative exponent at most two, because each level set is a pair $\{\pm a\}$ on
which $\mathbb E[h_2(Z)\mid t]=(a^2-1)/\sqrt2$ (fable, checked by the integrator).}
\fullproof{thm-extraction-exponent-price}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-02/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\section{Tampering with the weight law}

\begin{lemma}[The sparse-PCA backdoor is detected or the key buys almost nothing]\blabel{lem-sparse-pca-backdoor-width-price}\status{accepted}
In the construction of GKVZ22 Appendix A, honest weights are i.i.d.\ Gaussian and
backdoored weights i.i.d.\ $\mathcal N(0,I_d+\theta ss^\top)$ with $s$ a $k$-sparse unit
vector and $0<\theta\le k$, the classifier thresholds the mean ReLU response $A_m(x)$
of $m$ features at $\tau\in(0,1]$ on unit-norm data, and the trigger is
$x\mapsto x+\lambda s$. (a) The coordinate-variance test, in time $O(md)$, has
advantage at least $\frac34$ once $m\ge64k^2\ln(4(d+1))/\theta^2$. (b) If that test has
advantage below $\frac34$, then $\theta<\theta_*=8k\sqrt{\ln(4(d+1))/m}$, and Claim
A.5's $\lambda>1/\theta$ forces $\lambda k>\sqrt{m/(64\ln(4(d+1)))}$. (c) For an input
fixed independently of the weights, the mean response is $\sigma'/\sqrt{2\pi}$ with
$\sigma'$ the preactivation standard deviation, and
$\Pr[\lvert A_m-\mathbb EA_m\rvert\ge t\,\mathbb EA_m]\le2e^{-mt^2/(4\pi)}$. For a unit
$x\perp s$ the trigger in the backdoored network and the key-free radial input
$(1+\lambda')x$ with $\lambda'\le\lambda\sqrt{1+\theta}$ have the same mean response in
either network, and in the undetected regime $\sqrt{1+\theta}<1+\theta_*/2$. (d) Claim
A.5's activation condition is insufficient. At $\theta=1$, $\lambda=1.1$, $\tau=1$ and a
unit $x\perp s$ its hypotheses hold, yet the triggered mean response is
$\sqrt{3.42/(2\pi)}\approx0.738<\tau$, so the trigger fails with probability tending to
one as $m$ grows.
\usesstatement{fact-gkvz22-backdoor-scope}
\source{Laurent and Massart's tail bounds for $\chi^2_m$ and Gaussian concentration of
the averaged ReLU response, a $(\sigma'/\sqrt m)$-Lipschitz function of the
preactivations. The integrator re-derived (a) to (d) on 2026-10-26. The rounded mean
enters (d) as an inequality, since the exact mean is slightly below (sol, round 02).
Appendix A's claimed guarantees give no backdoor content, while Theorem 6.4 of GKVZ22,
the random Fourier features under CLWE, stands. The trainer's width prices the trade-off,
since widening the layer for accuracy shrinks $\theta_*$.}
\fullproof{lem-sparse-pca-backdoor-width-price}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-02/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[White-box detection of a planted weight law is testing the rows]\blabel{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}\status{accepted}
A random-feature release holds $G\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$, offsets $b$ independent
of $G$ and a head $w=H(G,b,S;\omega)$ trained on data $S$ with coins $\omega$
independent of $G$. Under $H_0$ the rows of $G$ are i.i.d.\ Gaussian, under $H_1$
i.i.d.\ from $P^A_s$ with $s$ drawn from a prior on the sphere. In every class of
algorithms that can sample the offsets, the data and the coins and run $H$, the best
advantage of telling $H_0$ from $H_1$ given the release equals the best advantage of
telling $\gamma_d^{\otimes m}$ from the mixture over $s$ of $(P^A_s)^{\otimes m}$ given
$m$ samples.
\usesstatement{fact-gkvz22-backdoor-scope}
\source{Simulation in one direction, GKVZ22's argument of Theorem 6.4 for any $A$, and
projection onto $G$ in the other. The auditor's sample count is the trainer's width, so
the detection price of every planted weight law is its NGCA testing price at $m$
samples. A backdoor also needs a trigger with a proved effect, and in this catalogue
only GKVZ22's pancake law has one. SQ lower bounds for NGCA transfer to this auditor
only under the heuristic that VSTAT$(m)$ models $m$ samples.}
\fullproof{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[For a uniform key the Gram matrix is all the auditor needs]\blabel{prop-gram-sufficiency-rotation-invariant}\status{accepted}
In the setting of \cref{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection} with the key
uniform on the sphere. (i) For every test $T(G,b)$, the randomized test that factors
$GG^\top=FF^\top$, draws a Haar $R\in O(d)$ and returns $T(FR,b)$ has the same
advantage, at the cost of $T$ plus $O(m^2d+d^3)$ operations. (ii) For ReLU features
without offsets the second moment of the features on Gaussian probes determines
$GG^\top$ through the arc-cosine kernel $\kappa$ of
\cref{prop-relu-client-second-moment-identifies-weights}, so an auditor that sees only
the law of the features on unknown Gaussian inputs detects exactly as well as the
white-box auditor. (iii) Rotation invariance of the prior is needed, since the
coordinate test of \cref{lem-sparse-pca-backdoor-width-price} uses the frame, which
$GG^\top$ does not carry.
\usesstatement{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}
\source{Equal Gram matrices give $F=GQ_0$ with $Q_0\in O(d)$, and $Q_0R$ is Haar given
$G$. Exact feature-law access supplies no finite-probe price, so the statement is not
yet an experimentally priced mapping. The number of probes, or the SQ tolerance, at
which $GG^\top$ returns to a precision a given test survives is open. The round-02
summary proposed keeping this statement and \cref{lem-feature-matrix-transpose} as
remarks under \cref{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}. They enter as
objects because the space accepted them.}
\fullproof{prop-gram-sufficiency-rotation-invariant}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-02/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[An auditor of a planted layer is a split server, transposed]\blabel{lem-feature-matrix-transpose}\status{accepted}
For any activation $\sigma$ applied entrywise, $\sigma(GX^\top)^\top=\sigma(XG^\top)$.
If $G$ has i.i.d.\ rows from $P^A_s$ and $X$ i.i.d.\ Gaussian rows, the auditor's
feature matrix on the probes $X$ is the transpose of the representation matrix of a
split server whose Gaussian client of width $N$, without offsets, received $m$ records
from $P^A_s$. The probes are the client's weights and the weight rows are the records.
An auditor who knows its probes is a seed-known server, and one who sees features on
unknown inputs is a seed-blind server. For ReLU and $N\ge2.1d$ an auditor who knows its
Gaussian probes recovers every row of $G$ exactly by layer-wise linear equations with
probability at least $1-me^{-\Omega(d)}$.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{fact-lei-random-expansive-layers-invertible}
\source{LJDD19 Theorem 3 per row with a union bound. The lemma transfers no detection
or key-recovery statement. Recovering the rows leaves the planted direction as hidden
as the white-box auditor finds it (sol, round 00).}
\fullproof{lem-feature-matrix-transpose}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{conjecture}[Detection of a weight-law backdoor is NGCA at the trainer's width]\blabel{conj-backdoor-detection-is-ngca}\status{refuted}
Round 00 conjectured four claims about a random-feature layer whose weight law is
planted. (a) White-box detection is two-sided equivalent to NGCA testing of the planted
law at the trainer's width. (b) An auditor with Gram access is as strong as the
white-box auditor. (c) The auditor's feature matrix is the transpose of a split
server's representation matrix and the seed separation transfers to the key's
projections. (d) The known thresholds price it. Part (b) is false for activations that
erase the weights, sol's constant activation, and holds for ReLU without offsets and
uniform keys as \cref{prop-gram-sufficiency-rotation-invariant}. The transfer of (c) is
false as stated, since recovering the rows leaves the planted direction hidden, and
only the identity and the inversion survive as \cref{lem-feature-matrix-transpose}.
Part (a) became \cref{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}, and the SQ
clause of (d) was dropped. Round 01 withdrew the conjecture and the space accepted the
withdrawal.
\usesstatement{prop-weight-testing-equals-white-box-detection}
\fullproof{conj-backdoor-detection-is-ngca}{lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-00/builder.md,lab/theory/spaces/hard-problem-mappings/round-01/builder.md}
\end{conjecture}

learning-based extraction, where the extractor sees the released model only on random
inputs, and it disappears with chosen inputs. Whether that game is one he wants priced
is his call. The carrier rule of \cref{def-hard-problem-mapping} is the space's reading
of the rule on cryptographic plug-ins, and the council's reading of the same rule asks
whether a pseudorandom seed generator, needed by \cref{thm-blind-split-seed-separation}
