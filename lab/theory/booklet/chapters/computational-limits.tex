\chapter{Computational limits}\label{ch:computational-limits}
Filled from the thinking space \texttt{computational-limits}. It asks three things. What
can an adversary achieve that must learn efficiently, as a learner trained by
gradient methods or a statistical-query or low-degree algorithm? Which known
learning problems does an attack task reduce to or from? Where does the
computational price depart from the statistical one? Three rounds on 25 October 2026 (discussants
astra, astra in place of argon, argon) ended with no blocking challenge. The
space made Halil's reconstruction example formal in split learning. The
reconstructor is a learner from representations to records. After a symmetry
argument its problem is non-Gaussian component analysis on the record law when
the server has no labels, and the trainer's own single-index problem for the
label-relevant part. No result of this chapter uses the learning oracle, and
each reduction costs one honest training run.

\section{A refuted bound, and what survives}

\begin{theorem}[The proof note's black-box reduction]\blabel{thm-fs-sq-black-box-reduction}\status{refuted}
The FORMAL\_SECURITY proof note \path{.tools/proof.tex} claims, as its Theorem 2,
that a black-box adversary against a model trained by a deterministic
statistical-query learner on the vertex-row encoding of a graph has advantage at
most the SQ advantage for planted clique, so that with polynomially many queries
of constant tolerance no black-box adversary has constant advantage. The claim is
false. With the exact oracle, or an honest oracle averaging each query over
$\lceil 32n^2\ln n\rceil$ fresh draws, a learner with $O(n^2)$ queries of tolerance
$1/3$ stores every row of the graph, and an unbounded adversary reading the model
decides the planted clique with advantage $1-o(1)$ at $k=n^{1/2-\delta}$.
\usesproof{fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\source{Counterexample by the space, accepted line by line by astra in round 00,
re-derived by the integrator. The note applies FGRVX17 to the empirical law of
the $n$ rows of one symmetric matrix, and chains a worst-oracle lower bound with
the run of one particular oracle. Its quoted bound also grows with the tolerance.
\texttt{prob-attacker-lower-bound-from-hard-learning} rested on this note. The
registers of \path{sink/formalizing/} hold it as \texttt{thm-sq-hardness-black-box-attacks},
with this slug as an alias.}
\fullproof{thm-fs-sq-black-box-reduction}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-00/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[An SQ learner cannot hand over what it cannot detect]\blabel{lem-sq-learner-post-processing}\status{accepted}
Let $k\le n^{1/2-\delta}$, let a learner reach its training law, uniform or planted
biclique, only through $m$ calls to 1-STAT with coins independent of the law, and
let any randomized map, unbounded and white-box, read its output. If the composition
distinguishes uniform from every planted law with probability $2/3$, then
$m=\Omega(n^2/k^2)$. The VSTAT form holds against every valid oracle, and by
\cref{fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd} it covers mini-batch SGD learners in the
coarse-precision regime.
\usesstatement{fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\usesproof{fact-sq-simulates-minibatch-sgd}
\source{The bound is priced in the trainer's fresh samples and binds unbounded
attackers. An SQ bound on the learner binds every attacker, while an SQ bound on
the attacker binds only SQ attackers.}
\fullproof{lem-sq-learner-post-processing}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-00/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\section{The reconstruction games}

\begin{definition}[Seed-blind reconstruction games]\blabel{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}\status{accepted}
The protocol class $\mathcal P_H$, for a closed subgroup $H\le O(d)$, holds split
protocols whose client applies an $H$-equivariant pre-map, a first layer
$W\pi(x)+b$ and any further map, whose initialization law is invariant under
$W_0\mapsto W_0Q$ for $Q\in H$, whose update of the first layer is equivariant
under right multiplication by $H$, and whose server, honest or not, never sees
first-layer weights, their gradients, records, or record-representation pairs.
Side data are invariant under $H$. A target is a map $g$ of the record with
finite second moment and a finite symmetry group $K$. In the \emph{sample game}
the attacker sees the view of one run on $N$ records, runs in time $T$, and its
fine success at scale $\epsilon$ is the chance that some $\kappa\in K$ puts every
output within $\epsilon$ of $\kappa g(x_i)$, minus the same chance for fresh records
(the narcissus baseline of \cref{fact-cohen-narcissus-equals-one-wayness}). In the
\emph{population SQ game}, for frozen clients, the attacker has $q$ queries of
tolerance $\tau$ to the law of (representation, side data) and its weak success is
the correlation of its output map with $\kappa g$. The sample game reconstructs the
observed records. The SQ game evaluates a reconstruction map on the record law. An
experiment fixes finite representations of queries and of returned maps.
Seed-known variants also give the attacker the initialization, and the
\emph{price of the seed} at a success level is the pair of budget sets at which
the two attackers reach it.
\notation{$\mathcal P_H$}{split protocols equivariant under a group $H\le O(d)$}
\usesstatement{fact-cohen-narcissus-equals-one-wayness,fact-orthogonal-equivariance-of-training}
\source{Accepted by astra in round 01. The integrator applied the wording
correction of round 02 to the sentence on observed records.}
\fullproof{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-01/builder.md}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The view is a function of rotated records]\blabel{lem-equivariant-view-coupling}\status{accepted}
For a protocol in $\mathcal P_H$ and $Q\in H$, the view of the run with coins
$\omega$ on records $x$ equals, pathwise, the view of the run with $W_0$ replaced by
$W_0Q^\top$ on records $Qx$. Hence for $Q$ Haar on $H$ a simulator that runs the
protocol from fresh coins on the rotated records $u_i=Qx_i$ reproduces the joint
law of every attacker's output with the targets, at the cost of one training run.
If the hidden part of the record law is $P^A_V$ off a label subspace and $H$ fixes
that subspace, the attacker's task becomes NGCA coordinate recovery with a
uniformly random frame.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{fact-orthogonal-equivariance-of-training}
\source{The induction of LZA21 Theorem C.1, carried to the whole transcript, an
adaptive server and side data, stated as a pathwise coupling. Adam, sign updates,
coordinate-wise normalization, convolutional first layers and published weights
each break a hypothesis.}
\fullproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-01/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Whitening a frozen linear client]\blabel{lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records}\status{accepted}
For a frozen client $z=Wx+b$ with $W$ of full column rank and right rotation
invariant in law, and records of mean zero and identity covariance, the population
whitening map $T$ satisfies $T(z-b)=Rx$ with $R\in O(d)$ a function of $W$ alone,
Haar-distributed and independent of the records.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\fullproof{lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-01/builder.md}
\end{lemma}

\section{Prices of blind reconstruction}

\begin{proposition}[SQ price of blind reconstruction]\blabel{prop-sq-blind-reconstruction-bound}\status{accepted}
Let the client be frozen in $\mathcal P_{O(d)}$, with no side data and the target
$\langle v,x\rangle$. (a) For pancake records $H_{v,\beta,\gamma}$ (homogeneous CLWE,
\cref{fact-clwe-hardness}) with $\gamma\ge2$ and $\beta\in(0,1)$, an attacker reaching
weak success $c_0$ with $q$ queries of tolerance $\tau$ makes at least
$\frac{11}{288}M\tau_*^2\beta^2/\gamma^2-d$ queries, with $M=\exp(\Omega(c_0^4d))$ and
$\tau_*=\min(\tau,\tilde\Theta(c_0d^{-1/2}))$, under two margins that hold for $d$ large
and $\gamma^2\ge C\log(d/c_0)$. (b) For records whose hidden coordinate law matches
moments under the hypothesis of DKRS23 Theorem 1.5, with a fixed witness degree,
the attacker needs $2^{d^{\Omega(c)}}$ queries or a tolerance polynomially small in
$d$, provided at least four moments match.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game,fact-clwe-hardness}
\usesproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling,fact-hclwe-sq-correlation-bound,fact-ngca-sq-lower-bound,fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\source{Part (b) is proven conditional on DKRS23 Theorem 1.5. The reducer turns a
reconstruction into a direction with $d$ truncated queries and then meets the
statistical dimension of FGRVX17 Theorem 3.2. The bounds are SQ only, and
lattice-reduction attackers lie outside them.}
\fullproof{prop-sq-blind-reconstruction-bound}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Fine blind reconstruction is as hard as hCLWE]\blabel{prop-fine-reconstruction-to-hclwe}\status{accepted}
For a protocol in $\mathcal P_{O(d)}$, frozen or trained, on pancake records with
$\gamma\ge2\sqrt d$ and $\gamma/\beta$ polynomial, an attacker of time $T$ that
reconstructs every record's hidden coordinate within $c_1/\gamma$, up to sign, with
probability $p$ gives an hCLWE distinguisher of time $T$ plus one protocol run plus
$\mathrm{poly}(d)$ and advantage $p-e^{-\Omega(d)}$. So $p$ is negligible for every
polynomial-time attacker unless a worst-case lattice problem has a polynomial-time
quantum algorithm (BRST21 Corollary 4.2).
\usesstatement{fact-clwe-hardness}
\usesproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling}
\fullproof{prop-fine-reconstruction-to-hclwe}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[The seed separates attackers for pancake records]\blabel{thm-blind-split-seed-separation}\status{accepted}
Let the client's first layer be $\mathrm{ReLU}(Wx)$ with i.i.d.\ Gaussian $W$ of
expansion at least $2.1$, further layers meeting the same assumption, an
orthogonally equivariant update, and pancake records with $\gamma\ge2\sqrt d$. (i)
A seed-known server recovers every record exactly in polynomial time, for a frozen
client and, for a trained one, for the records it sees under the initial weights.
(ii) A seed-blind SQ server of a frozen client pays the exponential price of
\cref{prop-sq-blind-reconstruction-bound} (a). (iii) A seed-blind polynomial-time
server with the record-level view, frozen or trained client, reconstructs finely
with negligible probability unless a worst-case lattice problem is quantum
polynomial-time.
\usesstatement{fact-lei-random-expansive-layers-invertible}
\usesproof{prop-sq-blind-reconstruction-bound,prop-fine-reconstruction-to-hclwe,lem-equivariant-view-coupling}
\source{The price of the seed is one linear solve per record against exponentially
many statistical queries or a quantum lattice algorithm. Astra judges this an
artificial-distribution reduction. The Gaussian layer is no plug-in, but the
hardness enters through the record law. Whether that meets Halil's rule on
cryptographic plug-ins is a decision for Halil. Tracking a trained client's weights
from a known seed is heuristic and is not claimed.}
\fullproof{thm-blind-split-seed-separation}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\section{The label-relevant coordinate}

\begin{theorem}[A good predictor hands over the label direction]\blabel{thm-reconstruction-from-conditional-mean}\status{accepted}
Let the client be frozen and linear of full column rank, records Gaussian, labels a
noisy function of $\langle u,x\rangle$ with conditional mean $m$ and first Hermite
coefficient $\beta_1\ne0$. A server holding a top model whose predictor has excess
square risk $\varepsilon$, the population whitening, and $N$ held-out
representations, recovers $u$ up to sign within $2(\sqrt\varepsilon+\eta_N)/|\beta_1|$
with probability $1-\delta$, where $\eta_N=B\sqrt{d/(N\delta)}$. It then reconstructs
the hidden coordinate of any records, training records included, with mean square
error at most that squared distance times the top eigenvalue of their empirical
second moment. This is the floor on attack success in Halil's example. The
attacker's error is bounded above by a function of the attacked model's excess
risk.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records}
\source{The integrator applied the wording correction of round 02 to the sentence on
Halil's example. The bound holds for any server and costs one pass over $N$
representations. It is an upper bound on attack cost.}
\fullproof{thm-reconstruction-from-conditional-mean}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-01/builder.md}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[What the labels cost the server]\blabel{prop-sq-label-relevant-reconstruction-bound}\status{accepted}
For a frozen linear client, Gaussian records and labels from a single-index law
with generative exponent $k^*\ge3$ and finite $\chi^2$ information, all fixed as
$d$ grows. (a) An SQ attacker reaching weak success $c_0$ with at most $d^r-d$
queries to VSTAT$(n)$ needs $n\ge c\,d^{k^*/2}$. (b) With population whitening, the
algorithm of DPLB24 on the whitened representations and denoised labels reaches the
direction from $O(d^{k^*/2})$ labelled representations and reconstructs the
coordinate.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling,lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records,fact-generative-exponent-sq-recovery-bound,fact-fgrvx17-planted-clique-sq-bound}
\source{The server's price for the label-relevant coordinate is the generative
exponent of the task, whatever the trainer's model learned. The lower bound
measures detection first and the upper bound is sample-based, so the two sides are
not one SQ price. The off-diagonal correlation bound uses $\lambda_k^2\le1$, which
holds by conditional Jensen for normalized Hermite coefficients (the integrator's
reading, DPLB24 not re-read).}
\fullproof{prop-sq-label-relevant-reconstruction-bound}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[Reconstruction at the cost of training]\blabel{conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost}\status{refuted}
Round 00 conjectured that blind reconstruction of the label-relevant coordinate is
equivalent to learning the task. Part (b) is false. The labels
$y=\langle u,x\rangle\xi$ with an independent sign $\xi$ have Bayes square-loss
predictor zero, so learning needs nothing, while the label law reveals
$\langle u,x\rangle$ up to sign at generative exponent two. Part (a) became
\cref{thm-reconstruction-from-conditional-mean}.
\fullproof{conj-reconstruction-at-training-cost}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-01/builder.md}
\end{conjecture}

\section{The open statement}

\begin{conjecture}[The seed is worth the NGCA price]\blabel{conj-seed-price-is-ngca-price}\status{open}
For frozen clients of $\mathcal P_{O(d)}$ from which a seed-known attacker recovers
every record exactly in polynomial time, and records from $P^A_V$ with $A$ of mean
zero, identity covariance and fixed dimension, the budget sets of seed-blind
attackers in the SQ game (weak success) and the sample game (fine success) equal,
up to polynomial factors, those of NGCA coordinate recovery for $A$ with a uniformly
random hidden frame. Settled so far are the lower inclusion for pancakes in both games
and for moment-matched laws in the SQ game, and the upper inclusion for frozen
linear clients given the population whitening.
\usesstatement{def-seed-blind-reconstruction-game}
\usesproof{lem-equivariant-view-coupling,lem-whitening-gives-rotated-records,prop-sq-blind-reconstruction-bound,prop-fine-reconstruction-to-hclwe}
\source{Open: the upper inclusion for ReLU and deeper clients, where the blind
server must learn the client map without the seed, the cost of estimating the
whitening, and coarse reconstruction in polynomial time. Accepted in round 02 as a
well-posed conjecture.}
\fullproof{conj-seed-price-is-ngca-price}{lab/theory/spaces/computational-limits/round-02/builder.md}
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Decisions proposed for Halil.} The proposed first target is the seed
separation \cref{thm-blind-split-seed-separation}, with \cref{conj-seed-price-is-ngca-price} as
its general form and \cref{thm-reconstruction-from-conditional-mean} with
\cref{prop-sq-label-relevant-reconstruction-bound} as the label side. The proposed
attacker class for lower bounds uses two games and never mixes them. Population SQ
attackers of frozen clients give unconditional bounds, and polynomial-time attackers
with the record-level view give bounds conditional on lattice hardness. The open question is whether a
separation whose hard instance is a record law meets the rule on cryptographic
plug-ins. The procedure is standard and no primitive is placed in it, yet the
hardness comes from the data.
