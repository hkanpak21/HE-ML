### `fact-small-seed-space-breaks-dp`
- **Truth and reading.** Section 6.3 Violation 2 (100 PRNG seeds violating claimed $\varepsilon=1.27$, caught by GDP and Lu et al. audits, missed by $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ audits, Fig. 8) matches [NHSBTJCT23.md](file:///Users/a90/Documents/RESEARCH/HE-ML/lib/literature/notes/usenix-security/NHSBTJCT23.md#L58-L60). - **Challenge `ch-finite-seed-not-top-oracle` (blocking).** Lines 130–132 claim: *"With $2^{n_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ seeds, enumeration realizes the top oracle at $2^{n_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ runs (heuristic, it assumes deterministic kernels)."* This statement is **false** (`proven`).
  - Let $\mathcal R = \{0,1,\dots,M-1\}$ with uniform $\mu$ ($M=2^{n_{\mathrm{bit}}}$ even), deterministic kernels, plain channel, and $\mathsf L(x_b; s) = (s + b\cdot M/2) \bmod M$.
  - Under the **top oracle**, the adversary receives the honest seed $s^*$ and has advantage $\mathrm D^\top_1 = 1$.
  - Under the **bottom oracle**, $s^*$ is hidden. Even if the adversary enumerates all $M$ seeds at $x_0$ and all $M$ seeds at $x_1$ (costing $2M$ runs when $\mathsf L$ is unknown, or $0$ runs when $\mathsf L$ is known), it learns only the two marginal mixture laws $P_0 = P_1 = \mathrm{Unif}\{0,\dots,M-1\}$, so every bottom-oracle adversary has advantage $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1) = 0 < 1 = \mathrm D^\top_1$ (`proven`).
  - Enumerating a finite seed space realizes *exact knowledge of the marginal laws $(P_0,P_1)$*, never the top oracle; $\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ coincides with $\mathrm D^\top_1$ only when the finite supports $\{\kappa(\mathsf L(x_0;s))\}_s$ and $\{\kappa(\mathsf L(x_1;s))\}_s$ happen to be nearly disjoint, which holds with high probability for $100$ Gaussian vectors in high dimension (`proven`).

#### Challenge `ch-generic-oracle-open-and-meter` (blocking — Claim 1)
Claim 1 ([builder.md](file:///Users/a90/Documents/RESEARCH/HE-ML/lab/theory/spaces/oracle-and-measurement/round-00/builder.md#L324-L329)) states that with $\mathsf{ev}(m,u)$ returning outputs, input gradients, or parameter gradients, *"the operations of `def-generic-training-oracle` are drawn-mode Train calls, Eval calls and Open."* This is **false as written** on two counts (`proven`):
1. *White-box leakage on shadow `Train` calls.* By `def-learning-oracle` ([learning-oracle-theory.md](file:///Users/a90/Documents/RESEARCH/HE-ML/sink/2026-10-16-formal-tuning/final/learning-oracle-theory.md#L34)), a caller holds label `open` on every name returned by its own calls, and `def-seed-access-modes` keeps this rule for model names. Hence any drawn-mode caller that invokes `Train` can call `Open` on its returned shadow model (and gets free `Eval` on `open` names, line 46). #### Explicit Lower-Bound Construction for `conj-exact-twin-price` (a) (`proven`)
A valid proof of `(a)` in **named mode** must defeat certifiers that pass *arbitrary adaptively chosen explicit seeds* $\rho\in\mathcal R$, seed names $s$, `Open` calls, and surrogate `Train` calls at any $x\neq x_1$ (`lem-needle-lower-bound` alone gives only linear decay $1-\alpha-N/J$, losing $\ln(1/\alpha)$, while naive uncountably-i.i.d. perturbations $\rho\mapsto\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})$ are non-measurable). Here is the exact measurable construction and sharp bound (`proven`):

1. **Finite-partition hypergeometric family on $(\mathcal R,\mu)$.** Assume $x_0\neq x_1$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\in(0,1)$. By Standing Fact `(F9)` (Kechris, Theorem 17.41), identify $(\mathcal R,\mu)$ modulo a $\mu$-null set $\mathcal N_0$ with $([0,1),\lambda)$. Fix an integer $M > 1/(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})$, set $m = \lfloor M\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\rfloor + 1 \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ so $p_M := m/M > \epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $p_M \to \epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ as $M\to\infty$, and partition $[0,1)$ into $I_r = [(r-1)/M, r/M)$ for $r=1,\dots,M$. For each subset $B\in\binom{[M]}{m}$, set $S_B = \bigcup_{r\in B} I_r$ (Borel) and define the deterministic-kernel learning function
   $$\mathsf L_B(x;\rho) = \begin{cases} \mathsf L^\circ(x_1;\rho) + (\Delta+1)\mathbf e_1 & \text{if } x=x_1 \text{ and } \rho\in S_B,\\ \mathsf L^\circ(x;\rho) & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
   Since $\mathsf L^\circ(x_0;\rho)=\mathsf L^\circ(x_1;\rho)$, $\|\mathsf L_B(x_1;\rho)-\mathsf L_B(x_0;\rho)\| = (\Delta+1)\mathbf 1[\rho\in S_B]$, so $\epsilon_0(\Delta;\mathsf L_B) = \mu(S_B) = p_M > \epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}$ for every $B$.
2. **Coupling against $\mathsf L^\circ$ and eliminating surrogates.** Couple the certifier's run against $\mathsf L^\circ$ and against $\mathsf L_B$ using identical internal coins and identical oracle draws $(\rho'_i,\upsilon'_i)_{i\ge 1}$. Every `Train(x,s)` call with $x\neq x_1$ (including $x_0$, fine-tuning from opened $W_0$, warm starts, and approximate unlearning) evaluates $\mathsf L_B(x;\cdot)=\mathsf L^\circ(x;\cdot)$, and `Eval`/`Open` read stored values. Let $T_1$ be the number of `Train` calls at $x_1$ made in the $\mathsf L^\circ$ run (independent of $B$ and $M$!). If $\mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}[T_1]=\infty$ we are done; otherwise $T_1<\infty$ a.s. In the $\mathsf L^\circ$ run, those $T_1$ calls visit $K\le T_1$ distinct cells of $\{I_1,\dots,I_M\}$. For uniformly random $B\in\binom{[M]}{m}$, on $\mathrm{Miss}_B := \{\text{none of the } K \text{ hit cells lies in } B\}$ the entire transcript and output $\hat t$ under $\mathsf L_B$ equal those under $\mathsf L^\circ$. Let $E_{\mathrm{low}} := \{\hat t \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}}\}$, with $p := \Pr_{\mathsf L^\circ}[E_{\mathrm{low}}] \ge 1-\alpha' > \alpha$. Soundness at $\mathsf L_B$ gives $\Pr_{\mathsf L_B}[\hat t < p_M] \le \alpha$; since $E_{\mathrm{low}} \subseteq \{\hat t < p_M\}$, averaging over $B$ gives
   $$\alpha \ge \mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}\!\Big[\mathbf 1[E_{\mathrm{low}}]\frac{\binom{M-K}{m}}{\binom{M}{m}}\Big] \ge \mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}\!\Big[\mathbf 1[E_{\mathrm{low}}]\mathbf 1[T_1 < M-m]\prod_{j=0}^{T_1-1}\Big(1-\frac{m}{M-j}\Big)\Big].$$
3. **Limit $M\to\infty$ and sharp Jensen step.** Letting $M\to\infty$ (with the $\mathsf L^\circ$ law fixed!) and applying Dominated Convergence yields $\mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}[\mathbf 1[E_{\mathrm{low}}](1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})^{T_1}] \le \alpha$. Conditioning on $E_{\mathrm{low}}$ and applying Jensen's inequality to the convex map $t\mapsto (1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})^t$ gives $(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})^{\mathbb E[T_1\mid E_{\mathrm{low}}]} \le \alpha/p$, hence
   $$\mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}[T_1] \ge p\,\mathbb E_{\mathsf L^\circ}[T_1\mid E_{\mathrm{low}}] \ge \frac{(1-\alpha')\ln\!\big((1-\alpha')/\alpha\big)}{-\ln(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})} \ge \frac{\ln\!\big(1/(\alpha+\alpha')\big)}{-\ln(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{acc}})},$$
   where the last inequality holds for all $0<\alpha<1-\alpha'\le 1$ because $g(\delta)=p\ln(p/(p-\delta))+\ln(1-\delta)$ satisfies $g(0)=0$ and $g'(\delta)=\frac{\delta(1-p)}{(p-\delta)(1-\delta)}\ge 0$ for $\delta=1-\alpha-\alpha'\in(0,p)$ (`proven`).
