\lrscope
The question is how the top venues of 2023 to 2026 state threat models for
multi-party learning, and which way they are moving. The method is a screen of
every accepted paper at eleven venues, followed by a reading of each kept paper
and its coding into the ledger of the adversary interface, one row per
coalition.

%%----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\lrresult
The sweep screened \lrv{Screened} papers, placed \lrv{Placed}, and coded the
\lrv{Coded} that pass the inclusion rule into \lrv{Rows} ledger rows.
Empirical: rows with a deviating adversary rose from \lrv{DevFirst} to
\lrv{DevLast} per cent between 2023 and 2026, and rows protected by HE rose from
\lrv{HEFirst} to \lrv{HELast} per cent (Table~\ref{tab:trends}). Empirical:
among deviating-adversary rows whose evaluation says whether the adversary
adapts to the defence, the adaptive share fell from \lrv{AdaptFirst} to
\lrv{AdaptLast} per cent (Table~\ref{tab:adaptive}). The field therefore states
a malicious adversary more often each year and evaluates it against an adaptive
adversary less often.

\lrboundary{Each row comes from one coder's reading of the paper, so every note
behind it is prov sweep, and the trend columns come from keyword matching on
the coded cells. The 2026 counts lack NeurIPS 2026 and may lack part of CCS
2026. Screening recall is unmeasured, since the drop record lists only near
misses. Citation data covers \lrv{WithCitations} papers so far, because the
day's OpenAlex budget ran out, so no prominence ranking is given here. EuroS\&P,
SaTML and CSF were not screened.}

\lrchanges
The study's opening observation, that papers claim a malicious adversary and
evaluate a weaker one, is now a measured trend over three years of top venues.
The protection layer the group works in, HE, is the one gaining share. A next
problem is therefore best placed where a deviating adversary meets an HE
protection layer, and where evaluation against an adaptive adversary is rare.
The ledger's edge and survives columns are the place to look for such a
problem.

%%----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\lrmethod
Six screeners read the accepted-paper lists, one per venue group. Each kept a
paper when it concerned training or fine-tuning by two or more parties holding
data or models, and its contribution concerned privacy or security against an
adversary. Each drop that needed an abstract to decide carries its reason in
the screening record. PDFs came from the venues' open proceedings, from arXiv,
and from the KU library. Every PDF was matched to its paper by the title and
authors on its first page before it was placed with its note.

A coder then read each placed paper and wrote one row per coalition on the 22
columns of the column dictionary. A coder that found a paper outside the rule
wrote no row, and the paper's note records the reason. Protocol papers on
threshold or multiparty HE stay on the protocol axis with their learning
columns set to n/a, because they bear on the group's own question. The merge
takes every venue from the paper's note, which carries the verified venue.
