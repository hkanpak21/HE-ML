\lrscope
Does a standard or framework fix the scenarios of multi-party learning and state, for each, what a protocol hides and what its released output still leaks?
The answer draws on the formalizing line's sweep of about \lrv{Taxonomies} taxonomies, its ledger of \lrv{LedgerPapers} multi-party papers, and \lrv{Searches} searches of 2026-11-11 on standards and on frameworks that join the two.

%%----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\lrresult
No standard or framework does it, and the place it would fill lies between three bodies of work, each holding one part.
Taxonomies give the vocabulary of stage, goal, knowledge and capability but prove nothing, and none has an axis for which parties the adversary controls.
Security games make exact what the adversary can tell, but Salem et al.~\cite{SCEKPSTZ23} state that their \lrv{SalemGames} games have one challenger training one model and do not express concurrent protocols.
Simulation proofs show that a protocol reveals nothing beyond the output of an ideal functionality~\cite{MOJC23,MWAPR23}, and they take what that output leaks as given.
The two layers meet only inside single constructions, in \lrv{Joins} of the \lrv{IdealPapers} ledger papers proven against an ideal functionality, each for its own protocol.
Standards for federated learning, MPC, FHE and DP fix vocabulary and the unit of privacy, and none fixes attack scenarios.

\lrboundary{Only Salem et al., Yeom et al. and HE-OFT were read in full. The other entries rest on sweep notes, abstracts or search snippets, and a limit marked as found by us may be wrong in detail. The ledger counts come from language-model coders of 2026-10-16 and were not re-read, and the full text of IEEE 3652.1 was not seen.}

\lrchanges
The framework Halil asked for is unoccupied, and the paper can therefore claim the framework itself if it reuses the standard objects of the three bodies.
Positioning against Villa et al.~\cite{VDKMR26}, which the sweep of 2026-08-05 named the closest work, is no longer needed, because its object is an agentic LLM system.
The closest prior work becomes Salem et al. for the output layer, Li et al.~\cite{LLLKPMW24} for games with several parties, and secure aggregation for the protocol layer.
The \lrv{Joins} joined constructions are instances the framework must reproduce as special cases.
