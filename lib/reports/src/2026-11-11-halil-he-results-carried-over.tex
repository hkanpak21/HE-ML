\lrresult
%% The first sentence is the verdict, including any narrowing. A reader who
%% stops after it has the answer. Plain sentence, no emphasis.
%% Then what the numbers are. Then the evidence. Then the boundary.

HE-ML carries \lrv{Problems} open problems, \lrv{Results} results and \lrv{Facts} facts on encryption, and none settles that threshold CKKS realizes an ideal release, which keeps reduction to CKKS heuristic.
Against a semi-honest coalition, reduction to CKKS rests on thm-dataflow-composition, proven given the realization error, and on HE-OFT's simulation of a server with fewer than t clients.
The simulation assumes a smudging that HE-OFT's measured configuration does not meet, and HHKLS26 is recorded, unread, as refuting per-party smudging, which HE-OFT and HLSS25 both use.
Against a deviating coalition reduction to CKKS lacks support, because active security was found only for BFV, BGV and FHEW decryption and HE-OFT admits no deviating server.
Steering lies outside reduction to CKKS, because a client of HE-OFT that inflates its encrypted per-class counts moves a row of the shared head at will (prop-he-oft-count-steering, proven).
In the scenario space, reduction to CKKS thus covers semi-honest servers and clients heuristically, and the cells of deviating servers and of steering need another route or are gaps.
