## Claim

Answering subset-sum queries on an $n$-bit database with perturbation $o(\sqrt n)$
lets a polynomial-time adversary reconstruct almost all of it, perturbation $o(n)$
lets an exponential-time adversary do so, and perturbation of order $\sqrt{T}$
protects against adversaries of running time $T$.

## Method

A database is a bit vector $d\in\{0,1\}^n$ with an access algorithm $A$ answering
subsets $q\subseteq[n]$ within perturbation $E$ (Definitions 1 and 2). It is
$t(n)$-non-private if for every $\varepsilon>0$ a machine of time $t(n)$ outputs a
string within Hamming distance $\varepsilon n$ of $d$ with probability $2/3$
(Definition 3). Theorem 2 queries all $2^n$ subsets and keeps any candidate
consistent with every answer, which lies within $4E$ of $d$. Theorem 3 asks
$n(\log n)^2$ random subsets, solves a linear program for a fractional candidate and
rounds at $1/2$. Theorem 5 adds independent perturbation and analyses the
adversary's confidence as a random walk.

## What they measured, and on what

Theorem 2 (§3.1): $o(n)$ perturbation gives $\exp(n)$-non-privacy. Theorem 3
(§3.2): $o(\sqrt n)$ perturbation gives poly$(n)$-non-privacy. §3.3: for the uniform
prior, a database that answers $|q|/2$ unless the true sum is far from it is within
$\tilde O(\sqrt n)$ perturbation and reveals nothing to polynomial machines except
with negligible probability, at the price of being useless. Theorem 5 (§4): for the
uniform prior and $T(n)>\mathrm{polylog}(n)$, an $\tilde O(\sqrt{T(n)})$-perturbation
algorithm is $(T(n),\delta)$-private in the two-phase sense of Definition 4.

## Epistemic status

Proven, Theorems 2, 3 and 5 and the §3.3 construction. The §4 usability example is
an argument. The feasibility results assume a prior on the database, which the
authors say is unavoidable.

## The limitation that matters to us

Stated. The prices are in queries and running time on a database, not in learning
resources, and the feasibility side needs a prior on the data.

## Relationship to this project

Prior art. It is the canonical statistical against computational gap for
reconstruction, $o(n)$ against $o(\sqrt n)$ perturbation.
