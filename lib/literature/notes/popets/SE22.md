# SE22, Formalizing and Estimating Distribution Inference Risks

## Claim

Distribution inference has a game of the form of Yeom et al.'s membership game, between two candidate training distributions, and its leakage can be stated in samples: n_leaked is the number of records drawn directly from the training distribution with which an optimal test reaches the attack's observed accuracy.

## Method

The challenger picks one of two distributions, draws a training set from it and trains, and the adversary guesses which distribution was used. For distributions that differ in the ratio of a Boolean property, the paper derives n_leaked in closed form from the observed accuracy and the two ratios (theorem proven in Appendix A.2), gives a version for an adversary that regresses the ratio with square error (Appendix A.3), and extends it to graph properties such as average degree and clustering coefficient.

## What they measured, and on what

Black-box attacks and improved white-box meta-classifier attacks across several distributions and graph properties. The worked example of Section 3: distinguishing ratio 0.5 from 1.0 at accuracy 0.95 is n_leaked of about 3, and 0.5 from 0.52 at the same accuracy is about 42. Inexpensive attacks are often as effective as meta-classifiers (abstract, experiments not read).

## Epistemic status

The n_leaked expressions are proven by the authors and not checked by us. The attack results are empirical.

## The limitation that matters to us

n_leaked converts one attack's accuracy into samples, so it is a measured level for that attack and not a price over all attackers, and closed forms exist only for the families derived. Found by us.

## Relationship to this project

Prior art, and the nearest published metric in learning units: it states property leakage as the number of records the attacker in effect received.

## What we need to consider for our work

A distinguish objective on a property of a client's distribution can report n_leaked against the client's dataset size, which puts it in the same unit as a query allowance of a fraction of the training set.
