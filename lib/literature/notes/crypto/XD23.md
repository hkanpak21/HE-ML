## Claim

The success of any reconstruction or inference attack on a release $M(X)$, against
an adversary that knows the data law, is bounded through the mutual information
$I(X;M(X))$, and for a deterministic black-box $M$ that information can be pushed
below a target with confidence by Gaussian noise whose covariance is estimated from
Monte Carlo runs of $M$ on fresh data.

## Method

Definition 1 calls $M$ $(\delta,\rho,\mathcal D)$ PAC private when no adversary who
knows $\mathcal D$ and $M$ outputs $\tilde X$ with $\rho(\tilde X,X)=1$ with
probability at least $1-\delta$. Definition 3 sets the optimal prior success
$1-\delta^\rho_o=\sup_{\tilde X}\Pr_{X\sim\mathcal D}[\rho(\tilde X,X)=1]$. Theorem 1,
by data processing for $f$-divergences, gives
$D_f(\mathbf 1_\delta\|\mathbf 1_{\delta^\rho_o})\le\inf_{P_W}D_f(P_{X,M(X)}\|P_X\otimes P_W)$,
and with KL and $P_W=P_{M(X)}$ the bound $\mathrm{kl}(\delta\|\delta^\rho_o)\le I(X;M(X))$.
Remark 1 conditions both sides on extra adversary knowledge. Theorem 3 bounds
$I(X;M(X)+B)\le\frac12\log\det(I_d+\Sigma_{M(X)}\Sigma_B^{-1})$ for deterministic $M$
and $B\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_B)$. Algorithm 1 estimates $\Sigma_{M(X)}$ from $m$ runs
on fresh draws of $X$ and sets anisotropic noise, and Theorem 4 relates the
security parameter $c$, the simulation count $m$ and the confidence $1-\gamma$
through display (6), for outputs bounded by $r$.

## What they measured, and on what

Corollary 1 gives $\delta^\rho_o-\delta\le\sqrt{I(X;M(X))/2}$. Theorem 2 refines the
bound for individual privacy and symmetric mechanisms on i.i.d. data, and Example 1
computes it for $N=100$ candidates and $I=1$ nat, success at most $0.36$ by Theorem 1
and $0.17$ at $n=10$ by Theorem 2. Theorem 9 shows that a data-independent Gaussian
mechanism bounding the worst-case mutual information by $v=o(1)$ needs
$\mathbb E\|B\|_2=\Omega(r\sqrt d/\sqrt v)$. Example 3 calibrates noise for the mean of
a random half of CIFAR-10 and finds $\mathbb E\|B\|_2=0.28$ for $I\le1$, against
$17.7$ from a zCDP calibration. Example 4 discusses deep learning, where no
worst-case proof is available.

## Epistemic status

Proven, Theorems 1 to 4 and 9 and Corollary 1, with proofs in appendices not read
here. The numbers of the examples are computations under the stated laws. The paper
does not blur these.

## The limitation that matters to us

Stated by the authors (§2, Lack of Knowledge). Every bound assumes the adversary
knows $\mathcal D$, and the framework cannot quantify the extra hardness of an
adversary that does not, because its tools rest on the entropy of the data. HSU25
prices exactly that ignorance, in samples. Found, not stated: Algorithm 1 spends
training runs of the mechanism on fresh data to certify the mechanism, which prices
the defender's certificate and not the attacker.
