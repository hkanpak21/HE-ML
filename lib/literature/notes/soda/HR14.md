# HR14, On the Lattice Isomorphism Problem

Read on 2026-10-25 by the computational-limits space session from arXiv
1311.0366v1, text-extracted: the abstract and Section 5.1 (Lemmas 5.1 and 5.4,
Remark 5.2). The algorithm for LIP and the rest were read by their headings only.

## Claim

LIP can be solved in time $n^{O(n)}$ times a polynomial in the input, and the tools
include a bound on how many discrete Gaussian samples generate a lattice.

## Method

Lemma 5.1: for a lattice $L$ of rank $n$ and a strict sublattice $M$, a sample of
$D_{L,s}$ lies in $M$ with probability at most $1/(1+e^{-\pi c^{-2}})$, if
$\mathrm{span}(M)\ne\mathrm{span}(L)$ and $s\ge c\lambda_n(L)$, or if $s\ge c\,bl(L)$ in
general, where $bl$ is the length of a shortest basis. Lemma 5.4: for $\det(L)\ge1$
and $s\ge bl(L)$, a set of $n^2+n\log(s\sqrt n)(n+20\log\log(s\sqrt n))$ independent
samples of $D_{L,s}$ fails to generate $L$ with probability $2^{-\Omega(n)}$. The proof
takes $n^2$ samples for full rank, bounds the index of the generated sublattice by
$(s\sqrt n)^n$, and then lowers the index with each further block by Lemma 5.1.
