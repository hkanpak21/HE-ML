# DK22, Non-Gaussian Component Analysis via Lattice Basis Reduction

Read on 2026-10-25 by the computational-limits space session from arXiv
2112.09104v1, text-extracted: Sections 1 and 2 in full (8 pages).

## Claim

The SQ information-computation tradeoff for non-Gaussian component analysis does
not bind all efficient algorithms: when the hidden univariate law is discrete or
nearly discrete, an LLL-based algorithm finds the hidden direction from $2d$
samples in polynomial time.

## Method

Definition 1.1: $P^A_v$ has projection $A$ on $v$ and a standard Gaussian on
$v^\perp$. Assumption 1.2: every sample of $A$ lies within $\epsilon$ of some
$\sum_{j\le k}n_jr_j$, $|r_j|=O(1)$, $n_j\in\mathbb Z$, $|n_j|\le B$, with
anti-concentration $\Pr[|X|>1/d]>1/d$ and concentration $\Pr[|X|>\mathrm{poly}(d)]<1/d$.
The algorithm (Section 2): round the $m=2d$ samples to a grid $\delta=\epsilon/N^2$,
$N=2^{Cmk^2}B^{Ck}$; let $V$ be the right kernel of the $d\times m$ sample matrix;
define $Q(n)=\|n\|_2^2+N\|\mathrm{Proj}_V(\sum_jn_{i,j}r_j)_i\|_2^2$ on
$\mathbb Z^{m\times k}$; LLL-reduce $Q$; keep the span $W$ of the basis vectors with
small Gram-Schmidt norm; maximize $R(w)/Q(w)$ over $W$ with
$R(n)=\sum_i(\sum_jn_{i,j}r_j)^2$; round; regress the samples on the resulting
values to get $v^*$. Lemma 2.1: $Q(n)$ is small only if $(\sum_jn_{i,j}r_j)_i$ is
nearly a multiple of $(v\cdot x_i)_i$, by a union bound over integer vectors, using
that $V$ given $y$ is a uniformly random $(m-d)$-dimensional subspace orthogonal
to $y$.

## What they measured, and on what

Theorem 1.3: for $\epsilon<2^{-\Omega(dk^2)}B^{-\Omega(k)}$, time
$\mathrm{poly}(d,k,\log B)$, $m=2d$ samples, and with constant probability the
normalized $v^*$ within $N^{-1}2^{O(m^2k^2/(m-d))}B^{O(mk/(m-d))}$ of $\pm v$, which is
exponentially small. Remark 2.2: any $m>d$ with a stronger bound on $\epsilon$.

## Epistemic status

Proven.

## The limitation that matters to us

Only exponentially thin noise is tolerated, so pancakes with inverse-polynomial
noise are out of reach, as the hardness of hCLWE requires. The paper states this
through its condition on $\epsilon$.

## Relationship to this project

Tool and constraint. Steps 3 to 9 see the samples only through the kernel of the
sample matrix, which an injective linear client does not change, so a seed-blind
server can run them on its representations. This is my reading of the algorithm,
used in the computational-limits aim `conj-sq-sample-games-separate`.

## What we need to consider for our work

SQ bounds for discrete hidden laws overstate the seed's worth: an LLL attacker in
the sample game is polynomial. Any experiment on discrete or quantized records
must include such an attacker.
